# Agentic AI Security CMM — D2 Identity & Authorization (Deep Dive)

Companion deep-dive to [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|the CMM]]'s D2 domain, written under the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026|recalibration method]]. D2 assigns every agent a per-agent non-human identity and governs its credential lifecycle. The threats this domain answers are Privilege Compromise (T3) and Identity Spoofing and Impersonation (T9) in [[owasp-agentic-ai-threats-mitigations|OWASP Agentic AI Threats and Mitigations]], whose Playbook 4 (authentication, identity, and privilege controls) calls for cryptographic identity verification for agents, mutual authentication between agents, and credentials that expire after a short time.[^atm-pb4] The level definitions below grade the first at L3 and the last at L4, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d5-egress-network|D5]] L3 grades mutual authentication on the inter-agent leg. Per-agent identity is generally available and platform-native on all three hyperscalers,[^entra-ga][^awsga][^gcpga] and per-task capability tokens sit at L5+, because no platform ships them. Every criterion carries a name, D2 and a mnemonic such as D2-OWNER, and §Assessor detail per level states each one with the evidence it names.

> [!gap] Single-source grounding
> Levels and cost model synthesize the recalibration method against the [[agentic-cmm-regulated-fi-stress-test|regulated-FI stress test]] plus vendor documentation. Tooling status is dated at each claim, most recently September 2026.

**D2-L3 raises the ceiling on D5 and D7.** The active dependency caps are **D2→D5** and **D2→D7**, so a deployment that reaches D2-L3 raises its own D5 and D7 effective-score ceilings to L3, and D2 is the only domain whose score caps two others. A rollout of verifiable per-agent identity meets the first of the six L3 criteria, and D5 and D7 rise under the higher ceilings only as far as their own criteria are met. Egress and observability cannot exceed D2's level, because both per-agent egress policy and per-agent behavioral baselining bind to a principal that D2-L3 verifies. [[agent-identity-architecture|Agent Identity Architecture]] builds the layers in that order for the same reason. For a Microsoft incumbent, D2-L3 costs near-zero licensing: Entra Agent ID rides the directory. Fund this level ahead of any other identity work.

The [[microsoft-zt4ai|Microsoft ZT4AI]] Identity pillar (verify explicitly / least privilege) supplies the named controls behind these levels: [[microsoft-entra-agent-id|Entra Agent ID]] per-agent identity, Conditional Access for Agent Identities, and ID Protection for agents, all crosswalked to the D2 levels in [[standards-review-microsoft-zt4ai-2026-Q2|the 2026-Q2 ZT4AI review]]. [[microsoft-agent-365|Microsoft Agent 365]] sits above these controls as a **management plane**, the [[agent-catalog|agent registry]] and admin surface that aggregates and operates them, rather than as a new enforcement control or a portable standard. It is vendor-bound and per-user-licensed, and an organization cannot implement it on another stack. [[standards-review-microsoft-rai-agent-365-2026-Q2|The 2026-Q2 RAI / Agent 365 review]] sets that distinction out in full.

## Threat coverage

D2 is the primary domain for **ASI03 (Identity & Privilege Abuse)** and **ASI10 (Rogue Agents)**, and the entry point for **Class 1 (AI-aware insider)** through the identities and grants an insider uses against the running system. Because per-agent egress (D5) and behavioral baselining (D7) are capped by it, D2 is the prerequisite for containing any per-agent threat. See the [[threat-taxonomy-reconciliation|Threat Taxonomy Reconciliation]] matrix and the [[agentic-ai-threat-classes-2026|threat classes]].

That entry point stops at the running system. No level defined below grades an MLOps role, a human engineer's access to the training environment, or any of the five development-time controls the [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] groups in §3.0, among them `DEV SECURITY`, `SEGREGATE DATA`, and `CONF COMPUTE`, so an insider already inside that environment sits outside this domain's graded criteria. The Exchange names insider access as one route to the confidentiality threat those three controls answer ([`/go/devmodelleak/`](https://owaspai.org/go/devmodelleak/)). Least-privilege MLOps role design, which [[agentic-ai-threat-classes-2026|the threat classes]] name as a Class 1 control, is stated in the class and graded in no level here. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-crosswalk|The crosswalk]] records the same bound on all four domains it places Class 1 on — D2, D3, D6, and D8 — each of which grades the system in operation.

The prerequisite is necessary and not sufficient, and the [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] states the insufficiency directly: authentication confirms identity and not intent, so a prompt-injected authenticated agent still passes its auth checks.[^aix-mac] Every control in this domain assumes the principal it names is acting on its own behalf. Where that assumption fails, D2's contribution is that the action carries a nameable principal for [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d5-egress-network|D5]] to constrain and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]] to baseline, which is the reason the caps run this direction. A deployment scored high on D2 and low on D5 and D7 names the principal behind an action and still cannot constrain what that principal does.

Entra Agent ID mints a credential-less service principal from an agent blueprint, and the blueprint holds the credentials that acquire the agent's tokens. For production, Microsoft prefers a managed identity or a certificate for that credential over a client secret, and states that managed identities "eliminate stored secrets entirely".[^entra-bp] A Microsoft deployment whose blueprint authenticates through a managed identity therefore reaches the zero-credentials row natively and buys no credential proxy. A blueprint on a certificate or a client secret still holds a stored credential.

AWS and Google Cloud keep stored credentials out of the agent's process on their native paths. For their own resources, AWS gives the agent IAM role credentials and Google Cloud gives it certificate-bound tokens, both short-lived. For a third-party service, AgentCore Gateway places an API key on the call it makes to a gateway target,[^awsgwout] and Google's Agent Gateway decrypts the end-user credentials that Gemini Enterprise connectors provision, so the agent never holds them.[^gcpid] Both vaults also hand credentials to the agent directly: AgentCore's API-key credential provider returns the key to the calling agent,[^awsapikey] and Google's auth manager returns API keys and OAuth tokens to an agent calling an external tool.[^gcpid] An agent that takes an API key into its own process fails D2-NOCRED on either platform.

Okta for AI Agents reached general availability on 2026-04-29.[^okta-ga] The schedule Okta published in September 2025 placed Early Access in the first quarter of FY27 and named no quarter for the general-availability phase, which the product then reached inside that same first quarter.[^okta-plan] Nothing in these level definitions depends on the product, because per-agent identity and credential lifecycle are platform-native on all three hyperscalers.

Cyera states that an agent inherits the access of the person who launched it and exercises that access from day one. D2-DELEGATE grades that model at L2: an agent acting for a human may exercise that human's access and nothing beyond it. Cyera also states its Discover phase inventories agents across cloud, SaaS, endpoint, and [[shadow-ai|Shadow AI]], which is a vendor example of the discovery D2-DISCOVER grades at L5 ([[cyera-agent-guardian-release|Cyera Agent Guardian Release]]).

Two September 2026 announcements supply further vendor examples against levels D2 already grades, and move none of them. CrowdStrike states that its [[crowdstrike-agentic-identity-provider|Agentic Identity Provider]] issues a cryptographically verifiable per-agent identity, brokers short-lived tokens scoped to one task in place of standing credentials, and binds every action to the delegating human or workload, which maps onto D2-IDENTITY-VERIFY and D2-TRACE at L3 and onto D2-NOCRED and D2-TASKBIND at L4. CrowdStrike's announcement carries its standing caveat that the unreleased features it discusses are still in development, gives no general-availability date, and publishes no token format, key derivation, or revocation model, so the product reaches no part of D2-TASKTOKEN at L5+. Ping states that [[ping-enterprise-personal-agent-access|Enterprise Personal Agent Access]] discovers personal agents including shadow AI, issues secretless just-in-time privileged access, and revokes that access in real time, which maps onto D2-NOCRED at L4 and D2-DISCOVER at L5. Ping states it is available now. Neither vendor publishes a coverage figure, a bypass rate, or an independent evaluation, so the graded status of every level named here is unchanged.

Google made IAM Unified Access Policies generally available for Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform on 2026-08-31.[^gcpuap] Agent Gateway evaluates each request an agent principal makes against rules whose conditions read only attributes of the destination, such as the tool called or the request path, and a Google-managed Context-Aware Access policy accepts the agent's token only with proof that the caller holds the agent's key. Agent Gateway, which enforces the policies, has been generally available since 2026-06-18,[^gcpuap] and Google's Context-Aware Access page still labels it Preview.[^gcpcaa] No rule the customer writes can read the agent's risk or attributes, so the conditional-access row stays Microsoft-only and D2-CONDITIONAL is not applicable to an agent whose identity Google issues. The rules name agent principals, which makes them Google-native evidence for D2-AUTHZ.

## Capability-decoupled levels

Stated as capabilities per [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026|rule 1]]; a control counts when it operates in production per rule 2. A control implemented through a product in the organization's approved-vendor pipeline with a documented production date satisfies its criterion on that basis alone, and the production date is the date the control entered the organization's production. A planned date satisfies none, and neither does a product the organization does not yet run in production, whatever the vendor has announced.

- **L1 — Initial.** Agents share human credentials or service accounts; no inventory.
- **L2 — Developing.** Each agent holds an identity of its own and appears in an inventory, and it acts for a human only within that human's access.
- **L3 — Defined.** Each agent's identity is verifiable and its lifecycle follows the deploy pipeline. Delegation runs through token exchange, and every agent, identity and action traces to a named human.
- **L4 — Managed.** No stored credential sits in agent context, and every session, token and delegated grant binds to one identity and one task, so nothing carries across a task boundary or a delegation hop.
- **L5 — Optimizing.** A unified agent-governance program operates in production over a registry the deploy pipeline maintains, and identity binding carries cryptographic attestation.
- **L5+ — Leading Edge.** Each capability token binds to one task and one holder and narrows at each hop, and agent identity federates and reconciles across vendors.

D2-DELEGATE-TOKEN and D2-DELEGATE-LINK supply the token property that [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]] L4 consumes. That level grades the policy engine validating a delegation chain end to end, enforcing a configured maximum depth, and holding a downstream agent to a subset of its delegator's grants. A chain is validatable only where the credentials carry it, so this domain grades the artifact and D3 grades the evaluation, and the capability is scored once in each place. Chain splicing is the failure the parent link closes: a credential correctly signed and correctly scoped, presented at a step of the chain it was never issued for. The [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] asks for the parent link "where feasible", and it states the residue that survives both halves: a structurally valid token can still be contextually unauthorised, and short lifetimes, revocation, and attenuation carry what token construction cannot.[^aix-leastmodelpriv] D2-DELEGATE-LINK requires the link unconditionally wherever a chain exists, because D3 L4's full-chain validation has nothing to read without it; the criterion is not applicable only where no agent delegates to another.

D2-TASKBIND governs the authentication session and the issued token. Destruction of a sandbox's transient state at task completion is graded in [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]] L3, and review and reset of agent memory context at a session boundary in [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d6-data-rag|D6]] L4; the three cover different artifacts and a program can hold any one without the others. D2-MUTUAL covers the agent-to-service call. The inter-agent channel, its transport profile, and its message-level replay protection are graded in [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d5-egress-network|D5]] L3, so an assessor scores the inter-agent leg once and in that domain.

D2-AUTHZ and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]] L3 grade two properties of one decision point. D3 L3 grades that a decision point outside the model context mediates every tool call, deny-by-default and failing closed, and D2-AUTHZ grades that the rules it evaluates name the calling agent. A tool left in log-only mode therefore counts once, against D3, and a rule set keyed on tool and amount alone counts once, against D2.

Per-task holder-bound capability tokens sit at L5+, because the only implementation in the control landscape above is an early-stage OSS primitive and the one platform grant that table records is per-resource, not per-task. This mirrors D6, which holds cryptographic attestation at L5+ and grades entitlement enforcement at L3.

## Assessor detail per level

L1 describes where a deployment starts and carries no criterion, so a deployment that does not meet every L2 criterion scores L1, or 0 under the measurement protocol's rubric where no evidence of the L1 baseline exists. The levels from L2 to L5+ each carry a criteria list below, each list stating what its own level adds.

Grading is cumulative: Level N requires every Level N–1 control plus the new criteria at Level N ([[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|the CMM]]), so a level is met only where every level below it is met.

Each criterion takes one of four verdicts. **Met** and **not met** are read from the evidence the criterion names, whoever operates the control. The assessment records the evidence's assurance class beside the verdict and names the artifact behind it, its issuer and its date, per [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|the measurement protocol]]:

- **Tested**: the customer or the assessor exercised the control and recorded what it did.
- **Inspected**: vendor tooling the customer can reach, or a record the organization keeps, such as an inventory or a plan, shows the control's state in the deployment.
- **Attested**: the vendor states the control in a document the customer holds, and the document's own scope names the control.

**Not applicable** is recorded where the deployment holds no instance of what the criterion governs, and the reduced scope is recorded as an intentional trade-off in the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-dependency-rules|effective-score]] strategic-rationale field. **Unanswerable** is recorded where the instance exists, the customer can run no test and keeps no record of the control's state, and the vendor supplies neither inspectable output nor an attestation that names the control. An unanswerable criterion never counts as met: its level stays open, and every level above it stays open under cumulative grading. The assessment names what would close it. A criterion that can be not applicable states that condition alongside the criterion. Each level's list below holds criteria only, and a paragraph after a list states no criterion: it carries maturity, market or provenance commentary, or the boundary between this domain and another.

**D2 grades one deployment at a time.** A deployment is the unit the assessment scope names: one agent application, or one agent platform a vendor provides together with the agents the organization runs on it. Evidence of a control counts only for the deployments the control covers, and the D2→D5 and D2→D7 caps read the D2 score of the same deployment. Three terms fix what the criteria range over:

- **An agent** is one deployed configuration: its code, instructions, tools and grants as the deployment runs them. Replicas of one configuration are one agent, and two configurations are two agents even where they share code.
- **The agents in scope** are those the deployment runs in production, per rule 2. An agent outside production enters scope once it holds a production credential or reaches production data.
- **The deployment's non-human identities** are every identity its agents authenticate as and every identity a broker or gateway uses on their behalf.

A criterion that names every agent or every non-human identity is met only where it holds for each one. No percentage threshold applies, because the deployment's own design enumerates its identities and one unowned identity inside it is the failure D2 grades. An agent nobody has found is D2-DISCOVER's concern at L5.

**Names and precedence.** Each criterion carries a stable name, D2 and a mnemonic for what the criterion requires, and the name leads the criterion in bold. The level is an attribute recorded beside the name, so a criterion that changes level keeps its name. Members of a family share its first mnemonic, as D2-ROTATE and D2-ROTATE-MAP do, and each member is a separate criterion because a deployment can hold one without the other and an assessor collects different evidence for each. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|The CMM]]'s D2 levels restate each criterion under the same name, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|the measurement protocol]]'s D2 questions and artifacts cite each one by that name, in plain text. A level's statement above summarizes its list, and where the two read differently the list governs.

**Vendor-held identity.** A vendor holds an agent's identity where the agent runs on the vendor's infrastructure, as an in-suite assistant does, and the vendor offers no agent principal for it in general availability. Where the organization runs no such principal in production either, the criteria that grade the agent's own identity are not applicable to that agent, and each states so beside itself:

- D2-IDENTITY at L2.
- D2-IDENTITY-VERIFY and D2-LIFECYCLE at L3.
- D2-IDENTITY-ATTEST at L5.

The vendor's documentation of the product's identity model is the evidence, and it settles the condition only where it describes that product. Where the vendor offers an agent principal in general availability, the criteria stand, and an agent that holds none is not met. The agent counts for every other criterion: it is inventoried, owned and traced, and it acts for a human only within that human's access.

### L2 detail

- **D2-IDENTITY.** Each agent authenticates as a non-human identity of its own, and no person, other agent or other workload can use any credential the agent authenticates with. An organization-wide secret that other workflows can read fails the criterion, and so does a developer's own token. Not applicable under the vendor-held identity condition. *Evidence:* the identity provider's record of each agent identity and, for each credential it holds, the principals able to use it.
- **D2-INVENTORY.** An inventory records each agent in the deployment and every non-human identity it holds, and each identity's entry names the agent it belongs to. An inventory kept by hand meets the criterion, and so does a registry the pipeline maintains. *Evidence:* the inventory export, reconciled against the identity provider's list of the deployment's identities.
- **D2-DELEGATE.** An agent acting for a human exercises no access beyond that human's own, so anything it reads, writes or triggers for that human, the human could do directly. An agent acts for a human when that human's request starts the run, in a session or by an explicit invocation. A component outside the model holds the agent to that bound, such as a downstream system authorizing the human's delegated token or a filter in the agent's own code, and an agent may call another agent where both stay inside it. Not applicable to a run that an event or a schedule starts on the agent's own grant. *Evidence:* the agent's effective access for a sampled human set against that human's own access, and the component that enforces the bound.

### L3 detail

- **D2-IDENTITY-VERIFY.** Each agent identity is issued by an identity provider, and each service the agent calls verifies it from an assertion the provider signs, such as a token or a certificate. Entra Agent ID, an AWS AgentCore workload identity, GCP Agent Identity and a SPIFFE workload ID are examples, and any identity issued and verified this way meets the criterion. The inter-agent leg is [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d5-egress-network|D5]] L3's. Not applicable under the vendor-held identity condition. *Evidence:* the identity provider's configuration for the agent, and a sample of signed assertions that called services accepted.
- **D2-DELEGATE-EXCHANGE.** Every delegation hop is a token-exchange flow: an agent acting for a human or for another agent obtains a new token that names both parties, through OAuth 2.1 token exchange or a platform flow such as Entra's on-behalf-of,[^ca] and passes on no copied or shared credential. Acting for a human is itself a hop, and a header or a parameter carrying the human's name leaves the hop unmet. Not applicable where the deployment's agents act for no human and delegate to no agent. *Evidence:* the token-exchange configuration, and a sample of exchanged tokens naming both parties.
- **D2-LIFECYCLE.** The deploy pipeline issues, rotates and revokes each agent's identity: creating the agent issues the identity, the pipeline's definition sets how its credential rotates, and retiring the agent revokes it. A person joining, moving or leaving triggers none of the three. Not applicable under the vendor-held identity condition. *Evidence:* the pipeline definition that creates, rotates and revokes the identity, with the retirement record of an agent where one exists.
- **D2-COUPLING.** The inventory classes every credential in the deployment as coupled or decoupled ([[identity-credential-coupling|identity-credential coupling]]). A coupled credential cannot be separated from the identity it grants, as with a storage access key or a personal access token. *Evidence:* the inventory's coupling field across the credentials the agents use and those a broker uses for them.
- **D2-OWNER.** Every agent and every non-human identity in the deployment names a human owner, and the organization's personnel record shows that owner as current. *Evidence:* the owner field for each agent and identity, checked against the personnel record.
- **D2-TRACE.** Every action an agent takes traces to the agent's identity and to the human accountable for it: the human the agent acted for, or its owner where the run acted on the agent's own grant. A component outside the model supplies both: the agent's identity from its authentication, and the human's from the session, the delegated token or, for a run on the agent's own grant, the owner record. The coding-shape D2 test below applies this criterion to a coding deployment. *Evidence:* a sample of actions re-traced from the downstream system's own records to the agent and the human, and the code or configuration that supplies the human's identity.

### L4 detail

- **D2-NOCRED.** Zero credentials in agent context: a broker or a credential-less identity model issues what the agent needs, and no stored credential sits anywhere the agent's process can read. That context covers the model context, the process memory, the environment, and every file or secret mounted into the process. A stored credential is a secret that stays valid until someone rotates it, such as a password, an API key, a client secret or a private key, and a short-lived token issued at run time falls outside the term. *Evidence:* the agent's deployment specification with its environment and mounted secrets, and the broker or vault log or the credential-less identity configuration.
- **D2-AUTHZ.** Per-agent authorization: the decision point that authorizes the agent's calls evaluates rules that name the calling agent's identity or a named group of agents, so each agent holds only the grants written for it. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]] L3 grades whether that decision point mediates every call. *Evidence:* the policy export with the identity each rule names, and a decision-log sample recording the calling agent.
- **D2-KILL.** An orphaned-agent kill switch: one operation revokes every credential, token and session one agent holds and stops the agent acting, while every other agent keeps running. The organization has executed it end to end since the revocation path last changed, against a running agent that uses the production identity provider, broker and revocation path. A tabletop exercise shows the procedure, and the criterion asks for the execution. *Evidence:* the execution record, naming what the operation revoked and stopped and how long it took.
- **D2-TASKBIND.** Task-bound sessions and tokens: each authentication session and each token an agent receives binds to the agent's identity and to the current task, so no session state or token scope carries across a task boundary. A task is one unit of work the agent was started for, such as one user request or one scheduled run. *Evidence:* a session and token sample showing the identity and task each binds to, and a token refused after its task ended.
- **D2-MUTUAL.** Every call from an agent, or from a broker acting for it, to a service authenticates both ends cryptographically, by mutual TLS or a signed token, and none relies on a shared static key ([[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]]).[^aix-mac] The inter-agent leg is [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d5-egress-network|D5]] L3's. *Evidence:* the authentication configuration of each service the agent calls, including the calls a broker makes for it.
- **D2-DELEGATE-TOKEN.** Delegated-credential construction: each credential an agent receives to act for a human or another agent is signed and names its delegator, its delegatee, the scope it permits, the task it was issued for, and a bounded expiry.[^aix-leastmodelpriv] Not applicable where the deployment's agents act for no human and delegate to no agent. *Evidence:* a sample of delegated credentials showing each field.
- **D2-DELEGATE-LINK.** A credential one agent issues to another links to the delegation it descends from, so a credential minted for one step of a chain fails validation when presented at another. A first-hop credential, issued on a human's authority, has no delegation above it, so the criterion is not applicable where no agent delegates to another. *Evidence:* a second-hop credential showing the field that names its parent.
- **D2-ROTATE.** Automated rotation per credential class: automation rotates every stored credential in the deployment on a schedule set for its class, and the rotation log shows each class rotating on schedule. *Evidence:* the rotation schedule per class and the rotation log.
- **D2-ROTATE-MAP.** A documented consumer-dependency map names, for each stored credential, every consumer that must take the new value when it rotates. *Evidence:* the map, and for one recent rotation, the consumers it named and the record that each took the new value.
- **D2-BASELINE.** A behavioral baseline per NHI: every non-human identity in the deployment has a recorded baseline of its normal activity, such as the resources it calls and where it calls from, and a detection alerts when the identity departs from it. The criterion grades that the baseline and its detection exist, and §Open questions states why no scoring method is graded. *Evidence:* the baseline record for each identity, the detection rule, and an alert it raised or a test of it.
- **D2-COUPLING-MIGRATE.** An active migration plan off coupled credentials: the plan names each coupled credential the inventory records, the person accountable for migrating it and a target date, and no target date has passed without the credential migrated or the date reset by a recorded decision. Not applicable where the inventory records no coupled credential in the deployment. *Evidence:* the plan, set against the inventory's coupled entries and the decisions that reset a date.

### L5 detail

- **D2-REGISTRY.** A unified registry: one registry records every agent in the deployment with the identities and credentials it holds, its owner and its grants, and the deploy pipeline writes each creation, change and retirement to it through an API. For any agent, the registry returns its identity graph: the identities it holds and what each can reach. *Evidence:* the registry export and the pipeline step that writes to it.
- **D2-OWNER-TRANSFER.** Ownership transfer: when an owner leaves or changes role, each agent and non-human identity they own passes to a named successor, and the registry records the transfer. *Evidence:* the trigger from the personnel record, and the transfer records for a sample of owners who left.
- **D2-ADMIN.** Scoped administration: roles grant the administrative rights over agents and their identities, and each role reaches only the agents it names. *Evidence:* the role definitions and their assignments.
- **D2-AUDIT.** Audit-log integration: every creation, change and retirement of an agent, an identity, a credential or a grant writes a record to the organization's audit log. *Evidence:* an audit-log sample covering each event type.
- **D2-DISCOVER.** Shadow-agent discovery: discovery runs on a schedule across the platforms the deployment's agents run on, reports every agent the registry does not hold, and ends each finding with the agent registered or removed. *Evidence:* the discovery schedule and scope, and recent findings with their outcomes.
- **D2-CONDITIONAL.** Risk and conditional access for agent identities: a policy evaluates each agent identity's token request against conditions about the agent, such as its risk level or its attributes, and blocks the request when they fail. Not applicable where the deployment's identity platform offers no such policy for agent identities in general availability, and the control landscape above records which platforms offer one. *Evidence:* the policy configuration, and a sign-in log sample showing the policy's evaluation.
- **D2-IDENTITY-ATTEST.** Identity binding carries cryptographic attestation: the identity provider issues each agent's credential only against a cryptographic attestation of the workload from the platform it runs on, so holding a stored secret cannot establish the identity. A SPIFFE JWT-SVID or a platform-attested identity meets the criterion. Not applicable under the vendor-held identity condition. *Evidence:* the attestation chain behind a sampled credential.
- **D2-COUPLING-ZERO.** No coupled credential remains among the deployment's credentials, whether the agents use them directly or a broker uses them for the agents. *Evidence:* the inventory's coupling field and the coupled-credential migration report.

### L5+ detail

- **D2-TASKTOKEN.** Per-task capability tokens: every capability an agent exercises comes from a token issued for one task and bound cryptographically to its holder, so the token grants nothing to another holder or in another task. [[tenuo-warrant|Tenuo Warrant]] belongs to the class, and the control landscape above records no platform-native implementation. *Evidence:* a per-task token sample with its holder binding.
- **D2-TASKTOKEN-ATTENUATE.** Monotonic attenuation: a task token an agent passes on carries a subset of the capabilities it holds, so authority narrows at each hop. Not applicable where no agent passes a task token on. *Evidence:* a two-hop token sample showing the narrowed scope.
- **D2-FEDERATE.** Multi-vendor federation: agent identities federate across two or more identity platforms from different vendors. *Evidence:* the federation configuration.
- **D2-FEDERATE-RECONCILE.** Cross-platform reconciliation: a reconciliation joins each agent's identities across those platforms into one identity graph. *Evidence:* the reconciliation report.
- **D2-STANDARDS.** Standards contribution: a named member of the organization contributes to a SPIFFE, OAuth or OIDC working group on agent extensions through a pull request, an RFC or specification authorship, and membership alone falls short. *Evidence:* links to the contributions.

## Right-sizing by deployment shape

| Deployment shape | Realistic D2 target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Internal RAG / support chatbot (no/few tools) | L3 | D2-IDENTITY-VERIFY, D2-OWNER and D2-COUPLING carry the level. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026\|The persona]]'s bot sits here; per-task tokens and federation are irrelevant |
| In-suite productivity assistant (Gemini for Workspace, Microsoft 365 Copilot) | L3 | The vendor-held identity condition decides the identity criteria, and D2-DELEGATE and D2-TRACE carry the level |
| Desktop-agent productivity assistant ([[claude-cowork\|Claude Cowork]] class) | L3 | The vendor-held identity condition decides the identity criteria. Connector tokens stay outside the sandbox and the tokens inside it expire within hours, which is D2-NOCRED evidence |
| Data-science / coding copilot | L3 → L4 | Touches secrets and the SDLC; the credential broker and rotation discipline earn their cost |
| MCP / skill provider serving others | L4 → selective L5 | Third-party blast radius raises the bar; scoped tokens per caller, attestation, shadow discovery |
| High-autonomy multi-agent mesh | L5 (selective L5+) | Delegation chains make D2-TASKTOKEN and D2-TASKTOKEN-ATTENUATE load-bearing, the one shape where Warrant-class authority is worth its immaturity cost |

An agent the organization builds on a vendor's platform, such as a Copilot Studio agent, takes the row its own function fits, and the vendor-held identity condition decides its identity criteria.

**Attribution back to a human is the coding-shape D2 test.** The test is D2-TRACE applied to a commit, a tool call or an MCP invocation: each must trace to an agent identity *and* to the human accountable for it. A coding agent that runs on a developer's own credentials fails D2-IDENTITY at L2 before the test is reached, because its actions carry the developer's identity alone. An organization that cannot separate agent-authored from human-authored change once it lands in the repository cannot scope a review policy, trace a defect to the tool that produced it, or measure which harness introduced what. No first-party harness feature supplies this across vendors; it currently requires a [[endor-labs-ai-code-governance|third-party control plane]] or in-house instrumentation. Because D2 caps effective D5 and D7, an unattributable coding fleet also caps those domains. See [[generative-coding-deployment-shape-2026|Generative Coding Deployment Shapes]] §Fleet and parallel.

The [[lethal-trifecta|lethal-trifecta]] test lowers the target level. An agent with no private-data reach or no external-comms path does not need the per-task-token and federation tail. A sound L3 with the trifecta broken is recorded as an intentional trade-off in the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-dependency-rules|effective-score]] strategic-rationale field.

## Cost model

Entra Agent ID riding the directory, plus the Entra ID P1 entitlement E5 already includes, sets the near-zero licensing this table reports, on Microsoft's terms as documented in May 2026.[^idgov] Agent Identity and the auth-manager credential vault cover the same ground for a Google Cloud buyer — per-agent identity, credential lifecycle, zero-credential context for Google Cloud resources — but stop there: the control landscape above marks conditional and risk-based access Microsoft-only, and records no condition on an agent's risk or attributes on Google Cloud. Coupled-credential migration labor and audit-log run-rate do not change between the two.

| Level | Licensing | Operational labor | Run-rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| L2 | ~0 | ~0.25 FTE: NHI inventory, owner fields | — |
| L3 | ~0 for an E5 incumbent (Entra Agent ID rides the directory)[^idgov] | ~0.5 FTE: per-agent identity rollout, coupled/decoupled classification, pipeline binding | identity sign-in log volume |
| L4 | ~0 native (Managed Identities / Entra); a commercial credential broker only where no platform-native path exists | the dominant cost: rotation automation + the coupled-credential migration project | — |
| L5 | ~0 native on the Agent 365 path (Conditional Access for agents needs Entra ID P1, included in E5)[^idgov]; some Agent ID / governance features meter on Entra P2 or an add-on | ownership-transfer process, shadow-AI triage, attestation-chain upkeep | audit-log ingestion scales with agent count |
| L5+ | Tenuo OSS ~0 license | high: capability-token design, federation reconciliation, standards participation | verifier compute (negligible per call) |

On Microsoft's May 2026 terms, licensing is near-zero for the E5 incumbent through L5, and the spend is the coupled-credential migration labor and the audit-log run-rate, which scales with agent count. Microsoft's current documentation puts the agent security and governance features behind a Microsoft Agent 365 license, an add-on to E5 that Microsoft 365 E7 includes.[^entra-whatis] Conditional Access for agents requires one for each user, and ID Protection for agents will, with enforcement of both still to come.[^ca][^idp] ID Governance for agent identities requires Agent 365 alongside at least Entra ID P1, or E7.[^idgov] An E5 incumbent that meets D2-BASELINE at L4, or D2-REGISTRY and D2-CONDITIONAL at L5, through these features therefore needs the Agent 365 license under the current documentation.

## Customer critiques folded in

- *"L5 names a just-GA'd product we cannot procure in 12–18 months."* Addressed: L5 is capability-stated, so any product in the approved-vendor pipeline that delivers a criterion's capability can meet it, and the production-maturity qualifier credits it from the documented date it entered the organization's production. A product still in procurement, or a date on a roadmap, meets nothing until then. Entra Agent ID reached general availability in April 2026.[^entra-ga]
- *"Microsoft is thin on per-task tokens."* Confirmed and bounded: the one genuine D2 residual gap is per-task holder-bound tokens, which sit at L5+ as D2-TASKTOKEN, so most buyers never need them.
- *"Cost was invisible."* Addressed: the migration labor and SIEM run-rate are named; licensing is near-zero for incumbents on Microsoft's May 2026 terms, and §Cost model names the Agent 365 license its current documentation adds.
- *"Zero-credential via Managed Identities is a cheap win."* Preserved: D2-NOCRED reads "a broker or a credential-less identity model issues what the agent needs", so the Azure Managed Identities path counts without a separate credential-proxy purchase.

## Open questions

- Tenuo production-readiness (early-stage OSS, no independent enterprise-deployment evidence) is the variable that would move D2-TASKTOKEN from L5+ to L5. The vault's reading of that product dates to August 2026 and records no general-availability declaration, no version tag and no production deployment; its reading of the company dates to May 2026 and records the public examples as demo-shaped with production case studies pending. Tenuo's maintainers have since classified release 0.3.0 of 2026-09-11 as "Production/Stable".[^tenuo-pypi] That classification is the vendor's own, and the variable above asks for independent deployment evidence. Re-check quarterly. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]] and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d5-egress-network|D5]] graded the same capability at L5 until September 2026 and now hold it at L5+ on the same reading, so one re-check answers for three domains.
- AWS AgentCore Identity has been GA since 2025-10-13 and GCP Agent Identity since 2026-04-22.[^awsga][^gcpga] Google's Agent Identity overview lists Agent Runtime, Gemini Enterprise, and Cloud Run as the services that support it and derives each identity from the Google Cloud resource hosting the agent, so coverage for an agent hosted outside Google Cloud is unstated there.[^gcpid]
- D2 maps cleanly to FFIEC/GLBA authentication-and-access expectations and NCUA third-party-NHI scrutiny; the mapping is deferred to the forthcoming FFIEC/GLBA crosswalk.
- Behavioral trust and reputation scoring for agent identities has no standardised method. The [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] names it as an agentic authentication element with decay and circuit breakers, states that industry scoring methods are not yet standardised, and admits it only as supplementary to identity, policy, and monitoring.[^aix-mac] D2-BASELINE is graded on existence for the same reason, and a scoring-quality criterion is unavailable until a method is published.

## Control anchors

[[agentic-ai-security-cmm-crosswalk|The crosswalk]] anchors `MODEL ACCESS CONTROL` to this domain for machine-to-machine and inter-agent calls, and records the qualification that a compromised principal authenticates correctly, so this domain's grade bounds who is calling rather than what the caller intends.
