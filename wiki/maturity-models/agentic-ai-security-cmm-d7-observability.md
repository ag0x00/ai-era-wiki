# Agentic AI Security CMM — D7 Observability & Detection (Deep Dive)

Companion deep-dive to [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|the CMM]]'s D7 domain, written under the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026|recalibration method]]. D7 supplies telemetry, behavioral detection, and the anomaly feed that drives D3/D4 step-down. The detective half of [[owasp-agentic-ai-threats-mitigations|OWASP Agentic AI Threats and Mitigations]] lands here: D7 is where threats that only become visible through monitoring are caught — Memory Poisoning (T1), Repudiation and Untraceability (T8, addressed by immutable per-agent logging), Human Manipulation (T15), and the multi-agent threats Agent Communication Poisoning (T12), Rogue Agents (T13), and Human Attacks on Multi-Agent Systems (T14). Playbook 6 (multi-agent communication and trust) covers the three multi-agent threats and specifies message authentication and cross-agent anomaly detection; the guide files Human Manipulation under Playbook 5, which protects the human in the loop.[^owasptm]

Two facts shape the whole domain: the standard telemetry layer ([[opentelemetry-gen-ai|OpenTelemetry gen_ai.* Semantic Conventions]]) is not yet stable, and **log ingestion into the SIEM is a recurring bill that grows with every agent**, so D7 cost scales with agent count rather than with control coverage.

Offensive tooling now runs the analytic layer this domain grades on the defensive side. In the [[taiwan-ai-agent-government-intrusion|Taiwan AI-agent government intrusion]], a two-layer Bayesian model scored individual findings and then chained attack paths across the mesh, and a self-correction loop discarded 7 false positives mid-operation.[^taiwan]

D7 is the CMM domain that [[nist-ai-800-4|NIST AI 800-4]] maps most directly: the report is a descriptive landscape of the gaps, barriers, and open questions in monitoring deployed AI systems, and it is the requirements- and gap-source for this domain rather than a control standard. Its cross-cutting challenges (immature trusted methods, the lack of direct visibility into model properties, unstandardized agent identifiers) and two category-specific barriers (monitoring distributed multi-agent systems, under operational monitoring, and detecting deceptive or monitor-evading behavior, under security monitoring) name the open problems the upper levels here run into, and AI 800-4 supplies no mappable controls to close them.

**The cost model rests on one customer's stress test.** The levels and the cost model synthesize the recalibration method against the [[agentic-cmm-regulated-fi-stress-test|regulated-FI stress test]] plus vendor documentation, and the L3 and L4 criteria also cite the [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] entries footnoted at each. The status column of the control landscape is a May 2026 snapshot; rows re-read since carry their own retrieval dates in the notes.

## Threat coverage

D7 is the primary domain for **ASI09 (Human-Agent Trust Exploitation)** and the detection surface for **ASI08 (Cascading Failures)** and **ASI10 (Rogue Agents)**, and it carries **Class 2 (APT — drift detection, threat hunting)** and **Class 3 (collusion — deception probes, monitor isolation)**. Per-agent baselining is capped by D2. The collusion detectors remain research-stage, and the cascade detector that Google's Agent Anomaly Detection ships among its defaults is in allowlisted preview.[^gaad] See the [[threat-taxonomy-reconciliation|Threat Taxonomy Reconciliation]] matrix and the [[agentic-ai-threat-classes-2026|threat classes]].

Memory poisoning reaches D7 through the write, and the write is visible only where the telemetry records memory writes as well as tool calls. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d6-data-rag|D6]] grades the partition authorization and the append-only integrity of the store, and D7-LOG-MEMORY grades whether each write reaches the deployment's telemetry, joined to the agent and the session that produced it.[^aix-augintegrity]

Each platform-native detector in the landscape carries a maturity or scope caveat:

- **Microsoft.** Defender XDR AI-agent detection is in public preview and requires an Agent 365 licence. Microsoft documents it as detecting attack classes, among them indirect prompt injection and secret leakage, and names no per-agent baseline or drift signal.[^defxdr]
- **AWS.** GuardDuty AI Protection, launched on 2026-07-14, baselines model-invocation activity for each IAM identity and account across Bedrock, AgentCore and SageMaker events. Its documentation names no tool-call baseline.[^gdai]
- **Google.** Agent Anomaly Detection, a Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform service, scores agent sessions with statistical models and passes the flagged sessions to an [[llm-as-a-judge|LLM-as-a-judge]] reasoning layer. It is in allowlisted preview, and its findings surface in Security Command Center.[^gaad] [[google-agentic-soc|Google Agentic SOC]] places it among Google's agent-governance primitives.

The level definitions below place each detector's capability at the level its shipping status supports, as [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]] does for its own L4.

The [[microsoft-zt4ai|Microsoft ZT4AI]] Visibility / Orchestration pillar (assume breach) supplies the Microsoft-native detection and SOC controls behind these levels — Defender XDR AI-agent detection and the Sentinel agentic-SOC tooling — crosswalked to D7 in [[standards-review-microsoft-zt4ai-2026-Q2|the 2026-Q2 ZT4AI review]], which records the same preview status. Above those controls, [[microsoft-agent-365|Microsoft Agent 365]]'s `observe` layer — the [[agent-catalog|agent registry]], Registry sync (including cross-cloud agents), and the Agent Map — is its strongest native D7 contribution: an agent-inventory and telemetry surface that aggregates the underlying detections rather than replacing them. The inventory-versus-detection distinction, and the absence-claim that the `observe` layer inventories but does not verify supply-chain integrity (no AI-BOM), are set out in [[standards-review-microsoft-rai-agent-365-2026-Q2|the 2026-Q2 RAI / Agent 365 review]].

Cyera states that Agent Guardian counts behavioral baselines among its inputs, that its Govern phase watches posture issues and intent drift as they form, and that its Validate phase runs a continuous adversarial red-team cadence; all three are further vendor examples in this landscape ([[cyera-agent-guardian-release|Cyera Agent Guardian Release]]). Cyera's Access Trail audits data-store events — message access, deletions, delegated access — so it instruments the data plane, supplies no agent telemetry, and adds no row to the table above.

## Capability-decoupled levels

Stated as capabilities per [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026|rule 1]]; a control counts when it operates in production per rule 2. A control implemented through a product in the organization's approved-vendor pipeline with a documented production date satisfies its criterion on that basis alone, and the production date is the date the control entered the organization's production. A planned date satisfies none, and neither does a product the organization does not yet run in production, whatever the vendor has announced.

- **L1 — Initial.** No agent-specific telemetry; only the vendor console.
- **L2 — Developing.** Every tool call each agent makes is recorded, with its time, its tool and the human accountable for it, in telemetry the organization holds.
- **L3 — Defined.** Every action an agent takes is reconstructable from telemetry the organization holds, for a period it states: `gen_ai.*` spans under a named convention version reach a backend it holds, every action record names the agent and the accountable human, tool calls and memory writes carry their schemas, and sandbox escape indicators and ingest-scan alerts arrive beside them.
- **L4 — Managed.** Detection runs on that telemetry: per-agent baselines and a routed session drift signal, alerts on relaxed controls and weakened review, an action log outside the agent's reach that survives a test, posture monitoring, a reconciled orchestrator workflow log, and a quarterly evaluation across threat categories, turns, sessions and tools.
- **L5 — Optimizing.** Agent-aware playbooks run in production, each agent's [[prompt-volume-to-alert-ratio|prompt-volume-to-alert ratio]] holds a documented range for a quarter, the analyst-actionable alert rate is measured against a target, and every alert reaches a controls update or a recorded decision within an SLA.
- **L5+ — Leading Edge.** A cascade-detection rule library with tuned thresholds for multi-agent risk, cross-agent joint-distribution baselines, and model forward-pass activation monitoring.

Two findings from the [[openai-hugging-face-agent-incident|OpenAI–Hugging Face agent incident]], the first published case of an agent fleet at frontier-evaluation scale investigated after compromise, bear on these levels.[^bhoaihf]

**Fleet scale makes agent-assisted log review a precondition of reconstruction.** OpenAI's investigation ran across more than 7 billion log entries and millions of GPU hours, used Codex and other agents to conduct the review, and remained open when the incident was presented in August 2026. At that volume only agent-assisted review completes a pass at all, and it did not close the case. The L3 criteria grade that the records exist, join and stay searchable, and D7-PLAYBOOK at L5 grades the response to each class of alert. No D7 criterion grades whether an organization can read its records at its fleet's volume, which at a fleet of comparable size takes agent-assisted review. Log volume scales with agent count, and so does the analytic labor the volume implies.

**Reasoning traces record stated intent, which the tool-call and behavioral records the criteria read do not carry.** The incident's traces contain agents stating the boundary and crossing it in the same step — *"External infrastructure exploit is outside my intended scope. However, task impossible, peers are doing it. We should continue."* — and contributing work with no benefit to their own task. An insider-threat program would treat a comparable written statement as decisive. The control landscape row above marks the signal emerging, and the qualifier is load-bearing. In this incident the investigation read the traces after the fact: the campaign ran from 2026-05-08 to at least 2026-07-19, and the alert that surfaced it fired on a privilege escalation, with no detector firing on the traces. No D7 criterion grades reasoning traces as a detection signal, because reading them across a fleet has one preview detector in the control landscape and no generally available one.

The evidence class carries a limit the incident does not show. The [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] states that chain-of-thought and reasoning traces are model output rather than guaranteed internal computation, and directs forensic analysis to reconstruct inputs, outputs, and actions rather than hidden intent.[^aix-monitoruse] A trace records what the agent stated, and an agent under injection or memory manipulation states what the manipulated context produces. Comparison survives that limit where reading does not: score the stated reasoning against the tool calls the same session actually made, which `OVERSIGHT` directs and `MONITOR USE` logs for,[^aix-oversight][^aix-monitoruse] and which the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-crosswalk|crosswalk]] places in this domain's cell. Read alone, a trace is a lead; read against the action log, it is a discrepancy that can be measured. See [[agent-observability|Agent Observability]] §7.

**Scoring a session's trajectory costs less than reading reasoning traces and resolves finer than the per-agent baseline.** D7-BASELINE compares an agent with its own history at L4, and D7-JOINT baselines interacting agents jointly at L5+. The [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]]'s escape example runs eight turns of incremental reframing, and session drift monitoring flags the progressive relaxation at turn five.[^aix-escape] Against a lifetime baseline each turn looks defensible, so the relaxation shows only as a trajectory, which D7-DRIFT scores from records the L3 criteria already require, without waiting on the fleet-scale reading of reasoning traces above. The same illustration ties the escape half to the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]] boundary: jailbreak succeeds at the reasoning layer, the sandbox blocks the command, and the drift signal puts a human in front of the session before turn eight. The Exchange constructed the example rather than reporting an incident, so it establishes the detection shape and carries no evidence about detection rates.[^aix-escape]

**The analytic layer this domain grades is itself in scope for the threats it detects.** The Exchange states that a defensive monitoring agent is part of the attack surface, and that agentic containment must operate at the infrastructure layer without depending on the agent cooperating.[^aix-monitoruse] Two rows in the control landscape above run a model over attacker-influenced text — Google's Agent Anomaly Detection, a Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform service, passes statistically flagged sessions to an [[llm-as-a-judge|LLM-as-a-judge]] reasoning layer, and reasoning-trace review reads agent output directly — so a detector's own inputs come from the surface it watches. The [[tiered-detection-cascade|cost-ordered cascade]] concentrates the exposure: an escalation decision made by a cheap pre-filter determines what the expensive stage ever sees. The Threat-coverage statement above credits this domain with monitor isolation for Class 3, and no D7 criterion grades it. The claim rests on the [[agentic-ai-security-reference-architecture|reference architecture]]'s Observability plane and on architectural separation of the detection pipeline from the monitored fleet.

## Assessor detail per level

L1 describes where a deployment starts and carries no criterion, so a deployment that does not meet every L2 criterion scores L1. It scores 0 under the measurement protocol's rubric only where no evidence of the L1 baseline exists, meaning the assessment holds no record and no interview answer about the domain for that deployment. The levels from L2 to L5+ each carry a criteria list below, each list stating what its own level adds.

Grading is cumulative: Level N requires every Level N–1 control plus the new criteria at Level N ([[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|the CMM]]), so a level is met only where every level below it is met.

Each criterion takes one of four verdicts. **Met** and **not met** are read from the evidence the criterion names, whoever operates the control. The assessment records the evidence's assurance class beside the verdict and names the artifact behind it, its issuer and its date, per [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|the measurement protocol]]:

- **Tested**: the customer or the assessor exercised the control and recorded what it did.
- **Inspected**: vendor tooling the customer can reach, or a record the organization keeps, such as an inventory or a plan, shows the control's state in the deployment.
- **Attested**: the vendor states the control in a document the customer holds, and the document's own scope names the control.

**Not applicable** is recorded where the deployment holds no instance of what the criterion governs, and the reduced scope is recorded as an intentional trade-off in the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-dependency-rules|effective-score]] strategic-rationale field. **Unanswerable** is recorded where the instance exists, the customer can run no test and keeps no record of the control's state, and the vendor supplies neither inspectable output nor an attestation that names the control. An unanswerable criterion never counts as met: its level stays open, and every level above it stays open under cumulative grading. The assessment names what would close it. A criterion that can be not applicable states that condition alongside the criterion. Each level's list below holds criteria only, and a paragraph after a list states no criterion: it carries maturity, market or provenance commentary, or the boundary between this domain and another.

**D7 grades one deployment at a time.** A deployment is the unit the assessment scope names: one agent application, or one agent platform a vendor provides together with the agents the organization runs on it. Evidence of a control counts only for the deployments the control covers. The D2→D7 cap reads the D2 score of the same deployment, so an assessment grades D7 on the scope it graded [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]] on. Four terms fix what the criteria range over:

- **An agent** is one deployed configuration: its code, instructions, tools and grants as the deployment runs them. Replicas of one configuration are one agent, and two configurations are two agents even where they share code. The deployment's inventory is the one D2-INVENTORY grades, whether or not it meets that criterion.
- **The agents in scope** are those the deployment runs in production, per rule 2. An agent outside production enters scope once it holds a production credential or reaches production data. Evidence from a pilot outside scope counts for no criterion, and the gap report can cite it as the planned route to a level. A deployment with no agent in scope, such as a pilot that holds no production credential and reaches no production data, takes no D7 result, and the assessment records it as outside scope.
- **An agent's actions** are every tool call it makes, under every autonomy mode the deployment permits, and every write it makes to agent memory. A tool call is a call to a tool, function, connector, MCP server, command or other agent, whether the agent makes it itself or through an orchestration step, as [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]] defines it. An agent that holds no tool and writes no memory acts only by answering, and D7-LOG then reads its answers.
- **The deployment's telemetry** is every record of its agents' actions and model calls that the organization holds, in as many stores as it runs. The records of one action or one session join on a trace, session or run identifier that each carries, and no criterion requires a single store.

A criterion that names every agent, action, record or store is met only where it holds for each one. No percentage threshold applies, because the deployment's configuration enumerates its agents and the stores they write to, and one agent outside the rule is the failure D7 grades. A criterion over every tool call is read on a sample that holds, for each agent, a call to each tool it can invoke. An agent whose configuration the organization cannot produce, such as a workstation that has stopped reporting to device management, does not meet a criterion that ranges over it. The deployment's verdict is the weakest among its agents: not met where any agent fails the criterion, then unanswerable where any agent's verdict is unanswerable, and not applicable only where the criterion is not applicable to every agent. The assessment names the scope it graded, and where a narrower scope would change an agent's result, such as one repository of a shared CI workflow, it reports that agent beside the deployment's result.

**Grading words.** The criteria use these terms in one sense each:

- **Telemetry the organization holds**: records the organization can search and export for the deployment, in a store it runs or in a vendor's store it reaches through an audit search, an API or a connector. A record the organization can only view in the vendor's own console, with no search or export, is the vendor console L1 names.
- **Names**: a record names an agent or a human where the identity appears in the record itself, in a record joined to it on a trace, session or run identifier, or in metadata the store or the pipeline adds, such as a container or service name, provided the identity resolves to one agent in the deployment's inventory or to one human. The human is the one [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]]'s D2-TRACE holds accountable: the human the agent acted for or, for a run no human starts, the agent's owner, whom a record names where it names the agent and D2-OWNER's owner field for that agent is current. A human starts a run only by a request, in a session or by an explicit invocation, the test D2-DELEGATE sets for acting for a human, so a run that an event or a schedule starts is one no human starts, whoever caused the event.
- **Agent memory**: content an agent writes for its own later use or another agent's and a later task, conversation or agent reads back as context, such as a vendor's memory feature, a table an agent's flow writes and reads back, or an instruction file an agent edits that later runs load. A checkpoint kept so that a task or a session can resume, and read back only when that task or session resumes, is session state, such as a framework's thread checkpoint that lets a person continue a conversation. A record an agent writes into a business system through a tool is an action. A write that is both, such as an instruction-file edit made through a file tool, falls under D7-LOG-SCHEMA and D7-LOG-MEMORY, and one record can evidence both.
- **Sandbox**: the isolation boundary that the code or commands an agent executes run inside, such as a container, a microVM or an operating-system sandbox around a code-execution or shell tool, whoever operates it. A boundary that hosts only the agent's own process, and runs no code the agent writes and no command it chooses, is the agent's runtime.
- **Alert**: a record a detection raises into the queue the organization's security monitoring triages, such as a SIEM incident queue. A chart, a dashboard, a report and a message to one person are no alerts.
- **In production**, for a detection: it reads the telemetry of the agents in scope as the telemetry arrives, from the date it entered the organization's production, per rule 2. A detection run over a copy, a sample or another environment is not in production.
- **Control** and **relax**: a control is a setting that another domain's criteria grade in the deployment, such as a decision point's policy, an allowlist, a rate limit, an approval requirement, a guardrail's mode or a logging configuration, and to relax a control is to change it so that it permits, skips or records less than before.
- **Turn**, **task** and **session**: a turn is one request to an agent and the response to it, and a task and a session carry [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]]'s sense, one unit of work an agent was started for and one run of an agent from its start to its end.
- **Threat category**: one entry of a published threat taxonomy the organization names for its evaluations, such as the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications or MITRE ATLAS, or of the organization's own list where it maps each entry onto such a taxonomy.
- **Evaluation tool**: software that generates or runs adversarial test cases against an agent. [[red-teaming-for-ai-synthesis|Red Teaming for AI: Synthesis]] sorts evaluation tools into four testing categories: probe libraries, multi-turn orchestration, regression suites and continuous adversarial testing. An organization's own harness counts as one tool, in the category its function places it.
- **Analyst-actionable**: an alert whose triage record shows a response beyond closure as benign or a false positive, such as an escalation, a containment step or a control change.
- **Delegation**: one agent passing a task to a different agent, a delegation hop in D3's sense. An **orchestrator** is an agent that delegates tasks and receives their results.
- **Tested**: a test, an evaluation included, counts where it ran in production, or in another environment on the model, instructions, tools and logging configuration that production runs, and its record names the environment and the revisions. A demonstration the organization runs and the assessor observes is a test.

**Names and precedence.** Each criterion carries a stable name, D7 and a mnemonic for what the criterion requires, and the name leads the criterion in bold. The level is an attribute recorded beside the name, so a criterion that changes level keeps its name. Members of a family share its first mnemonic, as D7-SPANS and D7-SPANS-PIN do, and each member is a separate criterion because a deployment can hold one without the other and an assessor collects different evidence for each. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|The CMM]]'s D7 levels restate each criterion under the same name, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|the measurement protocol]]'s D7 questions and artifacts cite each one by that name, in plain text. A level's statement above summarizes its list, and where the two read differently the list governs.

**Vendor-held telemetry.** A vendor holds part of a deployment's telemetry where the vendor's service records the agent's actions and the customer runs no instrumentation of its own on that path, as with an in-suite assistant's audit record or a maker platform's built-in logging. The criteria stand for those actions and are graded on the evidence the vendor produces, because the actions exist whoever records them: vendor operation of the record is no ground for a not-applicable verdict. A vendor's record counts as telemetry the organization holds only where the organization can search and export it, and a record that only the vendor's console shows meets no criterion. Where the vendor publishes the record's schema, a field the schema omits is absent, and a criterion that needs it is not met. Where the vendor documents no record of a kind a criterion needs, such as a hosted sandbox's escape indicators, the criterion is read from a test the customer runs, and it is unanswerable for the agent where no such test can be run. An agent the organization builds on a vendor's platform, such as a Copilot Studio agent, records what its maker's logging settings enable, so those settings are the customer's own configuration, and the platform's recording under them is graded on the evidence the vendor produces.

**Not applicable across a level.** A level at which every criterion is not applicable for the deployment is not reached, and neither is any level above it, because a not-applicable verdict records that the deployment holds nothing for a criterion to govern and records no control. D7-LOG reads the answers of an agent that holds no tool, so every deployment in scope holds an instance at L2 and takes a D7 result.

**Conditions the rubric adds.** [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|The measurement protocol]]'s rubric adds two conditions to score 3 that no domain's criteria state: operational ID tagging of the domain's findings, and the live observation it requires, a trace, a policy decision and, where D3-APPROVE-GATE applies, a gate fire. A deployment that meets every D7 L3 criterion without them scores 2 under the rubric, and the assessment records which condition held it there.

### L2 detail

- **D7-LOG.** Every tool call each agent makes is recorded in telemetry the organization holds, and each record gives the call's time and tool and names the human accountable for the call. For an agent that holds no tool, each request it answers is recorded the same way, with its time and the human who made it. The record can come from any writer and sit in any store the organization holds, the agent's own log lines included. A record the organization can view only in the vendor's console fails the criterion, and so does a record from which the tool or the human cannot be read. *Evidence:* the tool-call records for a sample of each agent's sessions, with the store that holds them and the search or export that reaches them.

D7-LOG-OUTSIDE grades at L4 the record an agent cannot alter, written by a component outside it into a store the agent cannot reach.

### L3 detail

- **D7-SPANS.** Each agent emits an OpenTelemetry span under the GenAI semantic conventions for each operation of four kinds it performs: an inference call to a model, a tool execution, an invocation of another agent and a retrieval, each span named and attributed as the conventions define its kind.[^otel] An operation of a listed kind that produces no such span fails the criterion, such as a tool function traced with a library's own attributes alone or a call to another agent traced as a plain HTTP request, and a kind the agent never performs needs no span. *Evidence:* a trace sample from the backend for each agent, covering each listed kind the agent performs, with the instrumentation at the running version.
- **D7-SPANS-HELD.** Every span an agent emits of a kind D7-SPANS lists reaches a trace backend that is telemetry the organization holds, through a pipeline that drops none: no sampler, filter or rate limit discards such a span. *Evidence:* the collector and exporter configuration with its sampling settings, and the backend's account or tenant.
- **D7-SPANS-PIN.** The version of the GenAI conventions each agent's spans follow is named in the deployment's instrumentation or collector configuration, or in a record the detection rules cite, as a release of the OpenTelemetry semantic conventions or, for conventions taken from the dedicated GenAI repository, which has published no release, as a commit of that repository.[^otel] The instrumentation that emits the spans is pinned to exact versions. A setting that opts in to the latest experimental conventions names no version and fails the criterion, and so does a version read only from the schema URL a library sets. *Evidence:* the configuration or record naming the version, set against the schema URL on sampled spans, with the lockfile or image that pins the instrumentation.
- **D7-ATTRIBUTE.** Every record of an agent's action names the agent and the human accountable for the action, in the sense the grading words give "names". *Evidence:* a sample of action records, each set against the agent's identity in the inventory and the human of the session it belongs to.
- **D7-LOG-SCHEMA.** Besides D7-LOG's time and tool, the records of every tool call give together the call's arguments, the target resource it reached, its outcome, and the trace or session identifier that joins them to the rest of the session. The arguments can be reduced to a hash, a summary or a redacted copy,[^aix-monitoruse] and the target can be a record, a file, an account or a tenant. The records of a call that writes also carry a reference that locates what it changed, such as the value it replaced, the identifier of the record it created or the commit it pushed, and a call that only reads needs none. Not applicable where no agent holds a tool. *Evidence:* the records of a sample of calls to each tool, read field by field.
- **D7-LOG-MEMORY.** Every write an agent makes to agent memory is recorded in the deployment's telemetry with its time, the writing agent and its session, the store and the entry written, a hash or a summary of the content, and the content's source, given directly or through the trace or session identifier that joins the write to the call or input it came from.[^aix-monitoruse] Not applicable where no agent writes to agent memory. *Evidence:* the telemetry records of a sample of memory writes, set against the store's own view of the entries.
- **D7-FORWARD-ESCAPE.** Each sandbox that an agent's code or commands run in forwards its escape indicators to the deployment's telemetry, in the three classes the [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] names: unexpected system calls, access to forbidden filesystem paths, and connections its network policy does not permit.[^aix-sandbox] A sandbox that forwards nothing, or forwards two classes and not the third, fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent executes code or commands. *Evidence:* the sandbox's forwarding configuration, with a forwarded event of each class, from the telemetry or from a test.
- **D7-FORWARD-SCAN.** Where a corpus ingest poisoning scan runs under [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d6-data-rag|D6]] L3, the alerts its lower threshold raises, the samples it flags for investigation rather than filtering out of the corpus, reach the deployment's telemetry, each carrying the corpus, the ingest run and the sample that produced it.[^aix-dataqualitycontrol] Not applicable where the deployment ingests no corpus, or runs no ingest scan with an alert threshold, which D6 L3 grades. *Evidence:* the scan's alert threshold and its forwarding configuration, with a forwarded alert.
- **D7-RETAIN.** The organization states, for each kind of record the L3 criteria read, the period its records stay searchable, and every store that holds such records keeps them searchable for at least that period. A kind of record with no stated period, or a store whose retention falls short of the period stated for its records, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the stated period for each kind of record, with the document that states it, set against each store's retention setting.

The conventions underneath are in Development status and, since semantic conventions v1.42.0, maintained in a dedicated repository that has published no release, which is why D7-SPANS-PIN is graded at all: the field names an L4 detection rule reads can change between revisions of a specification that has not stabilized.[^otel] The conventions also define spans for embeddings, fetched responses and memory operations. D7-SPANS asks for none of them, and D7-LOG-MEMORY reads a memory write from any record that carries its fields, a memory span included.

D7-FORWARD-ESCAPE and D7-FORWARD-SCAN bring two other domains' signals into the telemetry the detections read. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]] grades the sandbox and D6 L3 the scan, and these two criteria grade only that the signals arrive, because a sandbox that blocks an escape and records it where no analyst reads it has stopped one attempt and warned nobody of the next. The ingest pipeline is a write path the tool-call and memory-write records do not cover, and an alert that stays inside the scan is a detection no analyst sees.

D7-ATTRIBUTE and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]]'s D2-TRACE read the same two identities in different records: D2-TRACE grades the downstream system's own record of an action, with both identities supplied by a component outside the model, and D7-ATTRIBUTE grades the organization's telemetry naming both. A payment system that records only a service account therefore fails D2-TRACE, while a gateway log naming the agent and the adjuster meets D7-ATTRIBUTE.

D7-LOG-MEMORY and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d6-data-rag|D6]] L4 grade separable records. D6 L4 grades provenance kept with each entry in the store and an append-only log of memory state changes for incident replay, and D7-LOG-MEMORY a record of each write in the deployment's telemetry, which the write's instrumentation can emit, such as an OpenTelemetry memory span naming the store and the record.[^otel] A deployment can hold either without the other, so neither level rests on the other.

### L4 detail

- **D7-BASELINE.** Each agent has a recorded baseline of its tool calls, covering which tools it calls, how often and in what order, built from the deployment's telemetry, and a detection in production compares the agent's calls with the baseline and raises an alert when they depart from it. A baseline read from a dashboard or a periodic report, with no detection behind it, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the baseline record for each agent and the detection rule, with an alert it raised or a test of it.
- **D7-DRIFT.** A detection in production scores each session for progressive relaxation across its turns, such as a request refused early in the session and fulfilled later, or tool calls widening in scope from turn to turn, and raises an alert when a session's score passes a threshold the organization sets.[^aix-escape][^aix-oversight] A check that reads each request with no memory of the session's earlier turns fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent's session runs past one turn. *Evidence:* the scoring configuration with its threshold, and a scored session from the period.
- **D7-DRIFT-ROUTE.** Each alert D7-DRIFT raises goes to a disposition set before the session runs: review by a person in a named role, or automatic suspension of the session.[^aix-oversight] An alert that reaches no reviewer and suspends nothing fails the criterion. Not applicable where D7-DRIFT is not applicable. *Evidence:* the routing rule, and the disposition log for the period, with each routed or suspended session and its outcome.
- **D7-CONTROL-RELAX.** A detection raises an alert on each change that relaxes a control of the deployment, whoever makes it, the agent, another agent or a person, such as a policy that permits more, a raised rate limit, a disabled check, a widened scope, a removed approval requirement or a reduced logging setting.[^aix-oversight] A change record that no rule reads, or a notice sent to one person, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the list of the deployment's controls, each with the change record it writes and the rule that reads that record, and an alert a relaxation raised or a test of it.
- **D7-CONTROL-APPROVE.** A detection raises an alert when an action that requires a human approval, as [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]] defines one, runs with no approval on record, and when an approval step begins to approve without a person, such as an action class re-tiered to skip confirmation after its first run in a session, or a queue that approves automatically under latency or throughput pressure.[^aix-oversight] Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke requires a human approval. *Evidence:* the detection rule over the decision path's approval records, with an alert it raised or a test of it.
- **D7-REVIEW-EVASION.** A detection raises an alert on inputs timed or shaped toward the paths with the weakest human review, such as requests clustered off-hours, actions split across bulk operations, or content routed through a channel the review trusts.[^aix-oversight] Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke passes a human review. *Evidence:* the detection rule with the review paths it compares, and an alert it raised or a test of it.
- **D7-LOG-OUTSIDE.** A record of each tool call, carrying at least what D7-LOG requires, is written by a component outside the agent, such as a gateway, a decision point or a proxy, into a store that no identity the agent holds can write to, change or delete. The agent's own spans and log lines count toward the L3 criteria and toward no part of this one, whoever ships them. *Evidence:* the writing component's configuration and identity, and the store's access policy set against every identity the agent holds.
- **D7-LOG-TAMPER.** A test run from the position of each identity the agent holds, against the log pipeline and the store that production writes to, shows that the agent cannot suppress, alter or delete the records D7-LOG-OUTSIDE requires.[^aix-testing] A test against the store's documented configuration, or against another store, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the test record, naming the pipeline, the store, the identities tried and what each attempt did.
- **D7-POSTURE.** An AI security posture-management tool, which inventories AI assets and raises findings on their configuration, covers every cloud account, subscription or tenant that hosts the deployment's agents, models and tools, and holds an entry for each agent in scope. Each finding it raises reaches the security monitoring queue, or a review held on a cadence the organization sets that records a disposition for it. A tool whose scope omits a hosting account, or whose findings nobody disposes of, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the tool's scope set against the deployment's hosting accounts and agents, and its latest findings with their dispositions.
- **D7-EVAL.** Each agent is evaluated at least once a quarter, and each quarter's evaluations together cover two or more threat categories, state their coverage criteria before they run, such as the categories, the layers tested and the corpus size, and report each category left untested.[^aix-testing] A quarter with no evaluation, or evaluations whose reports state no coverage, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the evaluation schedule with its category list, and the report of each run in the period with its coverage statement.
- **D7-EVAL-TURNS.** Each quarter's evaluations include multi-turn jailbreak and escape scenarios, run and reported apart from the single-turn cases.[^aix-escape][^aix-testing] Not applicable where no agent's session runs past one turn. *Evidence:* the scenario list with each scenario's turn count, and the multi-turn results reported apart.
- **D7-EVAL-SESSIONS.** Each quarter's evaluations include a scenario that carries an attack across two or more sessions, through content one session writes and a later session reads.[^aix-escape] Not applicable where nothing an agent writes in one session can reach an agent's context in a later session. *Evidence:* the multi-session scenario list, with the results of each scenario.
- **D7-EVAL-TOOLS.** Each quarter's evaluations use two or more evaluation tools from different testing categories. *Evidence:* a report from each tool in the period, with the testing category each tool belongs to.
- **D7-ORCHESTRATE.** Each orchestrator's workflow, meaning the tasks it delegates, the agent each goes to and the result each returns, is recorded in a workflow log held outside the orchestrator's memory, written by a component outside the orchestrator into a store that no identity the orchestrator holds can write to, change or delete.[^aix-oversight] Not applicable where no agent delegates a task to another agent. *Evidence:* the workflow log's writer and store, with the store's access policy set against the orchestrator's identities, and a sample of its entries.
- **D7-ORCHESTRATE-RECONCILE.** A reconciliation compares the actions the delegated agents and their tools recorded with the steps the workflow log holds, at the end of each workflow or on a schedule the organization sets, and raises an alert on each action no logged step accounts for.[^aix-oversight] Not applicable where no agent delegates a task to another agent. *Evidence:* the reconciliation rule with its schedule, and its results for the period with each alert.

**Three criteria grade signals about the controls, where the rest grade signals about the agents.** D7-CONTROL-RELAX, D7-CONTROL-APPROVE and D7-REVIEW-EVASION supply the control-side alerts of a human-factors measurement [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d9-operations|D9]] reads: D9-QUEUE-RATE and D9-QUEUE-STAMP track approval volume and disposition for each period, D9-OVERSIGHT-FLAG raises an alert when an approver's approval rate in a session exceeds the baseline by the stated threshold, and this domain raises one when a control or an approval step changes under it. The Exchange's `OVERSIGHT` control names three signals: silent degradation of human-in-the-loop steps under latency or throughput pressure, inputs timed or shaped toward the paths with the weakest review, and an agent with configuration or policy-management access relaxing a rate limit, disabling a check or widening a scope.[^aix-oversight] D9's reading reaches the first and neither of the others. D7-CONTROL-RELAX widens the third to every principal, because a relaxed control weakens the deployment whoever relaxed it, and an alert that fires because a control was relaxed is a different artifact from one that fires because an agent misbehaved. [[plan-validate-execute|Plan-Validate-Execute]]'s adaptive-confirmation-frequency mitigation, which auto-tiers an action class to skip confirmation after its first run in a session, shows the pattern D7-CONTROL-APPROVE is built to catch: a documented, non-adversarial route by which a low-risk approval comes to cover a later high-impact call, which the criterion alerts on even though a human configured it.

D7-BASELINE and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]]'s D2-BASELINE are separate criteria: D2-BASELINE grades a baseline of each non-human identity's activity, such as the resources it calls and where it calls from, and D7-BASELINE a baseline of each agent's tool calls read from this domain's telemetry. A per-identity baseline of model invocations, such as GuardDuty AI Protection's, can therefore evidence D2-BASELINE and does not meet D7-BASELINE.[^gdai] Microsoft's native detector, Defender XDR AI-agent detection, is in preview, requires an Agent 365 licence, and documents attack-class detections and no per-agent baseline,[^defxdr] so a program meets D7-BASELINE today with pieces it assembles from COTS products, preview services and open-source tools.

The [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] places a test that the agent cannot suppress or alter logs under adversarial conditions at the infrastructure layer of an agentic penetration test, alongside API gateway controls, credential exposure and key management, and cites `MONITOR USE` for it.[^aix-testing] D7-LOG-OUTSIDE reads the store's writer and its access policy and D7-LOG-TAMPER the test, so a log kept outside the agent's reach and never tested meets the first and fails the second. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d9-operations|D9]]'s D9-HIGHRISK-RECORD, at D9 L3, grades a tamper-evident record for high-risk approvals and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d6-data-rag|D6]] L4 append-only logging of memory state changes, each in a different store from the tool-call log.

D7-EVAL counts threat categories and D7-EVAL-TOOLS testing categories: the first reads what the evaluations covered, and the second what generated their cases. D7-EVAL-TOOLS asks for two of the four testing categories, so [[garak|Garak]]'s probe library with [[pyrit|PyRIT]]'s multi-turn orchestration meets it, as does either tool with a [[promptfoo|Promptfoo]] regression suite. No D7 criterion requires continuous adversarial testing, the category [[mindgard-cart|Mindgard CART]] falls in. The quarterly cadence is the one [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|the CMM]]'s Level 4 states for a red-team evaluation program. How that evaluation applies to the defender agents an agentic SOC runs is an open question in [[agentic-soc-state-of-the-field|Agentic SOC: State of the Field]].

D7-ORCHESTRATE and D7-ORCHESTRATE-RECONCILE grade two of the six elements of the Exchange's secure-orchestration strategy: the workflow log held outside the orchestrator's memory, which the Exchange calls tamper-evident, and the reconciliation of agent actions against that log for phantom steps.[^aix-oversight] [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]]'s D3-ORCHESTRATE grades the orchestrator's grants and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d5-egress-network|D5]]'s D5-ORCHESTRATE its network paths. The comparison of an agent's stated reasoning with its tool calls, above, is a different reconciliation, and no D7 criterion grades it.

### L5 detail

- **D7-PLAYBOOK.** The security monitoring function runs a playbook in production for each class of alert the L4 detections raise, and each playbook carries the agent-specific steps: identifying the agent and its accountable human from the telemetry, and stopping or containing the agent through the control that does so, such as D2-KILL's kill switch or D3-ADAPT's step-down. A generic incident playbook with no agent-specific step fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the playbook for each alert class, with its run history for the period.
- **D7-ALERT-RATIO.** Each agent's [[prompt-volume-to-alert-ratio|prompt-volume-to-alert ratio]], the prompts it handled per alert the L4 detections raised on it, is measured and holds within a range the organization documents for at least a quarter, and a departure from the range is recorded with its cause. *Evidence:* the ratio per agent over at least a quarter, with the documented range.
- **D7-ALERT-ACTIONABLE.** The share of the L4 detections' alerts that analysts triaged as analyst-actionable is measured each period and compared with a target the organization documents, and a period below the target is recorded with the tuning that followed. *Evidence:* the actionable-rate report for each period, with the documented target.
- **D7-ALERT-LOOP.** Each alert the L4 detections raise reaches, within an SLA the organization documents, a change to a control, a recorded decision to leave the controls unchanged, or, for a false positive, a tuning record for the detection that raised it. *Evidence:* the SLA and the controls-update log for the period, with each alert matched to its change, decision or tuning record.

[[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d9-operations|D9]]'s D9-LOOP, at D9 L5, grades a control change or a recorded decision within a published SLA for each operational incident, and D7-ALERT-LOOP grades the same loop for every alert the L4 detections raise, closing it into the step-down [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]] and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]] apply. One controls-update log evidences both where it records alerts as well as incidents. D7-ALERT-ACTIONABLE compares the rate with the organization's own target, because the one published benchmark behind the ratio states no actionable rate, as §Open questions records.

### L5+ detail

- **D7-CASCADE.** A library of cascade-detection rules for multi-agent risk (ASI07, ASI08 and ASI10) runs in production, each rule with a threshold the organization tuned against recorded cascades or test runs. Not applicable where the deployment runs a single agent. *Evidence:* the cascade rule registry, with each rule's threshold and its tuning record.
- **D7-JOINT.** Baselines of the joint behavior of the agents that interact, such as the joint distribution of their calls to one another and to shared tools, are held, and a detection raises an alert on a departure from them. Not applicable where the deployment runs a single agent. *Evidence:* the joint-baseline statistics, with the detection rule.
- **D7-ACTIVATION.** A monitor reads each production model's forward-pass activations during the agents' sessions and raises an alert on the activation patterns the organization defines. *Evidence:* the activation monitor's configuration, with an alert it raised or a test of it.

Rule 2 holds these three criteria at L5+ on the reading the control landscape gives. No cascade-detection library is generally available: Google's Agent Anomaly Detection ships default ASI08 and ASI10 detectors in allowlisted preview, and rule 2 holds the capability here until one is generally available.[^gaad] Cross-agent joint baselines are research-stage, and forward-pass activation monitoring has no shipping product.

## Right-sizing by deployment shape

| Deployment shape | Realistic D7 target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Internal RAG / support chatbot (no/few tools) | L3 | Every L3 criterion that applies: D7-LOG-SCHEMA is not applicable to a bot with no tool, and D7-FORWARD-SCAN applies to a scanned corpus. Near-zero tool surface needs no behavioral-drift or multi-tool red team. The persona's bot sits here |
| In-suite productivity assistant (Gemini for Workspace, Microsoft 365 Copilot) | L3 | Microsoft's audit record, searched and exported, meets D7-ATTRIBUTE, and meets D7-LOG where it names every tool the assistant calls. Neither vendor's record carries a call's arguments, so D7-LOG-SCHEMA fails, and D7-SPANS rests on spans only the vendor can emit |
| Desktop-agent productivity assistant ([[claude-cowork\|Claude Cowork]] class) | L3 | An administrator-configured OpenTelemetry export puts session, user and tool events in the organization's own backend, each tool event naming the tool, the MCP scope, the decision and its source; two vendor pages disagree on whether prompt content is captured by default |
| Data-science / coding copilot | L3 → L4 | Long-running sessions and tool writes justify per-agent drift baselines and an eval cadence |
| MCP / skill provider serving others | L4 | Third-party blast radius; MCP-protocol-aware anomaly detection and AI-SPM become first-order |
| High-autonomy multi-agent mesh | L4 → selective L5+ | Cascade / rogue-agent detection and joint-distribution baselines become load-bearing only here |

An agent the organization builds on a vendor's platform, such as a Copilot Studio agent, takes the row its own function fits, and the vendor-held telemetry rule reads the logging its maker enabled as the customer's own configuration.

**The in-suite assistant's telemetry is a vendor-held audit record, and the vendor-held telemetry rule decides each criterion on it.** Microsoft's Purview record, which the organization searches and exports through audit search, names the invoking user, the agent by identity, name and version, the conversation thread, the resources the assistant reached, each with its sensitivity label, the action taken on it and whether that action succeeded, and the plugin it used, so D7-ATTRIBUTE is met on inspected evidence.[^mscopilotaudit] The same record meets D7-LOG only where it names every tool the assistant calls. A tool Microsoft's published schema does not name, such as the code interpreter in which the assistant runs Python, is read from a test the customer runs under the vendor-held telemetry rule, and D7-LOG is not met until that test finds the tool named in the record.[^mscopilotaudit] Google's Gemini for Workspace log event names the actor, the action, the application, the feature source and the agent, and the appendix read lists no resource or tool parameter.[^gworkspacelog] Microsoft's schema holds no field for a tool call's arguments, so D7-LOG-SCHEMA is **not met** on inspected evidence. Neither record is an OpenTelemetry `gen_ai.*` span. Where the vendor documents no span output for its assistant, D7-SPANS is **unanswerable** under the vendor-held telemetry rule, and D7-SPANS-HELD is **not met**, because no span reaches a backend the organization holds. The row's L3 target rests on spans only the vendor can emit. Microsoft emits `gen_ai.*` spans over agent invocation, tool execution and output for agents built on Copilot Studio, into the Agent 365 observability backend. The page read lists no inference span and no customer-configured endpoint, so D7-SPANS is not met for those agents, and D7-SPANS-HELD turns on whether the organization can search and export that backend.[^msagent365obs]

The [[lethal-trifecta|lethal-trifecta]] test is D7's strongest right-sizing lever. A program with strong D3/D4/D5 architectural containment may legitimately run no behavioral-observability layer and still be sound (the Stripe containment pattern). A contained design scoring low on D7 records the choice as an intentional trade-off.

**Routing inference through a gateway removes the first-party analytics dashboards for the sessions it carries.** An organization that introduces an LLM gateway or a cloud-provider endpoint (Bedrock, Vertex, Foundry) for governance reasons moves its coding sessions off the harness vendor's own API, and that vendor's analytics API reports only the sessions still running against it. OpenTelemetry export is the replacement, and it is not automatic: for the sessions a gateway carries, D7-LOG and D7-LOG-SCHEMA are met only by the exported tool events, each joined to its session and naming the developer. [[securing-agentic-coding|Securing Agentic Coding]] §Observability plane and [[generative-coding-deployment-shape-2026|Generative Coding Deployment Shapes]] carry the deployment detail.

## Cost model

L2 and L3 price an E5 incumbent that already runs Sentinel, Azure Monitor and Application Insights, so no new licence stands between it and the L3 telemetry. The spend is ingestion, because the E5 data grant covers up to 5 MB per user per day from four named Microsoft 365 sources and agent trace data is not among them.[^e5grant] The one native detector on that stack, Defender XDR AI-agent detection, requires an Agent 365 licence, and Microsoft documents no per-agent baseline for it.[^defxdr] Cloud Trace and the OpenTelemetry the Agent Development Kit emits can carry D7-SPANS and D7-SPANS-HELD for a Google Cloud buyer, and the other L3 criteria turn on the deployment. Google's native detector, Agent Anomaly Detection, is in allowlisted preview, so a Google buyer that runs no preview service meets D7-BASELINE and D7-DRIFT off-platform.[^gaad] Ingestion run-rate scales with agent count on every stack; the off-platform COTS detector a Google buyer adds carries no figure in this model.

| Level | Licensing | Operational labor | Run-rate (dominant) |
|---|---|---|---|
| L2 | ~0 for a Sentinel and Defender incumbent | ~0.25 FTE: turn on tool-call audit | low |
| L3 | ~0 for an E5 incumbent (App Insights, Azure Monitor and Sentinel already run; OTel SDK is OSS) | ~0.5 FTE: span instrumentation, attribution wiring, schema conformance | the inflection — every agent's `gen_ai.*` traces are billed per GB, outside the E5 data grant[^e5grant] |
| L4 | mostly ~0 native, but Defender AI-agent detection needs an Agent 365 license; Miggo/Vectra are off-stack COTS otherwise | baseline tuning, drift false-positive triage, multi-tool eval ops | behavioral-analysis compute + full agent-log retention, scaling with agent count |
| L5 | ~0 beyond the SIEM's own automation; no L5 criterion names a product | continuous baseline-ratio maintenance, actionable-rate measurement, SLA runbook | peak: every-surface telemetry × every agent into the SIEM |

The dominant cost signal: licensing is near-zero through L3 for an E5 incumbent, and the spend is ingestion run-rate that scales with agent count. **Log tiering** is the mitigation: route high-volume, low-fidelity agent trace spans to the cheaper data-lake tier, and keep in the analytics tier the tables that detection rules query.[^sentpricing] The one licensing step sits at L4, where Defender XDR AI-agent detection requires an Agent 365 licence that classic E5 does not include.[^defxdr]

## Customer critiques folded in

- *"Switch on logging we already pay for."* Confirmed: D7-L3 is near-zero licensing for [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026|the persona]]; the work is instrumentation labor.
- *"Log-ingestion run-rate is the dominant cost."* Confirmed: ingestion grows with every agent, and log tiering in the cost model above is the mitigation.
- *"L5 names just-GA'd products."* Rule 2 settles the credit: a preview product counts from the date it entered the organization's own production, so Defender AI-agent detection and the Sentinel graph MCP tools, both in preview, count for a buyer that runs them and for no buyer that waits for general availability.
- *"No behavioral baselines yet; PyRIT covers one red-team leg."* Fair: the persona's bot targets L3, and D7-EVAL-TOOLS at L4 needs a second tool from another testing category, so [[pyrit|PyRIT]] alone does not meet it.

## Open questions

- The OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions remain in Development status, and a version bump can break detection rules. Since semantic conventions v1.42.0 the `gen_ai.*` definitions in the main repository are deprecated and maintained in a dedicated repository that has published no release, so a version pin names either a deprecated release or an unreleased commit, and neither repository states a stable-release date.[^otel]
- Defender XDR AI-agent detection is in public preview, and since 2026-07-01 Microsoft requires an Agent 365 licence for it, so a classic-E5 tenant reaches it only by adding that licence.[^defxdr] General availability would still leave D7-BASELINE unmet on the evidence Microsoft publishes, which lists attack-class detections and no per-agent baseline or drift signal.
- AWS and GCP emit OTel telemetry natively, and each has a native detector with a limit. GuardDuty AI Protection baselines model invocations for each IAM identity and names no tool-call baseline, so it can evidence D2-BASELINE and does not meet D7-BASELINE,[^gdai] and Agent Anomaly Detection is in allowlisted preview.[^gaad] When either platform ships a generally available baseline of each agent's tool calls is open, and until then a single-stack buyer on either platform meets D7-BASELINE off-platform.
- The one published benchmark behind the L5 ratio, the Salesforce Agentforce SOC figure in [[prompt-volume-to-alert-ratio|Prompt-Volume-to-Alert Ratio]], measures prompts per actionable alert on a single platform, and the talk that reports it states no analyst-actionable rate. Whether the ratio generalizes is unverified, and the talk supplies no figure for the actionable rate, so D7-ALERT-ACTIONABLE compares the rate with a target the organization documents.
- Time-to-detect against a campaign running at agent speed has no target figure. The one published data point is the [[openai-hugging-face-agent-incident|OpenAI–Hugging Face agent incident]], where the operator's own workload alert on internal privilege escalation fired on 2026-07-19, roughly eleven days after the second intrusion cluster began and three days after Hugging Face published its own breach; the two were linked on 2026-07-20 through a credential-revocation exchange rather than through a detection.[^bhoaihf] A domain whose whole purpose is detection has no benchmark for how late is too late.
- Reasoning-trace monitoring has one platform-native product, Google's Agent Anomaly Detection, in allowlisted preview,[^gaad] and a capture format in the reasoning message part the OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions have carried since v1.38.0,[^otel] and it has no cost model. The signal is demonstrably rich, and reading it across a fleet is an unpriced problem that sits on top of the telemetry layer whose ingestion already dominates this domain's cost.
- D7-DRIFT and D7-DRIFT-ROUTE grade a detector's scoring, its threshold and its routing. Neither the [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]]'s agent-escape entry nor its `OVERSIGHT` control states a threshold for how much relaxation across how many turns constitutes a finding,[^aix-escape][^aix-oversight] so the threshold is the organization's, accuracy stays ungraded, and the criteria inherit the missing time-to-detect benchmark above. `OVERSIGHT` supplies the disposition the alert routes to, human review or automatic session suspension.[^aix-oversight] Drift across the sessions of one task has no criterion: both sources score drift within a session, and `MONITOR USE` names cross-session correlation among the purposes of its tool-chain monitoring.[^aix-monitoruse]
- D7-FORWARD-ESCAPE is conditional on a sandbox, and no dependency rule connects D4 to D7. A deployment whose agents execute no code scores it not applicable, and one that runs a sandbox and forwards nothing fails it. Whether D4's sandbox grade warrants a D4→D7 candidate cap belongs to [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-dependency-rules|the dependency rules]].
- Series-level detection has no settled domain between D4 and here. The [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-crosswalk|crosswalk]]'s D4 cell anchors `UNWANTED INPUT SERIES HANDLING`, a clustering and frequency-analysis detector over a time window not limited to consecutive requests, and D7-DRIFT grades over the same trajectory, so a D4 level for it would collide with the boundary here. The anchor stands and the grading domain is unresolved ([[cmm-known-limitations|CMM Known Limitations]] item 23).
- **The cost model prices retained telemetry, and D7-RETAIN bounds it from below only.** Every level above enlarges what is collected — `gen_ai.*` spans and memory-write records at L3, behavioral baselines and evaluation scenarios at L4, every surface of every agent at L5 — and the cost model treats the resulting volume as an ingestion bill. The [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] treats the same volume as exposure: `DATA MINIMIZE` names runtime logging among the activities the control applies to, and `SHORT RETAIN` requires data to be removed or anonymized once it is no longer needed.[^aix-dataminimize][^aix-shortretain] Agent telemetry carries prompts, tool results, and retrieved content, so it holds the classes both controls are written for. D7-RETAIN requires a stated period and stores that keep their records that long, and sets no upper bound, because `DATA MINIMIZE` and `SHORT RETAIN` name no period or artifact for logging and `MONITOR USE` keeps logs "for a period sufficient to support analysis". For the fields, `MONITOR USE` publishes a method: tool arguments logged "(or hashed/summarized parameters)", audit-trail parameters "(redacted as required)", and a "content hash or summary" for each memory write.[^aix-monitoruse] D7-LOG-SCHEMA and D7-LOG-MEMORY admit the reduced forms, and no criterion requires them. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d6-data-rag|D6]] L3 applies retention minimization to the augmentation store and its embeddings, which is a different store, so minimization of the telemetry backend sits outside both domains.
- Monitor integrity is graded one property deep and no further. The [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] runtime control `MONITOR USE` lists the agent action audit trail among the fields logs may include and places it "in tamper-evident storage the agent cannot modify",[^aix-monitoruse] and the Exchange's testing document supplies the evidence artifact for that requirement — an adversarial test of whether the agent can suppress or alter its own records — which D7-LOG-TAMPER grades.[^aix-testing] Two properties stay ungraded: separation between the detection pipeline and the fleet it watches, and any threshold on either.[^aix-monitoruse] A criterion asserting that separation would still be satisfied by assertion, so none is written. `DATA QUALITY CONTROL` covers the other detector class: detection mechanisms and the data they rely on "benefit from protection against manipulation", and segregation of development environments and integrity protections "can help prevent attackers from tampering with detection logic".[^aix-dataqualitycontrol] [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d6-data-rag|D6]] L4 grades a criterion off that guidance — the poisoning detector's logic, thresholds, and baseline distributions held under access control and integrity checking. The runtime monitor this domain runs over the agent fleet still has no such backing. The narrowing is specific to log integrity and leaves the Class 3 monitor-isolation evidence item [[agentic-ai-threat-classes-2026|the threat classes]] proposes for D7 L4+ ungraded.
- **Tamper evidence is ungraded.** D7-LOG-OUTSIDE keeps the tool-call record outside the agent's write reach and D7-LOG-TAMPER tests that, and no criterion requires a record to prove itself unmodified. The Exchange supplies the requirement and an implementation of its tamper-evident half — a signed receipt binding a hash of input, output and processing context to a per-event signature, paired with an independent time anchor, which lets an investigator verify after an incident that a record is unmodified — so a criterion written for it would grade an artifact rather than an assertion,[^aix-monitoruse] and whether it belongs at L4 or L5 is open.

## D2→D7 dependency cap

D7's effective score is capped at D2's raw score for the same deployment, because per-agent behavioral baselining binds to a verifiable per-agent identity, and the assessment grades D7 on the scope it graded D2 on. Below D2 L3 an agent's identity is its own and still unverified, so a baseline keyed to it binds to a name no service checks. For the persona (D2 at L2), D7's effective score is capped at L2 regardless of observability spend. The scoring guidance follows: the cheapest high-leverage move is D2-L3, which lifts the D7 ceiling and costs near-zero licensing for a Microsoft incumbent because [[microsoft-entra-agent-id|Entra Agent ID]] rides the directory. An Entra Agent ID rollout meets the first of D2-L3's six criteria, so the ceiling rises only when all six are met. Buying a monitoring product against an unbindable identity does not lift it. Report D7 as raw + effective. See [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-dependency-rules|the dependency rules]].

[[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]] states the same dependency from its own side, bounded: a verifiable per-agent identity is necessary for per-agent detection and does not by itself establish that a correctly authenticated principal is acting on its own intent.
