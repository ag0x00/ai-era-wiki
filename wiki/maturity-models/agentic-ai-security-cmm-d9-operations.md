# Agentic AI Security CMM — D9 Operations & Human Factors (Deep Dive)

Companion deep-dive to [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|the CMM]]'s D9 domain, written under the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026|recalibration method]]. Of the nine domains, D9 carries the most process and labor and the fewest products. Its operating-model core has no product answer on any platform: human-in-the-loop (HITL) fatigue and oversight-quality measurement, incident-playbook authoring, decommission drills, bus-factor continuity. Where products exist, they are platform-native primitives repurposed from adjacent domains (D2 identity lifecycle, D7 observability) rather than built as D9-specific tools.

**The cost model rests on one customer's stress test.** The levels and the cost model synthesize the recalibration method against the [[agentic-cmm-regulated-fi-stress-test|regulated-FI stress test]] plus vendor documentation, and the L3 and L4 criteria also cite the [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] entries footnoted at each. The status column of the control landscape is a May 2026 snapshot; rows re-read since carry their own retrieval dates in the notes.

## Threat coverage

D9 is cross-cutting: each of the [[agentic-ai-threat-classes-2026|five threat classes]] maps to it, with decommission and rollback answering model-version degradation (Class 4), sustained threat hunting answering long-running campaigns (Class 2) and the vendor-cutoff playbook answering jurisdictional adversaries (Class 5). Incident response and the approval measures answer human-agent trust exploitation in the [[owasp-agentic-ai-top-10|OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications]], and the bus-factor condition of the gate into L5 holds every other domain's L5 claim. The full mapping is in the [[threat-taxonomy-reconciliation|Threat Taxonomy Reconciliation]] matrix.

The load-bearing center of D9 has no product on any stack: HITL-fatigue measurement, bus-factor continuity, incident-playbook authoring and exercises, decommission cadence. That is a market-wide gap rather than a Microsoft-specific one, so no procurement decision closes it.

What [[microsoft-zt4ai|Microsoft ZT4AI]] does supply for D9 is the lifecycle-accountability layer. [[standards-review-microsoft-zt4ai-2026-Q2|The 2026-Q2 ZT4AI review]] crosswalks two sets of GA controls to D9. Entra ID Governance sponsors, with automatic transfer to a departing sponsor's manager, and Lifecycle Workflows map to lifecycle accountability at L3; access packages with expiry, and disable or decommission from My Account, map to L4. The same review finds no Microsoft control that measures approval fatigue, oversight load or rubber-stamp rate. The [[microsoft-rai|RAI Standard]]'s A5 (Human oversight and control) goal sets the human-oversight outcome at the goal level, and [[microsoft-agent-365|Microsoft Agent 365]]'s lifecycle management (create / review / decommission via the registry plus the Entra ID Governance sponsor model) supplies the management-plane mechanism behind it — the goal-and-mechanism pairing is set out in [[standards-review-microsoft-rai-agent-365-2026-Q2|the 2026-Q2 RAI / Agent 365 review]]. Neither instrument supplies the human-factors instrumentation (HITL-fatigue measurement, continuity testing) at the center of this domain.

Regulatory-notification coordination belongs to the compliance function. The Exchange routes it to `CHECK COMPLIANCE` and states the placement outright: a serious incident may trigger parallel obligations such as [[eu-ai-act|EU AI Act]] Art. 73 reporting and NIS2 notification timelines for in-scope entities, and the response is coordinated there rather than inside the monitoring controls ([[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]], [`/go/secprogram/`](https://owaspai.org/go/secprogram/)). D9's incident-response content and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]]'s monitoring content both sit on that line. D9-IR-NOTIFY grades the instrument, the owner and the clock in the incident playbook, and the telemetry pipeline carries none of the obligation.

Cyera's Incident Materiality Assessment is a vendor example of the data-impact analysis an incident playbook can call on, which no D9 criterion grades: a scoped inventory of exposed records by class, a timeline of who accessed what and when, and a report framed for legal, board, and regulatory readers ([[cyera-agent-guardian-release|Cyera Agent Guardian Release]]). Cyera states the service pairs data scanning with expert-led analysis, so the assessment is delivered as expert work and this domain's product landscape is unchanged. The release leaves the four load-bearing gaps at the center of this domain untouched: HITL-fatigue measurement, bus-factor continuity, incident-playbook exercises and decommission cadence.

## Capability-decoupled levels

Stated as capabilities per [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026|rule 1]]; a control counts when it operates in production per rule 2. A control implemented through a product in the organization's approved-vendor pipeline with a documented production date satisfies its criterion on that basis alone, and the production date is the date the control entered the organization's production. A planned date satisfies none, and neither does a product the organization does not yet run in production, whatever the vendor has announced.

- **L1 — Initial.** No runbook states what happens when a guardrail fails, when an agent's owner leaves or while approvals wait, nothing checks what the system prompts carry, and no incident playbook covers the agents.
- **L2 — Developing.** Runbooks state the operator's step when each guardrail fails, what happens to an agent and to the credentials a person held when the agent's owner leaves, and how each approval path is recorded and reviewed. No system prompt carries a credential, a database name, the users' roles or the permission structure.
- **L3 — Defined.** Each guardrail's latency and cost are tracked and its fail mode tested, orphaned agents and credentials are resolved within a set time, each approval path's approval rate and queue age are tracked, and a canary and leak probes guard each system prompt. High-risk approvals are categorized in advance and recorded tamper-evidently, the highest-risk actions wait a minimum delay and critical actions take two approvers, users are told an AI model is involved, and a record sets the disclosure against the five transparency properties. The organization names an AI security role with a deputy for each duty, reports through a disclosure community, and keeps an exercised AI incident playbook with each deployment's containment steps, its notification paths and a compromised-model scenario. A deployment that publishes components outside the organization publishes their deprecation policy.
- **L4 — Managed.** Each approval path's rubber-stamp rate, 95th-percentile queue age and involvement measure are tracked, and the path is tested adversarially with four techniques. Each approver's approvals are limited per session, and an alert fires when an approver's approval rate exceeds the baseline. Drift findings are classified benign or adversarial, a decommission drill runs each quarter, production pins each model to a fixed version, and the organization takes part in a coordinated-disclosure exercise.
- **L5 — Optimizing.** Each operational incident reaches a control change or a recorded decision within a published SLA, the approval measures stay within published thresholds, attested counts show no orphaned credential, no prompt leak and no retired model version for two quarters, and the deputies take a continuity test each quarter.
- **L5+ — Leading Edge.** The organization publishes metrics on the operation of its AI systems, contributes drift-detection patterns or bypass classes to a standards body or a public framework, and coordinates the disclosure of an AI vulnerability or incident among organizations.

**D9-NOTICE-PROPERTIES grades which of the five transparency properties the disclosure covers, and the depth of each answer stays ungraded.** The Exchange pairs `AI TRANSPARENCY` with `DISCRETE` and directs that the two be weighed against each other, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d1-governance|D1]]'s D1-DETAIL-REVIEW records, for each item published about a deployment, the technical detail it withholds and discloses ([`/go/discrete/`](https://owaspai.org/go/discrete/)).[^aix-discrete] The bound D9-NOTICE-PROPERTIES sets on that review reaches coverage only. A property answered in one line is covered, because the Exchange resolves the trade-off with a direction, to minimize what helps an attacker while being transparent, and marks no line where technical detail crosses from safe disclosure into attacker assistance.[^aix-discrete] [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-crosswalk|The standards-crosswalk matrix]] holds the domain split, and [[owasp-ai-exchange|the Exchange page]] holds the trade-off in full.

The disclosure addresses users rather than auditors. The Exchange states one floor, that users are told an AI model is involved, which it notes the EU AI Act requires for chatbots, and D9-NOTICE grades that floor for each of the deployment's users.[^aix-aitransparency] Three of the five properties can be answered from records other criteria already produce. A deployment that calls a third-party model answers the training-approach and data-source properties from the model cards [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d8-supply-chain|D8]]'s D8-MODEL-CARD holds, and the residual-risk property from D1-ALLOCATE-RESIDUE, which records the disposition of each threat's residue as accepted, mitigated by the organization's own control, or avoided.

**The Exchange lists four downsides of human oversight, and three have a graded counterpart.** Cost and slowness surface in queue age, approval fatigue in the rubber-stamp rate, and lack of involvement in D9-OVERSIGHT-INVOLVE. Lack of expertise has none: the Exchange asks that the human be qualified, instructed and motivated, and no D9 criterion grades it.[^aix-oversight] The Exchange classes lack of involvement as a form of lack of expertise: a reviewer who does not actively perform the task loses the understanding of whether it is correct and what its impact can be, so a badly informed "go ahead" is easy to give.[^aix-oversight] D9-OVERSIGHT-TEST attacks the approval path itself, because an approval path nobody has attacked leaves its human failure mode unmeasured, and the other approval measures observe the approver only through the approvals given.

> [!gap] No validated involvement indicator
> The out-of-the-loop phenomenon, which the [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] carries over from operators of automated systems such as self-driving cars, has no measurement instrument in any source this wiki holds.[^aix-oversight] Rubber-stamp rate, approval rate and queue age measure volume and disposition, and a disengaged approver produces unremarkable values on all three, so no threshold on them detects the failure. D9-OVERSIGHT-INVOLVE therefore grades that an involvement measure is named, its method stated and its value taken, and grades neither its accuracy nor a threshold against it, because no source supplies either. Candidate constructions are untested as indicators: rotating approvers through the task they later approve, spot-checks that require reconstructing an action rather than assenting to it, and sampled reversal of approved actions.

Nothing in D9 L1 to L5 requires tooling that reached general availability in the last year. The dependencies are CoSAI's AI Incident Response Framework V1.0, dated 27 October 2025, stable OpenTelemetry and canary patterns, and SCIM or NHI deprovisioning.[^cosai] The Entra Agent ID lifecycle path, generally available since 2026-05-01 alongside Agent 365, is one way to meet D9-REAP, and the stress test's cadence argument applies to it, so D9 is cadence-safe for a buyer who takes another path.[^entra-lcw]

## Assessor detail per level

L1 describes where a deployment starts and carries no criterion, so a deployment that does not meet every L2 criterion scores L1. It scores 0 under the measurement protocol's rubric only where no evidence of the L1 baseline exists, meaning the assessment holds no record and no interview answer about the domain for that deployment. The levels from L2 to L5+ each carry a criteria list below, each list stating what its own level adds.

Grading is cumulative: Level N requires every Level N–1 control plus the new criteria at Level N ([[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|the CMM]]), so a level is met only where every level below it is met.

Each criterion takes one of four verdicts. **Met** and **not met** are read from the evidence the criterion names, whoever operates the control. The assessment records the evidence's assurance class beside the verdict and names the artifact behind it, its issuer and its date, per [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|the measurement protocol]]:

- **Tested**: the customer or the assessor exercised the control and recorded what it did.
- **Inspected**: vendor tooling the customer can reach, or a record the organization keeps, such as an inventory or a plan, shows the control's state in the deployment.
- **Attested**: the vendor states the control in a document the customer holds, and the document's own scope names the control.

**Not applicable** is recorded where the deployment holds no instance of what the criterion governs, and the reduced scope is recorded as an intentional trade-off in the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-dependency-rules|effective-score]] strategic-rationale field. **Unanswerable** is recorded where the instance exists, the customer can run no test and keeps no record of the control's state, and the vendor supplies neither inspectable output nor an attestation that names the control. An unanswerable criterion never counts as met: its level stays open, and every level above it stays open under cumulative grading. The assessment names what would close it. A criterion that can be not applicable states that condition alongside the criterion. Each level's list below holds criteria only, and a paragraph after a list states no criterion: it carries maturity, market or provenance commentary, or the boundary between this domain and another.

**D9 grades one deployment at a time, and the organization once.** A deployment is the unit the assessment scope names: one agent application, or one agent platform a vendor provides together with the agents the organization runs on it. Evidence of a control counts only for the deployments the control covers. No active dependency rule caps D9 or reads its score, and an assessment grades D9 on the scope it graded [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]] on, so each deployment's matrix carries its D9 result beside its other domains. Eight terms fix what the criteria range over:

- **An agent** is one deployed configuration: its code, instructions, tools and grants as the deployment runs them. Replicas of one configuration are one agent, and two configurations are two agents even where they share code. The deployment's inventory is the one D2-INVENTORY grades, whether or not it meets that criterion. A vendor-built agent the organization enables for its users, such as one of an in-suite assistant's own agents, is an agent of the deployment that provides it.
- **The agents in scope** are those the deployment runs in production, per rule 2. An agent outside production enters scope once it holds a production credential or reaches production data. Evidence from a pilot outside scope counts for no criterion, and the gap report can cite it as the planned route to a level. A deployment with no agent in scope, such as a pilot that holds no production credential and reaches no production data, takes no D9 result, and the assessment records it as outside scope.
- **A service a tool calls**, such as a document-extraction service that runs a model of its own, is part of that tool, as [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]] defines an agent's model calls. Its runbooks, guardrails and prompts count toward no D9 criterion of the calling deployment, and it takes a D9 result of its own only where it runs agents.
- **An agent's owner** is the human D2-OWNER names for the agent, whether or not the deployment meets that criterion, or, where the deployment's records name none, the person the AI register names as accountable for the deployment under [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d1-governance|D1]]'s D1-REGISTER.
- **The deployment's credentials** are the stored credentials its agents authenticate with and those a broker or a gateway uses for them. A stored credential carries D2's sense: a secret that stays valid until someone rotates it, such as a password, an API key, a client secret or a private key. A credential a person created counts as one that person can read until it is replaced.
- **The approval queue** is every human approval the deployment's agents wait on. A human approval carries [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]]'s sense, a person's decision, given for one action before it runs, that the action can run, so the queue holds the approvals of the human an agent acts for, such as a developer's answer to a permission prompt or a user's send of a draft an agent prepared. A draft that waits for the user's send waits on an approval whether or not the agent can send it. An **approval path** is one route by which approvals reach an action, such as one workflow, one confirmation button or one permission prompt.
- **The deployment's system prompts** are the standing instructions the organization writes for each agent, which the agent loads on every run before any request, such as a system prompt, a maker's instructions or an instruction file the organization's configuration loads for every session. A prompt a vendor writes for its own assistant or harness is vendor-held, and the vendor-held operations rule below reads it.
- **The deployment's users** are the people who interact with its agents, and the people outside the organization who receive content an agent sends them with no person reviewing it first.

A criterion that names every agent, guardrail, approval, credential or incident is met only where it holds for each one. No percentage threshold applies, because the deployment's records enumerate its agents, its guardrails, its approval paths and its credentials, and one agent outside the rule is the failure D9 grades. An agent whose configuration the organization cannot produce, such as a workstation that has stopped reporting to device management, does not meet a criterion that ranges over it. The deployment's verdict is the weakest among its agents: not met where any agent fails the criterion, then unanswerable where any agent's verdict is unanswerable, and not applicable only where the criterion is not applicable to every agent. The assessment names the scope it graded, and where a narrower scope would change an agent's result, such as one repository of a shared CI workflow, it reports that agent beside the deployment's result.

**Unit tags.** Each criterion carries a unit tag after its name, organization or deployment. An organization criterion grades an artifact or a practice the organization keeps for all its AI systems at once: the assessment grades it once and reports its verdict once, in a table of the organization criteria beside the deployment matrices, and no deployment's row repeats it. A deployment criterion is graded for each deployment on the evidence that covers it, and its verdict is reported in that deployment's row. A deployment's cumulative grading reads each organization criterion's verdict at the level the criterion carries, so a deployment reaches a level only where every organization criterion at that level and below is met. An organization criterion not met or unanswerable at L3 therefore holds every deployment at L2, whatever the deployment's own criteria show, and each deployment's row names that criterion as the reason.

**Grading words.** The criteria use these terms in one sense each:

- **Runbook**: a written procedure the deployment's operators follow, in one document or several, such as an on-call runbook, a retirement procedure or a business procedure for an approval queue. A runbook states each step, who takes it and what starts it, and a document that describes a behaviour and assigns no step is no runbook.
- **Guardrail** carries [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]]'s sense: each safety filter, injection classifier, alignment monitor, judge, code scan and groundedness check the deployment runs on its agents' model calls. A guardrail **fails closed** where a call it could not screen, because it errored, timed out or was cut off by its budget, is blocked or held, and it **fails open** where that call proceeds unscreened.
- **High-impact call** carries D4's sense: a call that can carry out a destructive action, as D3 defines one, the organization's own additional classes included.
- **Orphaned agent** and **orphaned credential**: an agent is orphaned when its owner has left the organization, or moved to a role that no longer owns it, and no successor is recorded. A credential is orphaned when an orphaned agent holds it, when no agent in scope uses it, or when a person who has left can still use it.
- **Owner departure** and **retired**: an owner departs by leaving the organization or by moving to a role that no longer owns the agent, so a runbook whose steps start only when an owner leaves states nothing for an owner who moves. An agent is retired when it can no longer run and its credentials and connections are revoked, as D9-DRILL's retirement runbook does. An agent unpublished or disabled with its connections in place is stopped and is not retired.
- **Tracked**: a measure is tracked where the organization computes it for each period from the deployment's own records and keeps each period's value, so a change between periods can be read. Data held with no measure computed from it is not tracked, and neither is a measure over a population that mixes the deployment's approvals with others and does not separate them.
- **Period** and **quarter**: a period is the reporting interval the organization states for a measure, no longer than a quarter, and a quarter is a calendar quarter. A criterion that reads the last two quarters reads the two most recent complete quarters before the assessment starts.
- **Approval rate**, **queue age** and **95th percentile**: the approval rate is the share of an approval path's requests in a period that were approved. Queue age is the time from an approval request to its decision, or to its expiry where nobody answered it, and the 95th percentile of queue age is the wait that 95 of every 100 of the period's requests stayed within.
- **Rubber-stamp rate**: the share of an approval path's approvals in a period given with no sign of review, by a method the organization states for the path, such as a draft sent unedited, an approval whose reason is a stock phrase, or an approval given within a stated number of seconds of its request.
- **Involvement measure**: a measure of whether approvers stay engaged with the task they approve, such as a sampled reconstruction of approved actions or a rotation of approvers through the task, in the sense of the Exchange's out-of-the-loop phenomenon.[^aix-oversight] A measure of approval volume or disposition, such as an approval rate, a rubber-stamp rate or an override rate, is no involvement measure.
- **High-risk approval**: a human approval of an action in a category D9-HIGHRISK's definitions name. The **highest-risk actions** are those in the category the definitions rank highest, and the **critical actions** are those the definitions mark critical or, where they mark none, the highest-risk actions. Where the definitions rank no category, each category they name is the highest.
- **Tamper-evident**: kept where no approver, requester, agent or administrator of the approving system can change or delete a record unnoticed, such as a store that only appends, or records signed or hashed as they are written.
- **Exercise** and **drill**: an exercise is a scheduled rehearsal of a playbook or a runbook with the people it names, a tabletop included, recorded with its scenario, its participants and an after-action report. A drill is an exercise that carries out a runbook's steps against an agent on the production platform, such as a test agent registered for the drill, and a tabletop is no drill.
- **Operational incident**: an event on an agent in scope that the organization's incident process or a runbook records, in which a guardrail failed or was bypassed, an agent was stopped or decommissioned in response to the event, or an approval was given in error, given late or left unanswered past its time.
- **Incident class that can occur**: a class can occur on an agent that holds what the class attacks, whether or not the playbook lists the class, such as content the agent did not write for prompt injection, agent memory for memory injection, and a corpus the agent retrieves from for poisoning. **Agent memory** carries [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]]'s sense: content an agent writes for its own later use or another agent's and a later task, conversation or agent reads back as context, such as a vendor's memory feature, a table an agent's flow writes and reads back, or an instruction file an agent edits that later runs load. A playbook covers each agent its scope statement names, and D9-IR-CONTAIN reads each deployment's steps.
- **Drift finding**: an alert or a flag that an agent's behaviour, or its model's performance, departed from a recorded baseline, such as the alert of a [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]] detection or a monthly performance flag under the organization's model-risk monitoring.
- **Disclosure community**: a group of organizations that exchange reports of incidents and vulnerabilities under handling rules its members accept, such as an information sharing and analysis centre, a national exchange, or an AI security project that runs a reporting channel.
- **Published**: placed where each person the document applies to can read it and, for a document that applies outside the organization, where anyone can read it.
- **Attest**, for a count: state a quarter's count on the record, over the signature of the person the AI register names as accountable for the deployment, with the record the count is taken from. A count no one signed is not attested.
- **Tested**: a test counts where it ran in production, or in another environment on the guardrails, approval paths and configuration that production runs, and its record names the environment and the revisions. A demonstration the organization runs and the assessor observes is a test.

**Names and precedence.** Each criterion carries a stable name, D9 and a mnemonic for what the criterion requires, and the name leads the criterion in bold. The level and the unit tag are attributes recorded beside the name, so a criterion that changes level keeps its name. Members of a family share its first mnemonic, as D9-PROMPT-CANARY and D9-PROMPT-PROBE do, and each member is a separate criterion because a deployment, or the organization, can hold one without the other and an assessor collects different evidence for each. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|The CMM]]'s D9 levels restate each criterion under the same name, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|the measurement protocol]]'s D9 questions and artifacts cite each one by that name, in plain text. A level's statement above summarizes its list, and where the two read differently the list governs.

**Vendor-held operations.** A vendor holds part of a deployment's operations where the vendor's service runs a control this domain grades and the customer holds no configuration of it, as with an in-suite assistant's classifiers, its system prompt, the model its vendor chooses, the record of what its users do with a draft, or a platform's own switch that stops an agent. The criteria stand for those parts and are graded on the evidence the vendor produces, because the guardrails, the prompts, the models and the approvals exist whoever runs them: vendor operation is no ground for a not-applicable verdict. A vendor document whose own scope names the product and the control evidences it as attested, and a record of the control the customer can search, such as a log of each confirmation a user gives, evidences it as inspected. Where the vendor documents neither, the criterion is read from a test the customer runs, such as leak probes sent to the assistant or a stop control run against a test user, and it is unanswerable for the agent where no such test can be run. The procedures stay the organization's, because a runbook, a playbook, a drill and a measurement are records the organization keeps whoever runs the control they govern:

- A vendor's stop control, such as removing a licence or unpublishing an agent, counts toward D9-IR-CONTAIN where the organization's runbook names it, with the time the vendor documents for it to take effect, and toward D9-DRILL where a drill carries it out.
- A vendor's response to an incident in its own service counts toward the organization's playbook only through the steps the playbook takes with the vendor, such as its support route and the notice the vendor's agreement owes, and a vendor's statement that it responds to incidents in its own service meets no D9 criterion.
- A guardrail the vendor runs is read for its failure behaviour, its latency and its cost from the vendor's documentation or a customer test, and the step the organization's operators take when it fails stays required. Where the vendor documents no fail mode for a guardrail and no customer test can make the guardrail error or time out, D9-GUARD-FAILMODE is unanswerable for that guardrail. Where the vendor documents no latency for a guardrail and no customer test can show that latency, D9-GUARD-LATENCY is unanswerable for that guardrail.

An agent the organization builds on a vendor's platform, such as a Copilot Studio agent, runs the guardrails, instructions and models its maker's settings select, so those settings are the customer's own configuration, and the platform's operation under them is graded on the evidence the vendor produces.

**Not applicable across a level.** A level at which every criterion is not applicable for the deployment is not reached, and neither is any level above it, because a not-applicable verdict records that the deployment holds nothing for a criterion to govern and records no control. D9-OFFBOARD reads the owner of every agent, so every deployment in scope holds an instance at L2 and takes a D9 result.

**Conditions the rubric adds.** [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|The measurement protocol]]'s rubric adds two conditions to score 3 that no domain's criteria state: operational ID tagging of the domain's findings, and the live observation it requires, a trace, a policy decision and, where D3-APPROVE-GATE applies, a gate fire. A deployment that meets every D9 L3 criterion without them scores 2 under the rubric, and the assessment records which condition held it there.

### L2 detail

- **D9-GUARD-RUNBOOK.** *Deployment.* A runbook states, for each guardrail, the step the operator on duty takes when the guardrail fails, errors or times out, and what happens to the agent's model call meanwhile. For a guardrail the vendor runs, the behaviour is read from the vendor's documentation or a customer test, and the operator's step stays required. Not applicable where no guardrail runs on any agent's model calls. *Evidence:* the runbook's section for each guardrail, set against the deployment's guardrails.
- **D9-OFFBOARD.** *Deployment.* A runbook states what happens to each agent when its owner leaves the organization or moves to a role that no longer owns it: the agent passes to a named successor or is retired, and the runbook names who acts and within what time. The steps can be manual. A leaver procedure that ends the departing person's own access and names no step for the agents the person owns fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the runbook's owner-departure section, with the trigger it names.
- **D9-OFFBOARD-CREDS.** *Deployment.* A runbook states that each of the deployment's credentials that a departing person created, holds or can read is revoked or replaced when that person leaves, and names who does it. Not applicable where the deployment holds no stored credential, or where no person holds or can read one, as the credential store's access policy shows. *Evidence:* the runbook's credential section, set against the deployment's credentials and the access policy of the store that holds them.
- **D9-QUEUE-RUNBOOK.** *Deployment.* A runbook states, for each approval path, the record each approval writes, who reviews the path's requests and decisions and how often, and what the reviewer acts on, such as a request left unanswered past a stated time or a change in how often approvals are given. Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke waits for a human approval. *Evidence:* the runbook's section for each approval path, with the record it names.
- **D9-PROMPT-CLEAN.** *Deployment.* No system prompt of the deployment carries the sensitive information the first prevention strategy of `LLM07:2025` names: a credential or key, a database name, the users' roles or the application's permission structure.[^llm07] For a system prompt the vendor holds, the criterion is read from the vendor's documentation or a customer test. *Evidence:* each agent's system prompt as the agent runs it, read against the four kinds of information.

Several documents together can be the runbook, so an on-call runbook, a retirement procedure and a business procedure for a payment queue can each carry part of L2. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]]'s D2-OWNER, at D2 L3, names a current owner for each agent, and D2-OWNER-TRANSFER, at D2 L5, passes each departing owner's agents to a named successor and records the transfer, while D9-OFFBOARD asks at L2 for the written step, which can be manual. D2-LIFECYCLE, at D2 L3, has the deploy pipeline issue, rotate and revoke each agent's identity and lets no person joining, moving or leaving trigger any of the three, so a deployment that meets it and whose credentials no person holds or can read has no instance of D9-OFFBOARD-CREDS.

D9-PROMPT-CLEAN reads the prompt's content, because `LLM07:2025` holds that the system prompt should not be considered a secret or used as a security control, and places the risk in the sensitive information stored in it and in authorization checks delegated to the model.[^llm07] [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d6-data-rag|D6]]'s D6-INSTRUCT grades the prompt's integrity, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]]'s D4-OUTPUT-SCOPE records whether the output classifier detects system prompts among the exposure-restricted data an agent's model can reach.

### L3 detail

- **D9-GUARD-LATENCY.** *Deployment.* The latency each guardrail adds to each agent's model calls is tracked for that agent. For a guardrail the vendor runs, the latency is read from the vendor's documentation or a customer test. Not applicable where D9-GUARD-RUNBOOK is not applicable. *Evidence:* the latency series for each guardrail and agent over the period.
- **D9-GUARD-COST.** *Deployment.* The cost of each guardrail is tracked for each agent that uses it. Not applicable where D9-GUARD-RUNBOOK is not applicable, or for a guardrail whose cost the vendor includes in the price of the service it runs in, with no charge of its own. *Evidence:* the cost series for each guardrail and agent over the period.
- **D9-GUARD-FAILMODE.** *Deployment.* The organization records, for each guardrail, whether it fails closed or fails open, and a test that made each guardrail error or time out shows the recorded behaviour. Each guardrail on the model calls of an agent that can make a high-impact call fails closed. A unit test with a mocked guardrail, or a behaviour read only from code, fails the criterion. Not applicable where D9-GUARD-RUNBOOK is not applicable. *Evidence:* the recorded fail mode of each guardrail, with the record of the test that made it fail.
- **D9-REAP.** *Deployment.* A process finds the deployment's orphaned agents and orphaned credentials on a schedule the organization sets, and a rule sets the time within which each is passed to a successor, retired or revoked. Each orphan found in the twelve months before the assessment starts was resolved within that time. A process that looks for orphaned agents and not for orphaned credentials, or an orphan resolved past its time, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the process's schedule and scope, the rule with its time, and the period's findings with the date each was resolved.
- **D9-QUEUE-RATE.** *Deployment.* The approval rate of each approval path is tracked. Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke waits for a human approval. *Evidence:* the approval rate of each path for each period, with the records it is computed from.
- **D9-QUEUE-AGE.** *Deployment.* The median queue age of each approval path is tracked, with the requests that expired unanswered. Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke waits for a human approval. *Evidence:* the median queue age and the expired requests of each path for each period.
- **D9-PROMPT-CANARY.** *Deployment.* A canary token planted in each system prompt is checked for in every output and every tool-call argument the agent produces, and a match raises an alert, in [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]]'s sense: a record raised into the queue the organization's security monitoring triages ([[canary-tokens-for-llms|Canary Tokens for LLMs]]). For a system prompt the vendor holds, the criterion is read from the vendor's documentation or a customer test. *Evidence:* the planted token's record and the check's configuration, with an alert the check raised or a test of it.
- **D9-PROMPT-PROBE.** *Deployment.* Leak probes built from the four risk examples `LLM07:2025` lists, sensitive functionality, internal rules, filtering criteria, and permissions and user roles, run against each agent before each change to its system prompt or its model reaches production, and each run's results are recorded.[^llm07] A probe set that leaves out one of the four fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the probe set, each probe matched to its risk example, with the results of the runs in the period.
- **D9-MODEL-DEPRECATE.** *Deployment.* For each model, skill, MCP server or agent the deployment publishes for use outside the organization, the organization publishes a deprecation policy stating how long a version stays supported after its successor ships and how consumers are told before a version is retired. Not applicable where the deployment publishes nothing for use outside the organization. *Evidence:* the published policy, set against the components the deployment publishes.
- **D9-SHARE.** *Organization.* The organization belongs to at least one disclosure community whose scope admits reports about AI systems, and a procedure it keeps names who reports an AI incident or an AI vulnerability to that community, and when. Membership with no such procedure fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the membership record, with the procedure.
- **D9-ROLE.** *Organization.* A document the organization approved, such as a job description or a charter, names a role accountable for the security of the organization's AI systems in operation and lists the role's duties, and a person holds the role on the assessment date. *Evidence:* the document with the duties, and the personnel record of the person who holds the role.
- **D9-ROLE-DEPUTY.** *Organization.* For each duty D9-ROLE's document lists, an approved document names a deputy who performs the duty when the holder cannot, and the deputy holds the access the duty needs. A deputy named for some of the duties fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the deputy's designation for each duty, with the deputy's access to the systems the duty uses.
- **D9-IR.** *Organization.* An approved AI incident playbook covers each agent in scope and states, for each incident class it covers, the steps to detect, contain, eradicate and recover. It adapts an AI incident-response framework, such as CoSAI's AI Incident Response Framework V1.0: it follows the framework's lifecycle, and it adapts the framework's sample playbook for each incident class that can occur on the organization's agents, among them prompt injection on an agent that reads content it did not write, memory injection on an agent that keeps agent memory, and poisoning of a corpus an agent retrieves from.[^cosai] A playbook in the CACAO format and one in prose both count. *Evidence:* the playbook with its approval and scope, each incident class mapped to the framework's lifecycle and to the sample playbook it adapts.
- **D9-IR-CONTAIN.** *Deployment.* The AI incident playbook, or a runbook it names, gives the deployment's own containment steps: how to stop each agent, where each agent's records are, and who acts. A playbook whose steps name no control of the deployment fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the steps for the deployment, set against its agents' stop controls and record stores.
- **D9-IR-EXERCISE.** *Organization.* The AI incident playbook was exercised in the twelve months before the assessment starts, and the exercise's after-action report records each action it raised with an owner. *Evidence:* the exercise record with its scenario and participants, and the after-action report.
- **D9-IR-NOTIFY.** *Organization.* The playbook names the regulatory-notification path for an AI incident: each notification instrument that applies to the organization, the person who owns each notification, and the clock each runs on. *Evidence:* the playbook's notification section, set against the instruments that apply to the organization.
- **D9-IR-SCENARIO.** *Organization.* The playbook covers the compromise of a third-party model the organization's agents call: a model manipulated at its supplier and already acquired and in production. Not applicable where no agent in scope calls a model a third party supplies. *Evidence:* the playbook's scenario for a compromised third-party model.
- **D9-HIGHRISK.** *Deployment.* The categories of action that need a high-risk approval are defined before the deployment's agents can invoke those actions, along the four dimensions the [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] names: irreversibility, data classification, external parties and financial thresholds.[^aix-oversight] The definitions rank the categories. A definition that leaves out a dimension fails the criterion. The definitions can sit in an organization policy, which counts for each deployment it governs, or in the deployment's own record. Not applicable where no agent can invoke a write action, in D3's sense. *Evidence:* the category definitions, each dimension matched to the rule that applies it.
- **D9-HIGHRISK-RECORD.** *Deployment.* Each high-risk approval writes a tamper-evident record that carries the request, the parameters presented to the approver, the approver's identity and authentication method, the approver's explicit confirmation of the key parameters, the decision and the outcome.[^aix-oversight] Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke falls in a high-risk category. *Evidence:* the records of a sample of high-risk approvals, read field by field, with the store's write controls set against the approvers, the requesters, the agents and the approving system's administrators.
- **D9-HIGHRISK-DELAY.** *Deployment.* Each of the highest-risk actions waits a minimum delay, which the policy sets, between its approval and its execution.[^aix-oversight] Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke falls in the highest category. *Evidence:* the delay's setting in the approval path, with an action that waited it.
- **D9-HIGHRISK-SOD.** *Deployment.* Each critical action needs approvals from two or more people in different roles, and the policy names the roles, so neither the agent nor the requester chooses an approver.[^aix-oversight] Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke is a critical action. *Evidence:* the routing rule with the roles it names, and the approvals of a sample of critical actions.
- **D9-NOTICE.** *Deployment.* Each of the deployment's users is told that an AI model is involved, at the start of the interaction or with the first content an agent sends them.[^aix-aitransparency][^aix-oversight] A notice a user must look for elsewhere, such as a policy page, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the notice as each class of user meets it, set against each agent's channels.
- **D9-NOTICE-PROPERTIES.** *Deployment.* A record the organization keeps sets the deployment's published disclosure against each of the five properties `AI TRANSPARENCY` lists, the rough working of the model, the training approach, the type and source of the data used, the expected accuracy and robustness of the output, and any residual security risk, and marks each as covered in the disclosure or omitted from it ([`/go/aitransparency/`](https://owaspai.org/go/aitransparency/)).[^aix-aitransparency] The disclosure can be the organization's own text or a vendor's document the organization publishes or links for the deployment's users, such as a transparency note. *Evidence:* the coverage record, set against the disclosure.

D9-GUARD-LATENCY and D9-GUARD-COST measure what each guardrail costs an agent, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]]'s D4-BUDGET, at D4 L5, sets each guardrail's latency and cost budget, enforces it, and hands a call a breach leaves unscreened to the fail mode D9-GUARD-FAILMODE tests, so one breach test that exercises the fail mode can evidence both. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]]'s D3-MEDIATE-FAILCLOSED holds the enforcement point to deny a call when the decision point is unreachable, times out or errors, or when its policy source is absent or malformed, and D4-SANDBOX holds every command inside the sandbox. A policy sidecar's outage is therefore D3's, a command that runs outside a sandbox that failed to start is D4's, and a classifier's failure is D9's. D9 defines no risk tier of its own: a guardrail fails closed wherever its agent can make a high-impact call, which D4 defines from D3's destructive actions.

D9-REAP sweeps a deployment for agents whose owner has left and for credentials a leaver can still use or nothing uses. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d1-governance|D1]]'s D1-SHADOW-REAP registers or removes each agent the AI register lacks within a time the organization sets, D2-KILL is the operation that stops one agent, and D2-OWNER-TRANSFER records each transfer a departure starts. Where event-driven transfers leave no orphan, the sweep finds nothing to resolve, and D9-REAP is still graded on the sweep's schedule and scope, because a transfer finds no credential that nothing uses. On Microsoft, a Lifecycle Workflows leaver task transfers the sponsorships a departing sponsor holds to the manager, disabling an agent identity stops it from authenticating, and deleting a blueprint removes its child identities through a cascade that can run hours or days later.[^entra][^entra-lcw] Off Microsoft, NHI lifecycle tooling or SCIM deprovisioning can carry the removal, and on every platform the sweep and the rule that sets its time are the organization's.

D3 grades the gate and D9 the categories and the record. D3-TIER-DESTRUCT places each destructive action at confirm or block, D3-APPROVE-GATE holds a confirm-tier action until an approver acts, and D3-APPROVE-TOKEN, at D3 L5, binds each approval to its parameters, while D9-HIGHRISK defines which approvals are high-risk and D9-HIGHRISK-RECORD keeps what was approved, by whom and on what understanding. D3-SOD separates the proposer, the approver and the deployer of a change an agent proposes, and D9-HIGHRISK-SOD asks for two approvers in different roles on a critical action. D3-APPROVE-COVERAGE, at D3 L4, measures the share of each action class's executions that a human approved, and D9-QUEUE-RATE measures how often the approvers approve.

CoSAI's AI Incident Response Framework V1.0 adapts the NIST SP 800-61r3 lifecycle to AI and ships six sample playbooks: training-data poisoning, multi-channel prompt injection, memory injection, Poison-RAG, credential abuse through server-side request forgery, and memory and RAG injection.[^cosai] A manipulated third-party model already acquired and in production is an incident class none of the six describes, and the nearest detects poisoning in a training dataset the organization holds. The Exchange states that vulnerability response for a supplied model, data pipeline or dependency can be integrated into incident response processes.[^aix-supplychainmanage] [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d8-supply-chain|D8]]'s D8-DISCLOSE takes advisories on each agent's components into a triaged queue, D8-DISCLOSE-CONTAIN contains the ones that miss their deadline, and D9-IR-SCENARIO covers the incident that follows. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]]'s D7-PLAYBOOK, at D7 L5, runs a playbook in the security monitoring function for each class of alert the D7 detections raise, and D9-IR is the playbook the organization invokes once it declares an incident, so D7 detects and D9 responds, and one playbook can evidence both where it carries the alert classes and the incident lifecycle.

D1-ACCOUNTABLE, at D1 L2, names the role accountable for AI governance, and D9-ROLE the role accountable for the security of AI systems in operation, so one person can hold both and one document can evidence both. D1-DETAIL-REVIEW, at D1 L3, records what each item published about a deployment withholds and discloses, set against `AI TRANSPARENCY`, and D9-NOTICE-PROPERTIES records which of the five properties the users' disclosure covers, so the two criteria read one property list from opposite sides and an assessor grading either collects both. D9-MODEL-DEPRECATE governs what the organization owes the consumers of what it publishes, and D8-RETIRE is the other direction, retiring the deprecated or unmaintained components the agents use. D9-SHARE reads a named route for AI reports, because membership of an exchange whose procedures name no AI report shares nothing about an AI system.

### L4 detail

- **D9-QUEUE-STAMP.** *Deployment.* The rubber-stamp rate of each approval path is tracked, by a method the organization states for the path. Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke waits for a human approval. *Evidence:* the method for each path, with the rate for each period.
- **D9-QUEUE-P95.** *Deployment.* The 95th percentile of each approval path's queue age is tracked. Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke waits for a human approval. *Evidence:* the 95th percentile for each path and each period, with the records it is computed from.
- **D9-OVERSIGHT-INVOLVE.** *Deployment.* The organization names an involvement measure for each approval path, states the measure's method, and takes it for each period. Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke waits for a human approval. *Evidence:* the measure's method for each path, with each period's result.
- **D9-OVERSIGHT-TEST.** *Deployment.* In the twelve months before the assessment starts, the testing programme exercised each approval path adversarially, against people who hold the approver's role, with four techniques: urgency-driven bypass, approval-fatigue sequences and multi-step normalisation before a critical action, which the Exchange's `OVERSIGHT` names,[^aix-oversight] and confusion injection, which the Exchange's testing guidance names without defining and this domain reads as content in one approval request built to mislead the approver about what the action does.[^aix-testing] A programme that exercised three of the four techniques fails the criterion. Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke waits for a human approval. *Evidence:* the test report for each approval path, technique by technique, with its dates and results.
- **D9-OVERSIGHT-LIMIT.** *Deployment.* Each approval path limits the approvals one approver can give within one session, or within a window the policy sets, and a request past the limit waits or goes to another approver.[^aix-oversight] A limit on the requests an agent sends, such as a count of payment calls in a chat session, meets no part of the criterion. Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke waits for a human approval. *Evidence:* the limit's configuration in the approval path, with a request it held or routed.
- **D9-OVERSIGHT-FLAG.** *Deployment.* A detection compares each approver's approval rate in each session with a historical baseline the organization defines, such as the approver's own earlier sessions, and raises an alert when the rate exceeds the baseline by a threshold the organization states with its method.[^aix-oversight] A comparison with no stated threshold fails the criterion. Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke waits for a human approval. *Evidence:* the baseline's definition, the threshold with its method, and an alert the detection raised or a test of it.
- **D9-DRIFT-TRIAGE.** *Deployment.* Each drift finding on an agent in the period carries a recorded classification, benign or adversarial, made by a method the organization states that tests for an adversarial cause, such as a check of the period's inputs, configuration changes and detections, and each finding classed adversarial reaches the queue the organization's security monitoring triages. A cause recorded with no test for an adversarial cause fails the criterion. Not applicable where nothing monitors the agents' behaviour or their models' performance against a baseline. *Evidence:* the method, with each drift finding of the period, its classification and, for each adversarial one, its record in the monitoring queue.
- **D9-DRILL.** *Deployment.* A decommission drill ran in each of the last two quarters: the runbook that retires an agent was carried out against an agent on the production platform, such as a test agent registered for the drill, from stopping the agent to revoking its credentials and connections and closing its register entry, and each step's result and time were recorded. A tabletop exercise fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the drill report for each of the two quarters.
- **D9-MODEL-PIN.** *Deployment.* Each model an agent calls in production is named by a fixed version in the configuration production runs, such as a dated snapshot or a versioned model identifier, so the model cannot change under the agent without a change to that configuration. An alias the provider can repoint fails the criterion. For a model the vendor chooses, as for an in-suite assistant, the criterion is read from the vendor's documentation of how it changes the model. *Evidence:* the model identifier in each agent's production configuration, set against the provider's versioning scheme.
- **D9-SHARE-EXERCISE.** *Organization.* In the twelve months before the assessment starts, the organization took part in an exercise that a disclosure community or a supplier ran to rehearse the coordinated disclosure of an AI incident or an AI vulnerability among organizations. *Evidence:* the exercise record, naming its organizer, its scenario and the organization's part.

The rubber-stamp rate's method and the involvement measure are the organization's to define, and so are the thresholds D9-QUEUE-THRESHOLD reads at L5, because no source supplies a metric standard or a threshold for any of them. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]]'s D7-CONTROL-APPROVE raises an alert when an action that needs a human approval runs without one, or when an approval step starts approving without a person, and D9-OVERSIGHT-FLAG raises one when an approver's approval rate exceeds the baseline by the stated threshold. D7-DRIFT scores an agent's session for progressive relaxation, and D9-DRIFT-TRIAGE classifies the cause of each drift finding and sends the adversarial ones to the queue D7's detections feed.

[[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]]'s D2-KILL executes the one operation that stops an agent, once since its revocation path last changed, and D9-DRILL carries out the whole decommission runbook each quarter, so a quarter's drill that runs the kill switch against a running agent can evidence both. D2-ROTATE-MAP, at D2 L4, grades the map of each stored credential's consumers, and D8-VEX grades an exploitability statement for each vulnerability that affects a component the organization publishes outside it, so D9 grades neither. D8-VERSION records the version each model ran at over the twelve months before the assessment starts, an alias included where a run log names the version each run resolved to, and D9-MODEL-PIN requires a fixed version in production, so a deployment that records an alias's resolutions meets D8-VERSION and fails D9-MODEL-PIN.

### L5 detail

- **D9-LOOP.** *Deployment.* Each operational incident on an agent of the deployment in the last two quarters reached, within an SLA the organization publishes to the deployment's owners, a change to a control or a recorded decision to leave the controls unchanged, and the incident's record names the change or the decision. *Evidence:* the SLA, with each incident of the two quarters matched to its change or decision and its date.
- **D9-REAP-ZERO.** *Deployment.* At the end of each of the last two quarters the deployment held no orphaned credential, and each quarter's count is attested. *Evidence:* the attested count for each quarter, with the D9-REAP records behind it.
- **D9-PROMPT-ZERO.** *Deployment.* In each of the last two quarters the D9-PROMPT-CANARY check recorded no confirmed leak of a system prompt, and each quarter's count is attested. *Evidence:* the attested count for each quarter, with the check's record of alerts and their outcomes.
- **D9-MODEL-ZERO.** *Deployment.* In each of the last two quarters no agent in scope ran on a model version past the retirement date its provider announced for it, and each quarter's count is attested. *Evidence:* the attested count for each quarter, set against the providers' retirement notices for the models the agents ran.
- **D9-ROLE-CONTINUITY.** *Organization.* In each of the last two quarters a continuity test had the deputies perform the duties of D9-ROLE's role without its holder for a period the test states, including one run of the AI incident playbook from start to finish, and the test report records what each deputy did and each step that failed. *Evidence:* the continuity-test report for each of the two quarters.
- **D9-QUEUE-THRESHOLD.** *Deployment.* The organization publishes to the deployment's approvers and owners a threshold for each approval path's rubber-stamp rate, the 95th percentile of its queue age and its involvement measure, and in each period of the last two quarters each measure stayed within its threshold, or the excursion was recorded with the change to the approval path that followed. Not applicable where no action any agent can invoke waits for a human approval. *Evidence:* the published thresholds, with each measure's values over the two quarters and the record of each excursion.

The prerequisite gate into L5, graded once for the program, asks for bus-factor ≥2 with a documented continuity test, in which a deputy has run the runbook end to end at least once in the look-back window, and D9-ROLE-CONTINUITY asks for a test in each of the last two quarters, so D9's own L5 asks more than the gate. D9-REAP-ZERO, D9-PROMPT-ZERO and D9-MODEL-ZERO are clean-state attestations, which the gate's first condition accepts as evidence of D9's own two quarters of stable L4 and of no other domain's. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]]'s D7-ALERT-LOOP closes each detection alert, D8-LOOP-SLA each finding of the D8 controls, and D9-LOOP each operational incident, so one controls-update log evidences each loop whose records it carries. D8-RETIRE retires, replaces or excepts each deprecated or unmaintained component, and D9-MODEL-ZERO attests that no agent ran on a retired model version in either quarter.

### L5+ detail

- **D9-PUBLISH.** *Organization.* The organization published, in the twelve months before the assessment starts, metrics on the operation of its AI systems drawn from its own records, such as its operational incidents, its approval measures or its orphaned-credential counts, where readers outside the organization can read them. *Evidence:* the publication, with the records its metrics come from.
- **D9-STANDARDS.** *Organization.* A named member of the organization contributed, in the twelve months before the assessment starts, drift-detection patterns or bypass classes to a standards body or a public framework, such as CoSAI's incident-response work, the OWASP AI Exchange or MITRE ATLAS, through a pull request, an RFC or specification authorship, and membership alone falls short. *Evidence:* links to the contributions, with their dates.
- **D9-SHARE-LEAD.** *Organization.* In the twelve months before the assessment starts, the organization acted as the coordinator of a disclosure of an AI vulnerability or an AI incident among organizations, or led a disclosure community's work on AI incidents. *Evidence:* the disclosure record naming the organization as coordinator, or the community's record of its lead.

Each domain grades a contribution to its own subject: D1-STANDARDS to AI governance, D2-STANDARDS to identity working groups, D8-STANDARDS to the supply-chain standards and D9-STANDARDS to drift-detection patterns and bypass classes, so one piece of work counts in each domain whose subject it addresses. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d1-governance|D1]]'s D1-PUBLISH grades a governance artifact of the organization's own and D9-PUBLISH operational metrics, so one report that publishes both evidences both.

## Right-sizing by deployment shape

Right-sizing matters more in D9 than in any other domain: holding a single low-risk bot to mesh-grade incident response and continuity is the canonical over-scoping error.

| Deployment shape | Realistic D9 target |
|---|---|
| Member-facing RAG bot (no tools); [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026\|the persona]]'s bot, which holds a few tools, sits here | L2 → L3, narrow |
| In-suite productivity assistant (Gemini for Workspace, Microsoft 365 Copilot) | L3 → L4 |
| Desktop-agent productivity assistant ([[claude-cowork\|Claude Cowork]] class) | L3 → L4 |
| Coding / copilot | L3 → L4 |
| MCP / skill provider serving others | L4 + selective L5 |
| High-autonomy multi-agent mesh | L4 minimum, L5 where resourced |

**The PROMPT family and D9-OFFBOARD are the load-bearing D9 criteria for a read-only bot.** A canary token planted in the prompt signals a leak when it appears in output or in a tool call, and the four risk examples `LLM07:2025` lists supply the probes D9-PROMPT-PROBE runs. The same entry holds that the prompt is no secret and directs that credentials, roles and permission structure stay out of it.[^llm07] A bot with no write action has no approval queue, so the QUEUE, OVERSIGHT and HIGHRISK families are not applicable to it, and it runs no multi-agent incident response. Its target stops at L3, so the L4 approval measures and the quarterly drill sit beyond it, and holding the bot to them is the over-scoping the recalibration exists to prevent. The absent queue reaches the assessment as well as the level definitions: [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|the measurement protocol]] records the live approval-gate fire an L3 assessment observes as **not applicable** for a shape whose documented tier assignments place no action in the `confirm` tier, and [[cmm-known-limitations|CMM Known Limitations]] item 24 carries the change. The persona's bot is not wholly read-only: the stress test records a few tools and informal HITL on its write actions, so those actions bring the approval criteria into scope.

**The approval population for an in-suite productivity assistant is the whole workforce.** The approval measures carry the domain at that scale, and the vendor's own account of the class names approval-bot behavior as the expected drift ([[securing-workspace-genai-at-google-talk|Securing Workspace GenAI at Google]]). The talk's one reviewer metric is an override rate, the percentage of AI decisions humans reject, which Google monitors to recalibrate its own thresholds. It counts rejections as an approval rate counts assents, so it measures disposition and is no involvement measure. Workspace's Reports API logs each confirmation a user gives in the side panel, so the customer reads approval volume from its own tenant,[^gworkspacelog] and D9-OVERSIGHT-INVOLVE rests on a method the customer states. The vendor-held operations rule reads the rest: the assistant's classifiers, system prompt and model are graded on the vendor's documentation or a customer test, and the runbooks, the playbook and the notice stay the customer's.

**The approval measurements this domain asks for are readable from a desktop agent's own telemetry.** [[claude-cowork|Claude Cowork]]'s event export carries a decision and a decision source on every tool event, which separates a configured allow from a hook decision and from a human approval, so the approval rate, the rubber-stamp rate and queue age are measurements the customer takes from its own export. The target range still reaches L4 because the population is large: on Team plans the product is on for every member or for none, and only the Enterprise role model narrows it. Three limits sit against the measurement. Nothing is exported until an administrator sets the collector endpoint. Audit Logs do not yet cover the product, and the Compliance API returns transcript content rather than permission decisions, so the export is the only channel carrying the decision and its source, and D9-HIGHRISK-RECORD's record is tamper-evident only where the store the collector writes to is. A local session's conversation history stays on the member's computer, outside the retention policy and outside central management or export, which bounds what a decommission or a legal hold reaches. The queue also has a documented failure mode: a permission prompt a dispatched child task raises is denied after ten minutes and the task continues without that action, so an unattended approval becomes a dropped step rather than a blocked one, and D9-QUEUE-AGE counts it as an expired request. The row covers the desktop-agent variant alone.

**A copilot writes, and writing creates an approval queue that can be rubber-stamped.** D9-DRILL and the PROMPT family matter, and the approval volume makes D9-QUEUE-STAMP worth instrumenting.

**Approval fatigue is the mechanism that converts an interactive coding deployment into an unattended one.** The D9 human-factors question for agentic coding reaches past whether approvals are rubber-stamped to whether the approval volume has already changed the deployment shape. The documented remedy for prompt volume runs allowlisting, then autonomous modes, then suppressed prompts. Each step is defensible on its own, and together they move the deployment to a different row of the [[agentic-ai-security-reference-architecture|reference architecture]]'s shape table, where the load-bearing plane is Runtime rather than Control. D9-QUEUE-RATE and D9-QUEUE-STAMP track approval volume and disposition for each period, and a falling prompt count against a rising action count reads as a shape change that calls for re-assessment rather than as an efficiency gain. [[generative-coding-deployment-shape-2026|Generative Coding Deployment Shapes]] holds the shape definitions, and [[securing-agentic-coding|Securing Agentic Coding]] holds the shape's controls, none of which carries a D9 coordinate, so these level definitions grade the human-factors evidence directly.

**A provider publishes a deprecation policy for each skill and MCP server it ships, under D9-MODEL-DEPRECATE.** The exploitability statement its consumers read for each vulnerability that affects a published component is graded by [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d8-supply-chain|D8]]'s D8-VEX, and the feed that serves those statements by D8-VEX-FEED.

**HITL fatigue at scale is the dominant operational risk in a mesh**, and D9-LOOP earns its cost first at this shape. The [[taiwan-ai-agent-government-intrusion|Taiwan AI-agent government intrusion]] runs the same topology from the attacker side: a human set the objective but did not mediate step-by-step sub-agent coordination across a four-day, 12-wave campaign.[^taiwan] The continuity test D9-ROLE-CONTINUITY grades is no mesh-only control, because the gate into L5 reads one for the program and every domain's L5 claim rests on it.

A maker platform, such as Copilot Studio, is one deployment whose criteria are read agent by agent, and the platform's verdict is the weakest among its agents: each maker's settings are the customer's own configuration under the vendor-held operations rule, and a maker who names an approver in an agent's flow meets D9-HIGHRISK-SOD only where the organization's policy names that approver's role.

The [[lethal-trifecta|lethal-trifecta]] test lowers D9 the same way it lowers D3/D5: a contained, low-autonomy bot legitimately scores lower, and the score records that as an intentional trade-off.

## Cost model

Licensing is near-zero for an E5/Azure or AWS incumbent; the spend is people and run-rate.

The L3 row prices Entra, Sentinel and OpenTelemetry as entitlements, so the incumbent it describes runs on Microsoft. The latency and cost spans still bill as Sentinel ingestion, because the E5 data grant covers four named Microsoft 365 log sources and no agent telemetry.[^e5grant] Entra's sponsor-transfer Lifecycle Workflows, which transfer an agent's sponsorship to the manager when its sponsor leaves or changes roles, need an Agent 365 licence, an add-on to E5.[^entra-lcw] Of the two rows in the control landscape above that name a platform-native product, Google is thin on incident-response orchestration and carries no entry at all for agent decommission and orphan reaping, so a Google Cloud buyer builds the playbook that Microsoft and AWS configure, and the decommission automation the landscape credits to Microsoft alone. The four dominant costs sit on no licence line and are identical everywhere.

| Level | Licensing | Operational labor (the bottleneck) | Run-rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| L2 | ~0 | ~0.1–0.25 FTE: write the first runbook | — |
| L3 | ~0 (Entra, Sentinel and OTel already run) | the dominant cost: adapt CoSAI CACAO playbooks to your agents; stand up the canary hook and leak probes built from the LLM07:2025 risk examples; name a deputy for each duty of the AI security role; participate in a disclosure community | latency/cost-span logging into the SIEM (couples to D7 ingest) |
| L4 | ~0 incremental (MS) / small (AWS) | recurring: quarterly decommission drills; HITL-rotation staffing plus hand-built rubber-stamp / queue dashboards (no product); benign-vs-adversarial drift triage; disclosure-exercise participation | drift + HITL telemetry ingest |
| L5 | ~0 | highest: a closed-loop SLA needs an on-call rotation owning AI incident response; quarterly attestation evidence; a continuity test each quarter | continuous attestation + incident-replay logging |

Four costs dominate and none of them appears on a license line. An **on-call rotation** carries the largest of the four at L5. **Incident-playbook authoring** runs multi-week per agent class even though CoSAI ships the shape, and **decommission drills** cost quarterly facilitation labor. **HITL-rotation staffing** covers both the reviewers and the analyst time to hand-build the fatigue dashboards, because no product measures oversight quality.

## Customer critiques folded in

- *"D9 is L1–L2: no formal decommission or HITL-fatigue tracking."* The recalibrated L3 is the realistic near-term target for the persona's bot, and it is narrow because several L3 criteria are not applicable to a bot with a few tools, such as D9-MODEL-DEPRECATE where the deployment publishes nothing outside the organization. On the bot itself L3 asks for its guardrails' measures and fail mode, D9-REAP over its agents and credentials, which the Entra Agent ID platform every Entra tenant holds can support,[^entra-lcw] the PROMPT family, D9-IR-CONTAIN's containment steps, the user notice and its coverage record, and the HIGHRISK family for its write actions, beside the organization criteria every deployment reads. The stress test records informal HITL on the bot's few write actions, so its approval queue is small, and the recalibration meets the fatigue-tracking critique at L3 with D9-QUEUE-RATE and D9-QUEUE-AGE rather than with tooling.
- *"L5 assumes a cadence regulated FIs can't follow."* D9's L1–L5 dependencies are standards or stable patterns with one exception. The Entra Agent ID lifecycle path reached general availability on 2026-05-01, alongside Agent 365,[^entra-lcw] so the stress test's cadence argument applies to it. D9-REAP names no product, so a sweep that removes orphans through NHI lifecycle tooling or SCIM deprovisioning serves it as well as one on the Entra path, which keeps D9 cadence-safe for a buyer who takes that path, and the aspirational items sit at L5+.
- *"Cost under-tells the dominant spend."* Licensing is near-zero; the real spend is on-call, drill facilitation, and hand-built HITL dashboards, all labor and run-rate.
- *"Vendor-neutral catalog is an integration tax."* The platform-native column names Entra Agent ID and Sentinel for the all-Microsoft buyer; the off-stack residual (HITL-fatigue measurement, continuity practice) has no product on any stack, so it is a market-wide gap rather than a Microsoft-specific one.

## Open questions

- HITL-fatigue measurement has no product and no metric standard, so D9-QUEUE-THRESHOLD reads thresholds the organization publishes, and no source states when a rubber-stamp rate is too high.
- NIST AI 800-4 describes monitoring categories without prescribing a method, so D9-DRIFT-TRIAGE reads the organization's own method for telling benign drift from adversarial, and no source this wiki holds supplies an implementation spec for one.
- Entra Agent ID's orphan governance has been GA in both halves since 2026-05-01 and splits on licence instead: disable and delete-with-cascade come with the platform every Entra tenant holds, and the sponsor-transfer Lifecycle Workflows need an Agent 365 licence.[^entra-lcw] The cascade runs as a background task that can lag a blueprint's deletion by hours or days.[^entra]
- D9-NOTICE-PROPERTIES has no depth floor, and none can be written from the source. The Exchange lists five properties a disclosure can carry, states one floor — that users are informed an AI model is involved — and supplies no measure of sufficiency for any individual property,[^aix-aitransparency] so a one-line answer and a ten-page answer score the same. What a withholding decision under [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d1-governance|D1]]'s D1-DETAIL-REVIEW is bounded by is therefore that floor and a recorded coverage decision, and nothing finer.
- The Exchange's `CONTINUOUS VALIDATION` control is anchored to this domain in [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-crosswalk|the standards-crosswalk matrix]] and graded by no D9 criterion. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d6-data-rag|D6]] L3 grades protection of the reference corpus and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]] L4 grades a security eval cadence over jailbreak and escape scenarios; neither grades a model-quality measurement of correctness, robustness, or fairness. The D6 L4 clause requiring a data-removal decision to be justified against measured effect on model performance therefore carries its own measurement, since no level supplies one.
- The EU-side notification anchor exists: a serious incident may trigger EU AI Act Art. 73 reporting and, for in-scope entities, NIS2 notification, and the Exchange's regulatory map states the NIS2 clock as 24 hours / 72 hours / one month ([`/go/checkcompliance/`](https://owaspai.org/go/checkcompliance/)). What remains absent is the US FI-sector side — no FFIEC/GLBA/NCUA IR-notification crosswalk, including the GLBA breach-notification timeline; deferred to the crosswalk.

## Cross-cutting note and the gate into L5

D9 is a cross-cutting domain whose capabilities interact indirectly with the per-plane ones: HITL fatigue degrades D3's approval gates, decommission lag orphans D2 identities, and drift remediation feeds on D7 telemetry. Per [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-dependency-rules|the dependency rules]], D9 operational lag leaves upstream domains where they stand and is reported directly in D9's own row.

The gate a program clears to reach L5 rests on D9 twice, and the two roles differ. Bus-factor ≥2 with a documented continuity test is a program-level condition on D9's subject: the assessor grades it once, and every domain's L5 claim rests on it, so a program clears the gate's continuity bar before it reaches L5 anywhere. D9's own criteria carry the subject further, with D9-ROLE and D9-ROLE-DEPUTY at L3 and D9-ROLE-CONTINUITY's test in each of the last two quarters at L5. The two-quarter stable-L4 condition is graded per domain, so D9-REAP-ZERO, D9-PROMPT-ZERO and D9-MODEL-ZERO evidence D9's own two quarters and no other domain's. D9 therefore carries its own climb to L5 and one of the three program-level conditions every other domain's climb depends on.
