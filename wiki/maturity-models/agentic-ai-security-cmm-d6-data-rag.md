# Agentic AI Security CMM — D6 Data, Memory & RAG (Deep Dive)

Companion deep-dive to [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|the CMM]]'s D6 domain, written under the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026|recalibration method]]. The recalibration corrects a misweighting the [[agentic-cmm-regulated-fi-stress-test|regulated-FI stress test]] exposed. D6 was built around **corpus poisoning**, but the deployment that stress test scored, which the recalibration takes as its representative case, is a member-facing customer-service RAG bot over internal data, and there the live risk is **oversharing and inference exposure**: over-permissioned content surfaced or reconstructed for a user who should not see it ([[inference-exposure|inference exposure]]). Poisoning needs an open or multi-writer corpus to land; oversharing needs only ordinary enterprise permission sprawl. The recalibration adds answer-time entitlement enforcement as the load-bearing L3 capability and grades the tooling plainly: the Microsoft data-governance stack is largely GA, and the work it implies remains a multi-quarter labor project that licensing alone does not discharge.

**D6-ENTITLE grades the retrieval path.** The entitlement criterion reads what a retrieval layer returns from a corpus. Data that reaches an answer through a tool call, such as a claim record a claims system returns, is graded by [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]]'s D2-DELEGATE at D2 L2, and a corpus that every principal who can ask may read in full leaves D6-ENTITLE nothing to trim. A member-facing bot whose knowledge base is public product terms, and whose member data arrives through tools, therefore holds no instance of D6-ENTITLE, and D2-DELEGATE grades the member data.

In the [[agentic-ai-security-reference-architecture|Agentic AI Security Reference Architecture]], D6 grades the Data plane's rows other than its six supply-chain rows, which [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d8-supply-chain|D8]] grades, and the two domains share the plane's cognitive-file-integrity row, D6 at L3 under D6-INSTRUCT and D8 at L4 under D8-VERIFY-INSTRUCT.

D6's two risk families align with three [[nist-ai-600-1|NIST AI 600-1]] GenAI Profile risk categories:[^nist600]

- Oversharing and inference exposure are the §2.4 data-privacy concern, which covers models that leak, generate or infer sensitive information about individuals.
- Corpus poisoning is a §2.9 information-security risk. The Profile names data poisoning there, and its red-teaming action `MS-2.7-007`, tagged to information security among other risks, lists data poisoning and membership inference among the attacks to test.
- Corpus provenance is a §2.8 information-integrity concern. The Profile tags to it `MS-2.5-005`, which verifies the provenance of training data and of test, evaluation, verification and validation data, and that fine-tuning and retrieval-augmented generation data is grounded.

The Profile places both families inside GenAI risk management, and the levels below supply the graded controls and the recalibrated weighting between them.

**The reframe and the cost model rest on one customer's stress test.** The reframe, the levels and the cost model synthesize the recalibration method against the [[agentic-cmm-regulated-fi-stress-test|regulated-FI stress test]], the [[inference-exposure|inference-exposure]] concept and vendor documentation, and the L3 and L4 criteria also cite the [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] entries footnoted at each. The status column of the control landscape is a May 2026 snapshot; rows re-read since carry their own retrieval dates in the notes. The capabilities are stated to outlive that snapshot, and the launch stages in it go stale.

## Threat coverage

D6 is the primary domain for **ASI06 (Memory & Context Poisoning)** and a control surface for **ASI04 (Supply Chain)**, and it carries **Class 1 (insider — RAG and eval-harness integrity)** and **Class 4 (model-version — eval suite versioned independently)**. The customer-owned eval harness absorbs three classes at once — insider, APT, and model-version — which no other control in this domain does. See the [[threat-taxonomy-reconciliation|Threat Taxonomy Reconciliation]] matrix and the [[agentic-ai-threat-classes-2026|threat classes]].

**D6-SCAN-INJECT is evidenced through the route it screens.** The [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] specifies that route in its prompt-injection test procedure: where a system augments its input with untrusted data, whether retrieved content or, in an agentic system, typically a tool output, the attack inputs are presented through that insertion route as well, so each one passes the filtering, detection and insertion steps the untrusted data passes, which may require a dedicated testing API.[^aix-testing] A scanner evidenced from the user channel alone has shown only that it stops direct injection, so D6-SCAN-INJECT reads a test that presents crafted content through the ingest path, or a detection the scan recorded on content that arrived through it.

## Capability-decoupled levels

Stated as capabilities per [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026|rule 1]]; a control counts when it operates in production per rule 2. A control implemented through a product in the organization's approved-vendor pipeline with a documented production date satisfies its criterion on that basis alone, and the production date is the date the control entered the organization's production. A planned date satisfies none, and neither does a product the organization does not yet run in production, whatever the vendor has announced. The answer-time entitlement thread runs from D6-ENTITLE at L3 through D6-LABEL-GATE at L4 to D6-ENTITLE-INFER at L5.

- **L1 — Initial.** Retrieval inherits source-system permissions with no oversharing review, and nothing records corpus provenance or memory integrity.
- **L2 — Developing.** Every data source the agents read carries a class, each retrieved item names its origin, each extension of a corpus is reviewed before use, and a first assessment sets each corpus location's reach through the agents against the readers it is meant for.
- **L3 — Defined.** Each retrieval resolves the asking principal's read authorization in the corpus's own access model, from every copy it reads. Corpus content carries its source's trust level and passes injection and poisoning scans at ingest, the stores and the scope of each corpus are held, the first assessment's findings are closed, instruction files match a reviewed baseline, and the validation corpus is held apart.
- **L4 — Managed.** Agent memory is a governed store: partitioned, write-authorized, provenance-bearing, verified before use, reviewed between tasks and logged for replay. Retrieval is weighted by trust, runtime poisoning detection raises alerts with measured coverage, labels travel with answers and gate them, the reach assessment recurs, data decisions carry evidence, and memory and indexes can be rolled back.
- **L5 — Optimizing.** Corpus drift and cross-source contradictions are detected as they occur, scan thresholds rest on a documented poisoning-rate bound, a need-to-know boundary holds across a session's retrievals, and a quarterly drill restores state within a measured recovery time.
- **L5+ — Leading Edge.** Each item is signed and chained at ingest and verified at retrieval, trust labels propagate through a formal lattice, and sensitive retrievals carry zero-knowledge proofs of entitlement.

Document attestation defends against a poisoned corpus, and the member-data bot fails by *answering correctly from content the asker was not entitled to see*, so D6-ENTITLE sits at L3 and per-document attestation, D6-ATTEST, at L5+.

The entitlement thread has a boundary the level definitions state nowhere else. D6-ENTITLE at L3, D6-LABEL-GATE at L4 and D6-ENTITLE-INFER at L5 each consult an access model held beside the data: the authorization layer the corpus carries, then sensitivity labels, then knowledge-layer need-to-know. The [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] states that once training data is embedded in a model, the access-right variations that governed the original data cannot be controlled any more.[^aix-disclosureoutput] Data reaching the model through training or fine-tuning therefore sits outside the thread, and the wiki's [[inference-exposure|inference exposure]] page places that path inside the same risk class. Data reaching an answer through a tool call sits outside it too, and D2-DELEGATE grades it.

The training path carries two threats, model inversion and membership inference, which [[threat-taxonomy-reconciliation|the reconciliation matrix]] routes to this domain. The Exchange offers four routes against them.[^aix-inversion] The first is `SMALL MODEL`, the only control in the entry specific to this pair, which constrains model capacity when the model is trained.[^aix-smallmodel] The second is a set of four general input controls, three of which [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-crosswalk|the standards-crosswalk matrix]] anchors to D2, D5 and D7. The crosswalk leaves `OBSCURE CONFIDENCE` unanchored because no domain grades the confidence output it shapes, although a deploying organization sets it. The third is sensitive data limitation ([`/go/datalimit/`](https://owaspai.org/go/datalimit/)), which groups five data controls rather than input controls, four of them anchored by the crosswalk to this domain.[^aix-datalimit] The fourth is protection of the model against theft, because the Exchange states the attacks are much more efficient with full access to model attributes, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d8-supply-chain|D8]]'s D8-WEIGHTS grades weight protection for a model the organization trains or fine-tunes, at D8 L5. `SMALL MODEL` is graded at no level here, because a deployment consuming a third-party model does not set that parameter and an organization fine-tuning its own model is making a development-time choice this CMM does not assess. The threat is therefore recorded as ungraded with a named upstream control rather than as an L5 criterion. The mitigation the wiki does carry for it is [[differential-privacy|differential privacy]], which is subject to the same ownership boundary.

D6 grades three of the four sensitive-data-limitation controls the crosswalk anchors to it. D6-SCOPE, D6-SCOPE-IDENTIFIERS and D6-STORE-RETAIN at L3, with D6-SCOPE-MEASURE, D6-SCOPE-PROPAGATE and the obfuscation criteria at L4, are the memorization-side control set for the same two threats, acting on the data before it reaches the model where `SMALL MODEL` acts on the model's capacity. The fourth, the removal of data the intended purpose prohibits, is graded by no D6 criterion.

Four controls sit on the far side of the ownership boundary this domain already draws: three of the five data-poisoning controls the Exchange describes in its data-poisoning section, which also lists adversarial robust distillation among its controls, plus the one control it names against broad poisoning. All four are set when a model is trained:

- `MORE TRAIN DATA` increases the volume of benign data so that poisoned samples are outnumbered, at the stated risk of catastrophic forgetting and overfitting.[^aix-moretraindata]
- `TRAIN DATA DISTORTION` adds noise or smoothing so that inserted triggers stop being recognized.[^aix-traindatadistortion]
- `TRAIN ADVERSARIAL` reuses adversarial training against poisoning triggers built from slight alterations.[^aix-trainadversarial]
- `MODEL ENSEMBLE` splits the training set across several models so a deviating output signals possible manipulation of the training set.[^aix-modelensemble]

A deployment consuming a third-party model holds no instance of any of the four, and an organization fine-tuning its own model is making a development-time choice this CMM does not assess, the same reasoning that leaves `SMALL MODEL` ungraded above.

`TRAIN DATA DISTORTION` carries a control-ordering condition the others do not: distortion has no effect against an attacker with access to the training data after it has been distorted, so where the engineering environment is untrusted the control reaches only poisoning that happened outside it — collected at runtime or arriving through the supply chain — unless a separated training environment is established first.[^aix-traindatadistortion] `SEGREGATE DATA` is that prerequisite, and no D6 criterion grades the separated training environment it creates. The remaining two of the five poisoning controls fall on this side of the boundary. `DATA QUALITY CONTROL` is D6-SCAN at L3. `POISON ROBUST MODEL` can be applied to a model already trained, including one obtained externally, as the Exchange states,[^aix-poisonrobustmodel] and no level grades it: [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d8-supply-chain|D8]] records the choice to apply it outside this CMM's scope, and a method that retrains the model, such as fine-tuning on clean data or fine-pruning, yields a produced model that D8-LINEAGE and D8-WEIGHTS read where an agent in scope calls it.

## Assessor detail per level

L1 describes where a deployment starts and carries no criterion, so a deployment that does not meet every L2 criterion scores L1. It scores 0 under the measurement protocol's rubric only where no evidence of the L1 baseline exists, meaning the assessment holds no record and no interview answer about the domain for that deployment. The levels from L2 to L5+ each carry a criteria list below, each list stating what its own level adds.

Grading is cumulative: Level N requires every Level N–1 control plus the new criteria at Level N ([[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|the CMM]]), so a level is met only where every level below it is met.

Each criterion takes one of four verdicts. **Met** and **not met** are read from the evidence the criterion names, whoever operates the control. The assessment records the evidence's assurance class beside the verdict and names the artifact behind it, its issuer and its date, per [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|the measurement protocol]]:

- **Tested**: the customer or the assessor exercised the control and recorded what it did.
- **Inspected**: vendor tooling the customer can reach, or a record the organization keeps, such as an inventory or a plan, shows the control's state in the deployment.
- **Attested**: the vendor states the control in a document the customer holds, and the document's own scope names the control.

**Not applicable** is recorded where the deployment holds no instance of what the criterion governs, and the reduced scope is recorded as an intentional trade-off in the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-dependency-rules|effective-score]] strategic-rationale field. **Unanswerable** is recorded where the instance exists, the customer can run no test and keeps no record of the control's state, and the vendor supplies neither inspectable output nor an attestation that names the control. An unanswerable criterion never counts as met: its level stays open, and every level above it stays open under cumulative grading. The assessment names what would close it. A criterion that can be not applicable states that condition alongside the criterion. Each level's list below holds criteria only, and a paragraph after a list states no criterion: it carries maturity, market or provenance commentary, or the boundary between this domain and another.

**D6 grades one deployment at a time.** A deployment is the unit the assessment scope names: one agent application, or one agent platform a vendor provides together with the agents the organization runs on it. Evidence of a control counts only for the deployments the control covers. D6 carries no dependency cap and takes [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]]'s unit, because D2-DELEGATE grades the data that reaches the same deployment's answers through tool calls, and the two halves of the deployment's data access are read on one scope. Six terms fix what the criteria range over:

- **An agent** is one deployed configuration: its code, instructions, tools and grants as the deployment runs them. Replicas of one configuration are one agent, and two configurations are two agents even where they share code. The deployment's inventory is the one D2-INVENTORY grades, whether or not it meets that criterion.
- **The agents in scope** are those the deployment runs in production, per rule 2. An agent outside production enters scope once it holds a production credential or reaches production data. Evidence from a pilot outside scope counts for no criterion, and the gap report can cite it as the planned route to a level. A deployment with no agent in scope, such as a pilot that holds no production credential and reaches no production data, takes no D6 result, and the assessment records it as outside scope.
- **A corpus** is content the deployment's configuration connects for its agents to draw on as context through a retrieval layer: the knowledge sources and grounding scope a platform agent is given, an index or a vector store the organization builds, the mail, files, calendar and chats of a tenant an in-suite assistant grounds on, or the working copy a coding harness runs in. **The retrieval layer** is the component that selects content from a corpus for an agent's context in answer to a query or a task, however the model reaches it, through the platform's grounding step or through a tool such as an MCP server over an index, and **a retrieval** is one such selection. Content an agent obtains by calling a tool on a system that holds business records, such as a claims or a payroll system, reaches its context through a tool call, which D2-DELEGATE grades, whatever the tool is called.
- **A memory store** holds agent memory in [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]]'s sense: content an agent writes for its own later use or another agent's and a later task, conversation or agent reads back as context, such as a vendor's memory feature, a table an agent's flow writes and reads back, or an instruction file an agent edits that later runs load. A checkpoint kept so that a task or a session can resume, and read back only when that task or session resumes, is session state, such as a framework's thread checkpoint that lets a person continue a conversation. A record an agent writes into a business system through a tool is an action. A store that is both a corpus and a memory store, such as a vector store agents write summaries into and retrieve from, falls under both sets of criteria.
- **An agent's instructions** are the files it loads as standing instructions, whoever writes them: its system prompt, identity files such as `IDENTITY.md`, and the instruction or rules files a harness loads, such as `CLAUDE.md` or `.cursorrules`. The settings, hooks and MCP server definitions a harness reads are the agent's configuration in [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]]'s sense.
- **Fine-tuning data** is data the organization supplies to fine-tune a model its agents call, whoever runs the training. A model that a tool or a service calls for the agent, such as a document-extraction service, is part of that tool, as D4 defines an agent's model calls, so its training data counts for none of the deployment's fine-tuning criteria and its validation data for neither holdout criterion.

A criterion that names every agent, corpus, retrieval, store, entry or file is met only where it holds for each one. No percentage threshold applies, because the deployment's configuration enumerates its agents, the corpora their retrieval layer serves and the stores they write, and one corpus outside the rule is the failure D6 grades. A criterion over every retrieval or every entry is read on a sample that holds, for each corpus or store, retrievals or entries from each kind of location it holds. An agent whose configuration the organization cannot produce, such as a workstation that has stopped reporting to device management, does not meet a criterion that ranges over it. The deployment's verdict is the weakest among its agents, corpora and stores: not met where any fails the criterion, then unanswerable where any verdict is unanswerable, and not applicable only where the criterion is not applicable to every one. The assessment names the scope it graded, and where a narrower scope would change an agent's result, such as one repository of a shared CI workflow, it reports that agent beside the deployment's result.

Each criterion names the object it ranges over, a corpus, a retrieval, a memory store, an agent's instructions, a fine-tuning dataset or a validation corpus, and is not applicable where the deployment holds none. A corpus two deployments share, such as a site that an in-suite assistant and a maker platform's agents both ground on, is graded in each deployment that retrieves from it, on that deployment's retrieval layer and the principals who can ask its agents, and one record of the corpus, such as its classification or its reach assessment, evidences each deployment whose scope it covers. A memory store two deployments write is graded the same way. A uniformly public corpus, such as the web a grounding step searches, takes one class under D6-CLASSIFY and holds no location D6-REACH assesses.

**Grading words.** The criteria use these terms in one sense each:

- **Authorization layer**: the access model a corpus carries, which D6-ENTITLE resolves and at whose grain D6-CLASSIFY and D6-REACH read the corpus. A corpus holding no per-document access model is graded on the layer it does hold:
    - A document corpus carries per-document entitlements.
    - Source control carries the asking developer's read grants, at the grain the product sets them, such as the repository on GitHub, with the path scope of the working copy the agent retrieves from.
    - A whole-tenant corpus carries the tenant's access-control lists with label-aware policy over them.
    - A corpus reached through a connector carries the source system's own permissions, and a folder an agent reads on a device carries the operating-system account's reach inside the folders the organization selects.
- **Uniformly public**: a corpus is uniformly public where every item in it is readable by every principal who can ask the deployment's agents, and its access model holds it so, such as the organization's published documents, or a policy site every employee can read where only employees can ask. The principals are those who can ask any of the deployment's agents, in the deployment's result and in a side report alike, and an item is readable by a principal where its own access at its source admits that principal, so a platform's uploaded knowledge files hold a uniformly public corpus only where each file's source admits every principal who can ask.
- **Asking principal**: the human whose request starts the run, in a session or by an explicit invocation, the test D2-DELEGATE sets for acting for a human. A run that an event or a schedule starts has no asking principal, whoever caused the event.
- **Extension**: a component that adds a source to a corpus the deployment's retrieval layer serves, such as a knowledge source added to a platform agent, a connector or plugin that feeds an index or a grounding scope, web grounding switched on, an MCP server over an index, or an editor extension that indexes content for a harness. A tool that reads a system of business records adds no source, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]]'s D3-ALLOW grades the tool list. An agent the deployment's agents call, such as a published agent an assistant reaches by an @mention, adds no source either: its knowledge sources are graded in the deployment that runs it, and D3 grades the call.
- **Class**: a class of the organization's classification scheme, such as Public, Internal, Confidential or Restricted.
- **Store**: a component that holds a copy of corpus content for the retrieval layer, such as an index, a vector store with its embeddings, or a platform's uploaded knowledge files. The working copy a harness reads is the repository at a ref, and no store.
- **Oversharing**: reach through the agents to a corpus location wider than the readers its class and its owner intend, whether a retrieval runs under an identity that reads more than the asker or the location's own grants are wider than intended.
- **Poisoning** takes the [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]]'s two classes: sabotage, which produces unwanted results for regular inputs, and targeted poisoning, or a backdoor, which fires only on specific inputs.[^aix-datapoison]
- **Validation corpus**: a set of inputs with expected outputs the organization runs to decide whether an agent in scope, or a model its agents call, performs correctly before a change reaches production, such as a golden set of questions with expected answers or a hold-out split of training data. A suite of adversarial test cases is an evaluation, which [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]]'s D7-EVAL grades. A copy outside the organization's stores is one placed beyond the corpus's access policy, such as an extract sent to a supplier, and the queries a validation run sends to a model place none. An organization that has placed no copy meets D6-HOLDOUT-TRANSFER on the corpus's storage and access policy, and a copy found later fails it.
- **An agent's context**: the content its model reads for a turn, the prompt, the retrieved items, the tool results and the entries read back from memory.
- **Exposure-restricted data** carries D4's sense: personal data, confidential identifiers, passwords, tokens, the classes the organization's scheme restricts, and system prompts.
- **Alert** and **in production**, for a detection, carry D7's senses. An alert is a record a detection raises into the queue the organization's security monitoring triages, and a detection runs in production where it reads the deployment's data as the data arrives.
- **Task** and **session**: [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]]'s sense, one unit of work an agent was started for and one run of an agent from its start to its end.
- **Tested**: a test counts where it ran in production, or in another environment on the corpus, the retrieval configuration, the stores and the access model that production runs, and its record names the environment and the revisions. A demonstration the organization runs and the assessor observes is a test.

**Names and precedence.** Each criterion carries a stable name, D6 and a mnemonic for what the criterion requires, and the name leads the criterion in bold. The level is an attribute recorded beside the name, so a criterion that changes level keeps its name. Members of a family share its first mnemonic, as D6-MEMORY-WRITE and D6-MEMORY-LOG do, and each member is a separate criterion because a deployment can hold one without the other and an assessor collects different evidence for each. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|The CMM]]'s D6 levels restate each criterion under the same name, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|the measurement protocol]]'s D6 questions and artifacts cite each one by that name, in plain text. A level's statement above summarizes its list, and where the two read differently the list governs.

**Vendor-held retrieval and memory.** A vendor holds a deployment's retrieval layer or memory where the vendor's service indexes the corpus, selects what the model reads or stores what an agent remembers, and the customer holds no configuration of that component, as with an in-suite assistant's semantic index and memory feature or a maker platform's knowledge feature. The criteria stand for those components and are graded on the evidence the vendor produces, because the corpus, the retrievals and the stored entries exist whoever operates them: vendor operation is no ground for a not-applicable verdict. A vendor document whose own scope names the product and the control evidences it as attested, and a record the customer can search, such as an audit record naming each resource an answer drew on, evidences it as inspected. Where the vendor documents neither, the criterion is read from a test the customer runs, such as a two-account comparison for D6-ENTITLE, and it is unanswerable for the agent where no such test can be run. The corpus stays the customer's, so D6-CLASSIFY, D6-REACH and its family, and the extensions the customer switches on are graded on the customer's own records. An agent the organization builds on a vendor's platform, such as a Copilot Studio agent, retrieves from the knowledge sources and writes to the stores its maker selected, so those selections are the customer's own configuration, and the platform's operation under them is graded on the evidence the vendor produces.

**Not applicable across a level.** A level at which every criterion is not applicable for the deployment is not reached, and neither is any level above it, because a not-applicable verdict records that the deployment holds nothing for a criterion to govern and records no control. D6-CLASSIFY ranges over every data source an agent reads, so a deployment whose agents read a corpus, a system through a tool or a memory store holds an instance at L2.

**Conditions the rubric adds.** [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|The measurement protocol]]'s rubric adds two conditions to score 3 that no domain's criteria state: operational ID tagging of the domain's findings, and the live observation it requires, a trace, a policy decision and, where D3-APPROVE-GATE applies, a gate fire. A deployment that meets every D6 L3 criterion without them scores 2 under the rubric, and the assessment records which condition held it there.

### L2 detail

- **D6-ORIGIN.** Each item a retrieval returns into an agent's context carries its origin in the terms the corpus indexes, set by the retrieval layer: the document for a document corpus, the repository, the ref and the path for source control, and the mailbox, site or drive for a whole tenant. An item that reaches the context with no origin, or with one only the model states, fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent retrieves from a corpus. *Evidence:* a sample of retrievals from a log, a trace or the answers' citations, each item naming its origin.
- **D6-EXTEND.** Each extension is reviewed by a named person before its first use, on a record that names the extension, the source it adds to the corpus and the reviewer, dated before the extension first served a retrieval. The extensions differ by shape: knowledge sources and web grounding on an in-suite assistant or a maker platform, connectors and plugins that feed an index or a grounding scope, and MCP servers over an index or editor extensions that index content for a coding harness. An approval of a class that admits extensions nobody reviewed, such as a setting that lets any maker add a knowledge source, fails the criterion for each extension it admits. Not applicable where no agent retrieves from a corpus. *Evidence:* the review record of each extension, set against the list of the corpus's sources with the date each first served a retrieval.
- **D6-CLASSIFY.** A record the organization keeps assigns a class to each data source an agent reads and each memory store it writes: each corpus its retrieval layer serves, at the grain the corpus's authorization layer grants access on, each system its tools read, and each store, which takes the highest class of the content it holds. The grain differs by corpus: each document, or the library that holds it, for a document corpus, each repository for source control, or each branch where the product grants read access per branch, and each site, drive and mailbox, with its calendar and chats, for a whole tenant. A class set by a rule that names the units it covers, such as a default label on every file and message in the tenant or a class the data inventory gives a whole system, assigns it. A unit the record leaves without a class, or a scheme that names no unit of the corpus, fails the criterion, and the items need not carry the class at this level. Not applicable where no agent reads content beyond the prompts it receives and no agent writes agent memory. *Evidence:* the classification record, set against the list of each corpus's units, the systems the agents' tools read, the stores and the memory stores.
- **D6-REACH.** A dated assessment covers every location of every corpus the agents retrieve from and records, for each location, the principals who can reach its content through the agents, set against the readers its class and its owner intend, and it lists each location of oversharing as a finding. The assessment reads both routes to oversharing, a retrieval under an identity that reads more than the asker and a location whose own grants are wider than intended, such as a site open to every employee or a sharing link open to anyone. For source control the locations are the repositories, set against the developers who can read each, and for a whole tenant they are every site, drive, mailbox, calendar and chat. An assessment that leaves a location out, such as a vendor's default assessment of a tenant's most-used sites, or one that lists who uses the agent without setting each location's reach against its intended readers, fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent retrieves from a corpus, or where every corpus is uniformly public. *Evidence:* the assessment report, with its scope set against the list of each corpus's locations and its findings.

**L2 takes its grain from the corpus's authorization layer.** Grading is cumulative, so a level is met only where every level below it is met, and an L2 criterion written for one corpus shape would put L3 out of reach for the others whatever L3 says. D6-ORIGIN, D6-CLASSIFY and D6-REACH therefore read each corpus at the grain its authorization layer grants on, the grain D6-ENTITLE resolves at L3. GitHub grants read access per repository, so there the class sits on the repository, and a per-file scheme would grade nothing the level above enforces. The paths a task does not need are D6-SCOPE's exclusions at L3. No product in the control landscape above applies a data classification to a repository, so a program builds the register itself and evidences it as a record it keeps.

D6-CLASSIFY reads every data source an agent reads, a system its tools read included, where D6-ORIGIN, D6-EXTEND and D6-REACH read the corpora a retrieval layer serves. An agent that reads everything through tools therefore holds an instance at L2, and its tool data is D2-DELEGATE's. D6-EXTEND reviews what widens a corpus: the allowlist of the tools an agent may call is D3-ALLOW's, and the provenance of an acquired skill, plugin or MCP server is [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d8-supply-chain|D8]]'s D8-ABILITY-SOURCE, so one review record can evidence D6-EXTEND and D8-ABILITY-SOURCE where the component adds a source and the record names its publisher and source.

### L3 detail

- **D6-TRUST.** Each item a retrieval returns carries the trust level of its source, from a scale the organization records, such as an employee author, an external submitter and a public web page, set by a component outside the model from the item's source record and delivered with the item into the agent's context. An item delivered with no trust level, or with one the model assigns, fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent retrieves from a corpus, or where each corpus takes content from one source only, such as a library one team writes. *Evidence:* the trust scale, the code or configuration that attaches the level, and a sample of retrieved items carrying it.
- **D6-SCAN.** Each item that enters a corpus, and each dataset the organization supplies for fine-tuning, is analysed for deviation from the expected content of its corpus or dataset by a method the scan's configuration names, such as statistical deviation or outlier detection over the items' embeddings, and the items already held are analysed again on a schedule the organization sets.[^aix-dataqualitycontrol] The scan carries two thresholds: an item past the higher is filtered out, and an item past the lower is flagged for investigation. A scan with one threshold, or one that reads new items and never the content already held, fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent retrieves from a corpus and the organization supplies no fine-tuning data. *Evidence:* the scan's configuration naming its method and both thresholds, with run records over new items and over the content already held.
- **D6-SCAN-TEST.** The scan's method is shown fit for the poisoning the corpus or dataset faces by a targeted test, such as a run against a poisoned-dataset benchmark or a resistance test with planted samples, whose record gives the share of planted samples each threshold caught.[^aix-dataqualitycontrol] A method adopted with no test record fails the criterion. Not applicable where D6-SCAN is not applicable. *Evidence:* the test record, naming the benchmark or the planted set and the result at each threshold.
- **D6-SCAN-INJECT.** Each item that enters a corpus is scanned for instructions addressed to a model before it can be retrieved, and a detected item is kept out of the corpus or held for review. The criterion is read from a test that presented crafted content through the ingest path itself, or from a detection the scan recorded on content that arrived through it, because a scan shown on the user channel alone shows only that it stops direct injection.[^aix-testing] Not applicable where no agent retrieves from a corpus. *Evidence:* the scan's configuration on the ingest path, with a test record of crafted content presented through that path or a detection the scan recorded on ingested content.
- **D6-INSTRUCT.** Each file an agent loads as instructions matches a baseline recorded for its reviewed version, checked when the agent loads it, and a change reaches the agent only as a new reviewed version with a new baseline. Two mechanisms meet the check: a hash baseline verified at session start and after each change, where a mismatch holds the session or raises an alert, or an immutable artifact pinned by digest, such as a container image, which the runtime verifies when it loads it and which the running agent cannot write. A file that an agent or another writer can change and a later load reads with no check against a baseline, such as a rules file one session edits and the next loads unchecked, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the baseline for each instruction file, with the check that verifies it at load, and for a hash baseline its record of a mismatch or a test of it.
- **D6-STORE-ACCESS.** Each store's access policy lets the retrieval layer's identity and named administrators read its content, and no other identity or application reads the store directly.[^aix-augleak] A store open beyond them, such as a vector database any service in the account can query, fails the criterion. Not applicable where the retrieval layer keeps no copy of corpus content. *Evidence:* each store's access policy, set against the identities that hold access to it.
- **D6-STORE-ENCRYPT.** Each store encrypts the corpus content and the embeddings it holds at rest.[^aix-augleak] A store with any part unencrypted, such as an embeddings table outside the encrypted volume, fails the criterion. Not applicable where the retrieval layer keeps no copy of corpus content. *Evidence:* each store's encryption configuration.
- **D6-STORE-RETAIN.** Each store removes an item's copy and its embeddings once the source item is deleted or leaves the corpus's scope, within a period the organization states for the store.[^aix-shortretain] A store that keeps copies of deleted items past the period, or has no stated period, fails the criterion. Not applicable where the retrieval layer keeps no copy of corpus content. *Evidence:* the stated period and the deletion or rebuild job, with a deleted source item's copy found absent after the period.
- **D6-SCOPE.** A recorded scope decision states, for each corpus the retrieval layer serves and each dataset the organization supplies for fine-tuning, the fields and records the application needs, and the corpus, each copy the retrieval layer keeps of it, and the dataset hold nothing beyond that scope.[^aix-dataminimize] For source control the decision names the paths excluded from retrieval, and for a whole tenant it names the locations the assistant grounds on and any it excludes. A corpus or dataset with no recorded decision, or one holding content the decision excludes, fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent retrieves from a corpus and the organization supplies no fine-tuning data. *Evidence:* the scope decision for each corpus and dataset, set against a sample of its content and of each copy.
- **D6-SCOPE-IDENTIFIERS.** Each identifier a fine-tuning dataset keeps only to serve data-removal requests or lifecycle management is listed, and the listed identifiers are excluded from the data the model trains on.[^aix-dataminimize] An identifier kept in the dataset and absent from the list, or listed and still trained on, fails the criterion. Not applicable where the organization supplies no fine-tuning data. *Evidence:* the retained-identifier exception list, set against the fields the training run excludes.
- **D6-ENTITLE.** Each retrieval made for an asking principal returns only items that principal can read under the corpus's authorization layer, resolved when the retrieval runs, from every copy it reads: the source, an index, and a vector store with its embeddings.[^aix-augleak] The assessment names the layer each corpus carries and reads, for each layer, a test in which two principals holding different grants asked for the same content and each received only what that principal can open. For source control the two developers differ in a repository one of them cannot read. A retrieval that runs under a service identity meets the criterion only where the asking principal's grants narrow that identity's reach on each retrieval, such as a search service that checks each result against the source's permissions for the caller, and fails where content the service identity reads reaches an asker who cannot open it. The source systems' access records name the service identity, so they do not settle the verdict. Not applicable where no agent retrieves from a corpus for an asking principal, or where every corpus is uniformly public. *Evidence:* the authorization layer named for each corpus, with a two-principal test record for each layer, or for a layer the vendor operates, the vendor document stating the enforcement.
- **D6-ENTITLE-TASK.** Each retrieval made in a run no human starts, such as an event-triggered build or a scheduled job, runs under a scope that covers the repositories, sites or mailboxes its task reads and no more, bound to the task by the component that starts the run. A scope that reaches the whole estate, or one the agent can widen during the run, fails the criterion. Not applicable where every run that retrieves from a corpus has an asking principal. *Evidence:* the scope the run's identity holds, set against the corpora its task reads, with the setting that binds the scope to the task.
- **D6-REACH-REMEDIATE.** Each finding of the latest assessment that meets D6-REACH is closed within a period the organization states, by a change to the location's grants, by removal of the content, or by an exception the location's owner records with the principals it accepts. A restriction that hides the location from the agents and search and changes no grant, such as Restricted Content Discovery, which Microsoft describes as a temporary governance control, holds a finding open as interim.[^rcd] A finding past the period, or closed with no record, fails the criterion. Not applicable where D6-REACH is not applicable. *Evidence:* the remediation record, each finding of the assessment matched to its grant change, removal or recorded exception and its date.
- **D6-HOLDOUT.** Each validation corpus is stored apart from the training data, the model artifacts and the agent's code and instructions, whichever the organization holds, and no identity that can write any of those can write the validation corpus, so reaching the model or the data behind it does not reach the baseline the model is measured against.[^aix-continuousvalidation] A validation corpus in the same store or under the same write access as any of them, such as a hold-out split in the training bucket, fails the criterion. Not applicable where the organization keeps no validation corpus. *Evidence:* the validation corpus's storage and access policy, set against those of the training data, the model artifacts and the agent's repository.
- **D6-HOLDOUT-TRANSFER.** A copy of a validation corpus leaves the organization's own stores only under a recorded transfer that names the recipient and binds it to an access policy at least as restrictive as the corpus's own.[^aix-devdataleak] A copy held outside the organization with no such record, such as a monthly extract to a model supplier, fails the criterion. Not applicable where the organization keeps no validation corpus. *Evidence:* the register of the corpus's copies outside the organization's stores, each with its transfer record.

**D6-ENTITLE and D2-DELEGATE divide an agent's data by path.** [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]]'s D2-DELEGATE at D2 L2 bounds everything an agent acting for a human reads, writes or triggers to that human's own access, and it grades data that reaches an answer through a tool call. D6-ENTITLE at L3 grades what a retrieval layer returns, resolved at answer time in the corpus's authorization layer and across the layer's own copies. A retrieval layer that trims each retrieval to the asking human's grants evidences both criteria, and one that returns content the asker cannot open fails both. A run no human starts falls outside D2-DELEGATE, and D6-ENTITLE-TASK grades its retrieval path. The tool reach of such a run, such as a mail trigger whose connection opens more mailboxes than its task reads, is graded by neither criterion.

The service-identity rule follows the [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]]'s alignment statement: augmentation data is best assumed to leak to the output, so its access rights align with those of the users who can see the output.[^aix-augleak] A retrieval under a service identity aligns where each result is checked against the caller's own grants, and a service identity whose reach nothing narrows leaks that reach to every asker. The layer follows the corpus, and a desktop agent of the [[claude-cowork|Claude Cowork]] class carries two: a connector resolves the member's permissions in the source system, and a connected folder resolves the member's operating-system account inside the folders the organization selects.

D6-SCAN-INJECT and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]]'s D4-INJECT-INDIRECT screen the same content at two points: D6-SCAN-INJECT as it enters the corpus, so a detected item never persists, and D4-INJECT-INDIRECT on the model's request path as a retrieval delivers it. A component that screens at both points evidences both, and a detection flag an assistant's audit record carries on a resource it reached at answer time is D4's evidence.

The poisoning scan's residual is stated at the source. False positives may lead to unnecessary investigation or to the removal of rare but valid samples, which can harm model accuracy, and false negatives may occur where a poisoned sample resembles normal data. A sophisticated attacker can design poisoned samples to sit inside the normal distribution, which reduces the reach of any purely anomaly-based approach.[^aix-dataqualitycontrol] The Exchange also states that where thresholds consistently fail to separate poisoned from benign samples, alternative or complementary controls may be more effective, so a D6-SCAN-TEST record that shows no separation shows a scan that runs and a method that does not fit. The Exchange describes its five detection methods for training data. Reject on Negative Impact and gradient fingerprinting measure a sample's effect on training, and activation clustering reads a model's activations, so for a retrieval corpus D6-SCAN names statistical deviation or outlier detection over the items' embeddings as its example. The scans apply to a closed, single-writer corpus as well, because the writer can be compromised, and the right-sizing below sets the target for that shape.

**D6-HOLDOUT grades an asset this domain already claimed.** The threat-coverage section above assigns eval-harness integrity and an independently versioned eval suite to D6, and records that the customer-owned eval harness alone reaches the insider, APT, and model-version classes together. The Exchange supplies both the control and its bound: test data stored separately from training data and model artifacts, and kept less exposed than either, can help validation surface integrity issues while other parts of the system are compromised, and where an attacker can manipulate both the model and the test data, the validation results may no longer be trustworthy.[^aix-continuousvalidation] D6-HOLDOUT reads "less exposed" as write access, because the bound turns on an attacker changing both. D6-HOLDOUT-TRANSFER carries the confidentiality half, because the Exchange scopes development-time data leak to train **or test** data and states that either can be confidential because it holds personal data or intellectual property ([`/go/devdataleak/`](https://owaspai.org/go/devdataleak/)).[^aix-devdataleak] Response to a validation result sits in other domains, such as version pinning under [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d9-operations|D9]]'s D9-MODEL-PIN, and no domain holds the validation cadence itself. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]]'s D7-EVAL grades a quarterly security evaluation, with multi-turn and multi-session scenarios under D7-EVAL-TURNS and D7-EVAL-SESSIONS, and names correctness, robustness and fairness in none of them, so no level in this CMM grades a model-quality validation cadence. D6-HOLDOUT protects the reference itself and grades nothing about how often it is run.

D6-INSTRUCT reads the files an agent loads as instructions, the object [[cognitive-file-integrity|cognitive file integrity]] protects. The harness's settings, hooks and MCP server definitions are the agent's configuration in D4's sense: [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]]'s D3-MEDIATE-OUTSIDE bars the agent's own tool calls from writing the scope its policy comes from, and D4-SANDBOX-CONFINE bars the code in the sandbox from changing that configuration. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d8-supply-chain|D8]]'s D8-VERIFY-INSTRUCT, at D8 L4, reads the same instruction files and adds that the digest a load checks traces to the file's publisher and that a mismatch stops the load, and neither criterion reads the configuration. One baseline and its load-time check can evidence D6-INSTRUCT and D8-VERIFY-INSTRUCT where they meet D8's two conditions. An instruction file an agent edits is agent memory as well, and the L4 memory criteria grade its writes.

**D6 grades no groundedness check.** [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]]'s D4-GROUND grades the check between an answer and the content retrieved or supplied for it at D4 L4, in path before the answer reaches the person, and D6's criteria grade the corpus the check reads: its entitlements, its provenance and its integrity. A sampled review of answers after delivery meets neither domain's criteria.

The store criteria apply the Exchange's `AUGMENTATION DATA CONFIDENTIALITY` control, access control, encryption and minimized retention, to the retrieval layer's own copies. Augmentation data stored outside its regular storage lacks that storage's protection, and vectors representing augmentation data are typically vulnerable to information extraction and belong inside the protection.[^aix-augleak] D6-ENTITLE covers what those copies return to an asker and the store criteria who reaches a copy directly, so remediating a document archive without its vector projection leaves an unclassified copy of the corpus behind a separate access model. The Exchange states that limiting a retention period can be seen as a special form of data minimization ([`/go/shortretain/`](https://owaspai.org/go/shortretain/)), so D6-STORE-RETAIN and D6-SCOPE grade one capability at two horizons.[^aix-shortretain]

### L4 detail

- **D6-TRUST-WEIGHT.** The retrieval layer ranks or filters the items it returns by the trust level D6-TRUST attaches, under a weighting its configuration records, so that of two items matching a query equally the lower-trust one ranks lower or is withheld. Not applicable where D6-TRUST is not applicable. *Evidence:* the weighting configuration, with a test in which a lower-trust item planted to match a query ranked below a higher-trust one.
- **D6-MEMORY-PARTITION.** Each memory store keys every entry to a partition, such as the agent, the session or the principal the entry serves, and a component outside the model lets an agent or a session read only the partitions its policy names, from an identity or a key the model's output cannot set.[^aix-augintegrity] A partition held only by a filter value the model supplies, or a store every agent and session reads in full, fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent writes agent memory. *Evidence:* the partition key and the read check for each store, with a read refused across partitions.
- **D6-MEMORY-WRITE.** A component outside the model lets an agent or a session write only to the partitions its policy names, and a write outside them is refused.[^aix-augintegrity] A store any agent or session can write in full, such as an instruction file every session in a repository can change unasked, fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent writes agent memory. *Evidence:* the write policy for each store's partitions, with a write refused outside them.
- **D6-MEMORY-PROVENANCE.** Each entry a memory store holds records its source, the agent or session that wrote it, the time of the write and its partition, set by the store or the component that writes and not by the model.[^aix-augintegrity] An entry whose writer names an identity several agents or sessions share, such as a maker's account a flow runs on, or that records no source, fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent writes agent memory. *Evidence:* a sample of each store's entries, read field by field.
- **D6-MEMORY-VERIFY.** Each entry is verified against an integrity record set at the write, such as a hash or a signature, before it enters an agent's context, and an entry that fails is rejected or quarantined.[^aix-augintegrity] Not applicable where no agent writes agent memory. *Evidence:* the verification step with its integrity record, and an entry it rejected or quarantined, or a test of it.
- **D6-MEMORY-RESET.** An agent's context is reviewed between tasks and reset where the review finds content that could steer the next task, so adversarial content in one task's context does not carry into the next.[^aix-augintegrity] A context that carries from task to task with no review, or a review that resets nothing it finds, fails the criterion. Not applicable where every task starts with a fresh context and no agent writes agent memory. *Evidence:* the review step between tasks and its reset rule, with a reset it performed or a test of it.
- **D6-MEMORY-LOG.** Each memory store's state changes are written to an append-only log that no identity the agents hold can change or delete, so the store can be replayed at an earlier point.[^aix-augintegrity] A store whose changes overwrite in place with no log, or whose log an agent's identity can alter, fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent writes agent memory. *Evidence:* the change log's configuration with its immutability setting, set against the identities able to write it, and a sample of its entries.
- **D6-DETECT.** A detection in production reads each write an agent makes to agent memory or to a corpus and raises an alert on an entry it scores as poisoned, such as an instruction addressed to a model or a fabricated fact planted for later reads.[^aix-augintegrity] A check that reads only the ingest pipeline's content, which D6-SCAN grades, or that flags entries into a report no analyst triages, fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent writes to agent memory or to a corpus. *Evidence:* the detection rule over memory and corpus writes, with an alert it raised or a test of it.
- **D6-DETECT-CLASSES.** The coverage of each poisoning detection the deployment runs, D6-SCAN's scan and D6-DETECT's detection, is measured for both poisoning classes on planted samples: sabotage samples that degrade answers to ordinary queries, and targeted samples that fire only on chosen inputs, which for a retrieval corpus include passages crafted to steer the answer to a chosen question, as PoisonedRAG's are.[^aix-datapoison][^poison] The record gives, for each class, the share of planted samples the detection flagged. A coverage statement that reports one rate across both classes, or leaves the targeted class out, fails the criterion. Not applicable where D6-SCAN and D6-DETECT are both not applicable. *Evidence:* the coverage record, with each class's planted set and the share flagged.
- **D6-DETECT-PROTECT.** The logic, thresholds and baseline distributions of each poisoning detection, D6-SCAN's and D6-DETECT's, sit under an access policy that no identity able to write to the ingest pipeline, a corpus or a memory store holds, and under an integrity check that records each change, so an attacker reaching the data does not thereby reach the check on it.[^aix-dataqualitycontrol] Not applicable where D6-SCAN and D6-DETECT are both not applicable. *Evidence:* the access policy over each detection's configuration and baselines, set against the identities that write the pipeline and the stores, with the integrity check's record.
- **D6-SCOPE-MEASURE.** Each field kept in or removed from a fine-tuning dataset rests on a measurement of its effect on the model's correctness, robustness or fairness, recorded with the decision.[^aix-dataminimize] A removal or a retention asserted with no measurement fails the criterion. Not applicable where the organization supplies no fine-tuning data. *Evidence:* the removal justification with its measurements, set against the dataset's fields.
- **D6-SCOPE-PROPAGATE.** A deletion or a correction applied to a source record reaches every training dataset derived from it, as the Exchange directs,[^aix-dataminimize] and every corpus entry, copy and embedding derived from it, which this level adds, on a record that links each source record to what was derived from it. A derived copy that keeps a deleted or corrected record past the period the organization states, or a derivation with no link record, fails the criterion. Not applicable where the deployment's corpora and fine-tuning data derive from no source records, such as a corpus the retrieval layer reads in place. *Evidence:* the source-to-derived link record, with a propagated deletion traced through it.
- **D6-OBFUSCATE.** Each exposure-restricted field a fine-tuning dataset keeps is obfuscated before training, such as by tokenization, masking or generalization.[^aix-obfuscate] A field kept in the clear fails the criterion. Not applicable where the organization supplies no fine-tuning data, or where the dataset keeps no exposure-restricted field. *Evidence:* the obfuscation configuration, set against the dataset's exposure-restricted fields.
- **D6-OBFUSCATE-TABLES.** The mapping tables of any token-based obfuscation are held under an access policy at least as restrictive as the policy over the data they reverse.[^aix-obfuscate] Not applicable where no obfuscation keeps mapping tables. *Evidence:* the mapping tables' access policy, set against the policy over the source data.
- **D6-OBFUSCATE-RESIDUAL.** A record names the quasi-identifiers the obfuscated dataset still holds, such as locations, times, visited websites and timestamped activity, and states for each whether it is removed, generalized or accepted, with the person who accepted it.[^aix-obfuscate] Not applicable where D6-OBFUSCATE is not applicable. *Evidence:* the residual record, set against the dataset's fields.
- **D6-REACH-CADENCE.** The assessment D6-REACH describes runs again over the same scope at a cadence the organization states and after each new corpus or extension, and each run's findings reach D6-REACH-REMEDIATE's closure within its period. A single run, or a cadence with a missed run in the period, fails the criterion. Not applicable where D6-REACH is not applicable. *Evidence:* the assessment schedule, with the run history for the period and each run's findings with their closure.
- **D6-LABEL-CARRY.** Each answer an agent gives from a corpus shows the highest class among the items it drew on, and each item it creates from them, such as a document or a message it drafts, carries that class, set by a component outside the model.[^labels] An answer that draws on classed items and shows no class, or content created from them that carries none, fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent retrieves from a corpus. *Evidence:* a sample of answers and created items, each set against the classes of the items it drew on.
- **D6-LABEL-GATE.** A policy outside the model keeps content of the classes the organization names out of the agents' answers, for the principals or channels the policy names, by excluding the items from what the retrieval layer returns or by blocking the answer that draws on them.[^dlp] An answer that draws on content the policy names and reaches a principal or a channel the policy excludes fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent retrieves from a corpus. *Evidence:* the policy, with an answer it gated or a test of it.
- **D6-ROLLBACK.** Each memory store, and each index or vector store the organization keeps over a corpus, can be restored to a recorded earlier state, and a test restored each and recorded the state the restore returned.[^aix-dataqualitycontrol] A store with no recorded earlier state, or a restore never tested, fails the criterion, and a rebuild from the current sources restores no earlier state. Not applicable where the deployment keeps no memory store and no index or store over a corpus. *Evidence:* the restore test record, naming the store, the earlier state and what the restore returned.

**The structural criteria carry the weight the detector cannot.** The Exchange states that distinguishing legitimate memory updates from adversarial poisoning at scale remains difficult, and that structural access control and write authorization limit blast radius when detection is uncertain.[^aix-augintegrity] It also describes cross-agent memory access without explicit authorization as lateral movement through shared state, which is why D6-MEMORY-PARTITION and D6-MEMORY-WRITE sit beside D6-DETECT; [[agent-memory-isolation|agent memory isolation]] carries the design and [[memory-poisoning|memory poisoning]] the attack shapes. Provenance records and read-boundary integrity checks add storage and latency, which the cost model's L4 row prices.

D6-MEMORY-PROVENANCE and D6-MEMORY-LOG, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]]'s D7-LOG-MEMORY at D7 L3, read separable records. D6's two criteria grade provenance kept with each entry and an append-only log of the store's state changes, and D7-LOG-MEMORY a record of each write in the deployment's telemetry, which the write's instrumentation can emit without either, so neither level rests on the other and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-dependency-rules|the effective-score dependency rules]] record no prerequisite between them. D6 takes D7's definition of agent memory, so one store falls under both domains' memory criteria or under neither.

D6-DETECT and D7-FORWARD-SCAN grade two alerts. D7-FORWARD-SCAN at D7 L3 brings the flags D6-SCAN's lower threshold raises at ingest into the telemetry. D6-DETECT grades a second detection, which reads the writes agents make to memory and to corpora as they happen, a memory write passing no ingest scan, and raises an alert of its own. The Exchange states that sabotage surfaces on regular inputs while a backdoor evades review, because a model holds no code to inspect, its parameters are unreadable to a human, and testing runs on normal cases the trigger avoids by design, so D6-DETECT-CLASSES reports the two rates apart.[^aix-datapoison] The Exchange states no rate for either class, so the criterion records the measurement and sets no threshold.

D6-MEMORY-RESET reviews an agent's context between tasks. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]]'s D2-TASKBIND binds the session and the token to one task, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]]'s D4-SANDBOX-CLEAN destroys a sandbox's transient state at the task's end, so the three cover different artifacts and a program can hold any one without the others.

D6-LABEL-CARRY and D6-LABEL-GATE read labels at two points. Microsoft 365 Copilot in Word, PowerPoint and Outlook applies the source's label to the content it creates, and Copilot Chat shows the highest-priority label among the items a response used,[^labels] which evidences D6-LABEL-CARRY. A data-loss-prevention rule on sensitivity labels keeps labeled files and emails out of Copilot's response, though an item can still appear among the citations,[^dlp] which evidences D6-LABEL-GATE.

D6-SCOPE-PROPAGATE's link record is required by no other level in this CMM. D6-SCOPE at L3 grades a scope decision and no lineage, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d8-supply-chain|D8]] inventories acquired datasets and corpora with source, version and hash, at the grain of the dataset rather than the record, so a program meets D6-SCOPE-PROPAGATE only by building the record itself.

Two sources bound minimization from different directions. The Exchange records that AI models often tolerate reduced feature sets and incomplete data better than traditional applications, which loosens the utility bound on how much can be removed without functional loss.[^aix-dataminimize] [[microsoft-sdl-evolving-security-practices|Microsoft's SDL post]] records that purpose limitation and data minimization become harder to enforce once a platform blends structured and unstructured data, tools, APIs and agents into one surface.[^mssdl] Both propositions hold of the same system, and they name different bounds. D6-SCOPE-MEASURE reads the utility bound for fine-tuning data, where the model's performance can be measured, and D6-SCOPE at L3 with D6-SCOPE-PROPAGATE at L4 read the enforcement bound, a reduction held across every derived copy.

The obfuscation criteria follow the Exchange's control and its two stated limitations: removing or obfuscating personal data is often insufficient, because identity can be induced from other retained data such as locations, times, visited websites and timestamped activity, and token-based approaches add risk if their mapping tables are compromised.[^aix-obfuscate] D6-OBFUSCATE-RESIDUAL records the first, and D6-OBFUSCATE-TABLES grades the access model over the second. The control is scoped to training data throughout, so the criteria reach fine-tuning data the organization supplies and do not extend to the retrieval corpus, which D6-ENTITLE and the store criteria cover. The deployer may implement part of `DATA MINIMIZE` and the whole of `OBFUSCATE TRAINING DATA` where better positioned, provided the provider communicates the requirement.[^aix-dataminimize][^aix-obfuscate] `OBFUSCATE TRAINING DATA`, which the source files at development time, can therefore be owned by the deploying organization, and the fine-tuning criteria read the data the organization supplies whoever runs the training.

### L5 detail

- **D6-SCAN-DRIFT.** A detection in production compares the content distribution of each corpus, as items arrive, with a baseline recorded for it, and raises an alert when the corpus departs from the baseline past a threshold the organization sets. A drift chart or a periodic report with no detection behind it fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent retrieves from a corpus. *Evidence:* the corpus baseline and the detection rule with its threshold, with an alert it raised or a test of it.
- **D6-SCAN-BOUND.** The organization documents, for each corpus and fine-tuning dataset, the largest share of poisoned items it tolerates, with the reasoning its domain supports, and sets each poisoning detection's thresholds from that bound. A threshold with no documented bound behind it fails the criterion. Not applicable where D6-SCAN and D6-DETECT are both not applicable. *Evidence:* the threshold-justification record, naming each bound and the thresholds set from it.
- **D6-CONTRADICT.** A detection checks each answer that draws on items from more than one source for contradicting statements before the answer reaches the person, and an answer it flags is withheld, qualified or routed to review under a rule the organization sets, with the items and their sources recorded. A check run on a sample after delivery fails the criterion. Not applicable where no corpus takes content from more than one source. *Evidence:* the detection's configuration with its rule, and its flags for a sample of answers or a test.
- **D6-ENTITLE-INFER.** The retrieval layer applies a need-to-know policy that states, for each role, the topics or classes of content it may receive, and decides each retrieval against what the session has already returned as well as against the item's own grant, so items each readable alone are refused where together they disclose what the role may not know. A check of each item alone fails the criterion. Not applicable where no agent retrieves from a corpus for an asking principal, or where every corpus is uniformly public. *Evidence:* the need-to-know policy, with a test in which retrievals within one session, each permitted alone, were refused once together they crossed the policy.
- **D6-ROLLBACK-DRILL.** A drill each quarter restores a memory store or an index over a corpus to a recorded earlier state, in production or on production's configuration, and measures the time the restore took against a recovery-time target the organization states. A quarter with no drill, or a drill with no stated target, fails the criterion. Not applicable where D6-ROLLBACK is not applicable. *Evidence:* the drill report for each quarter, with the measured restore time and the target.

System-prompt leak detection is [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d9-operations|D9]]'s trip-wire, D9-PROMPT-CANARY with the leak probes of D9-PROMPT-PROBE at D9 L3, so D6 grades no second instance. D6-INSTRUCT grades the prompt's integrity, and D4's D4-OUTPUT-SCOPE records whether the output classifier detects system prompts.

Cross-source contradiction detection has no vendor production answer in the control landscape above, and a program assembles it from components generally available for longer, such as a natural-language-inference model or a judge model reading the retrieved items. That reading keeps D6-CONTRADICT at L5, as [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]] keeps its L4 criteria. D6-SCAN-BOUND lets D6-DETECT-CLASSES's measured coverage be read against a stated tolerance, and D6-SCAN-DRIFT watches each corpus as a whole where D6-SCAN reads each item. D6-ENTITLE-INFER closes the retrieval path of [[inference-exposure|inference exposure]], and whether label-granular gating suffices without it is the open question below.

### L5+ detail

- **D6-ATTEST.** Each item is signed at ingest by its author or the ingest pipeline and linked into a hash chain over the corpus, and the retrieval layer verifies the signature and the chain before the item enters an agent's context, refusing an item that fails. Signed commits in a repository whose harness never verifies them before reading fail the criterion. Not applicable where no agent retrieves from a corpus. *Evidence:* the attestation chain, with the retrieval layer's verification record and a refused item or a test.
- **D6-TRUST-LATTICE.** Each item carries a label from a formal lattice of trust levels, and each answer's label is computed from the labels of the items it drew on, as the lowest trust among them under the lattice's order, so an answer drawing on contradicting sources carries the lower trust and a rule outside the model decides what it may be used for. Not applicable where D6-TRUST is not applicable. *Evidence:* the lattice definition and its implementation, with the computed labels for a sample of answers.
- **D6-ENTITLE-PROOF.** Each retrieval of content of a class the organization names carries a zero-knowledge proof that the items returned satisfy the asking principal's entitlements, and a verifier outside the retrieval layer checks each proof. Not applicable where no corpus holds content of the named classes. *Evidence:* the verifier's logs.

Rule 2 holds these three criteria at L5+: per-document attestation has no vendor production answer and its open-source tools are research-grade, as the control landscape records, a formal taint lattice for cross-source contradiction is research-stage, and zero-knowledge proofs for retrievals have no shipping product.

## Right-sizing by deployment shape

| Deployment shape | Realistic D6 target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Member / customer-service RAG bot over internal data | L3 → L4 | Oversharing is the live risk: D6-ENTITLE and D6-REACH-REMEDIATE carry L3, and member data a tool returns is D2-DELEGATE's. Trust weighting and runtime detection earn their cost on open corpora |
| RAG over open / multi-writer or web corpus | L4 → L5 | Poisoning becomes first-order: D6-TRUST-WEIGHT, D6-SCAN-DRIFT and D6-CONTRADICT earn their cost |
| Agent with long-lived memory | add the L4 memory criteria | Context/memory poisoning and rollback become first-order regardless of corpus shape |
| No-retrieval tool agent | L3 | No corpus: D6-CLASSIFY over the systems its tools read carries L2, D6-INSTRUCT carries L3 with D6-HOLDOUT and D6-HOLDOUT-TRANSFER where a validation corpus exists, and its tool data is D2-DELEGATE's |
| Coding agent over a repository | L3 | The repository is the retrieved corpus; it is multi-writer and attacker-reachable, and D6-ENTITLE resolves against the developer's repository grants |
| In-suite productivity assistant (Gemini for Workspace, Microsoft 365 Copilot) | L3 → L4 | The whole-tenant corpus spans every mailbox, file and calendar the employee can reach; the tenant access-control list with label-aware policy is the authorization layer, and oversharing is the recorded failure mode |
| Desktop-agent productivity assistant ([[claude-cowork\|Claude Cowork]] class) | L3 → L4 | Two authorization layers carry D6-ENTITLE: a connector resolves the member's permissions in the source system, and a connected folder resolves the member's operating-system account; D6-REACH covers the folder selection as well as the tenant |

**A coding agent is a retrieval agent, and the repository is the corpus.** Filing coding agents under "no-retrieval tool agent" understates D6 for this shape. [[guardfall-shell-injection-audit|GuardFall]] delivered its payloads through injected READMEs, compromised Makefiles, and malicious MCP servers, and the [[claude-code-github-action-credential-exposure|CI credential exposure]] takes its instructions from an issue body, a pull-request description or a comment. Every corpus criterion applies to the repository, D6-TRUST, the ingest scans and D6-ENTITLE among them, and D6-INSTRUCT covers the instruction files, since `CLAUDE.md`-class files are corpus and instructions at once; an instruction file the agent edits falls under the memory criteria as well. Boundary marking between repository content and instructions is input segregation, which [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-crosswalk|the standards crosswalk]] anchors at [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]], where D4-CONTEXT grades the rule that holds where the marking fails.

**The in-suite assistant's index, memory and system prompt are vendor-held, and the vendor-held rule decides each criterion on them.** Microsoft states that every Copilot prompt runs in the security context of the user who initiates it,[^dlp] which evidences D6-ENTITLE as attested, and a two-account comparison over mail, calendar and chats as well as files raises it to tested. The criteria over the index's stores, its ingest scans and the assistant's system prompt read the vendor's documentation, and they are unanswerable where it states nothing, so the row's L3 target rests on evidence only the vendor can supply. The corpus stays the customer's: D6-CLASSIFY, D6-REACH and D6-REACH-REMEDIATE read the tenant's own records.

The [[lethal-trifecta|lethal-trifecta]] test lowers the target level directly here: a bot with no private-data access and no exfiltration path does not need the full answer-time enforcement stack. Removing the sensitive-retrieval capability costs less than controlling it.

## Cost model

Every instrument this model treats as entitled is Microsoft's: Purview DSPM, label-aware DLP, and Restricted Content Discovery, which needs a Copilot licence. The `~0` at L2 therefore describes an E5 tenant with Copilot and nobody else. A Google Workspace buyer holds answer-time entitlement natively, because Gemini has the same access to Workspace data as the signed-in user,[^gworkaccess] so the tenant access-control list and its DLP rules are the authorization layer, as the in-suite row above records. The control landscape above names no Google counterpart for the oversharing assessment, and Google Cloud's Data Security Posture Management, which covers BigQuery and Cloud Storage, shuts down on 2027-02-01,[^gcpdspm] so the model prices that assessment for a Google buyer as an off-stack component. The remediation labor this model calls the dominant cost is unaffected by which stack funds the rest.

| Level | Licensing | Operational labor | Run-rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| L2 | ~0 for an E5 + Copilot tenant (Purview DSPM and DLP are in entitlements) | the first assessment + a labeling scheme | — |
| L3 | ~0 incremental | **the dominant cost: a multi-quarter oversharing-remediation project** | classification and scan consumption |
| L4 | ~0 incremental | continuous posture management + detector tuning + SIEM integration | SIEM ingest for detector signals + storage and latency of memory provenance and integrity checks |
| L5 | mostly off-stack for contradiction / attestation gaps | drift-bound justification, rollback drills, semantic-boundary tuning | streaming-scan + retrieval-check cost |

**For an incumbent the licensing line is near zero, and the remediation labor carries the spend.** Microsoft's deployment blueprint for Copilot runs in three steps: remediate oversharing, then set up guardrails, then meet regulations.[^msblueprint] One managed-service provider, Fusion Computing, runs its pre-Copilot audit as inventory, classify, triage, remediate and lock, and puts a 50-seat tenant at 40 to 60 IT-hours over 30 calendar days.[^fusion] Small-tenant figures do not scale linearly. At enterprise scale no search-scope control stands in for remediation: Restricted SharePoint Search caps its allow list at 100 sites and retires on 2027-01-31,[^rss] and Restricted Content Discovery, which Microsoft names in its place, hides a site from Copilot and organization-wide search without changing who can open it.[^rcd] Plan two-to-four quarters and one-to-two FTE-equivalent, recurring. New content re-introduces oversharing, so the project never fully closes.

## Customer critiques folded in

- *"D6 is over-built around poisoning for our member-data bot."* Addressed: oversharing and inference exposure are the core of the L3 criteria for the closed-corpus shape. L3 keeps the ingest scans, D6-SCAN with its test and D6-SCAN-INJECT. D6-TRUST-WEIGHT and D6-DETECT sit at L4, and drift and contradiction detection at L5, where the open-corpus shapes need them.
- *"Microsoft is our weakest plane for data."* Reframed: the assessment-and-labeling tooling is GA and capable. The genuine weaknesses are narrower:
    - Remediation is a labor project no tool performs.
    - Retrieval-scope reduction is interim. Restricted SharePoint Search retires on 2027-01-31, and Restricted Content Discovery, generally available, is a temporary governance control that changes no permission.[^rss][^rcd]
    - Cross-source contradiction detection and per-document attestation have no vendor production answer.
- *"The cost was invisible."* Addressed: the cost model names the multi-quarter remediation project and marks licensing near-zero for an E5 + Copilot shop.

## Open questions

- **Answer-time semantic entitlement at scale.** Whether sensitivity-label-granular gating under D6-LABEL-GATE is sufficient, or whether knowledge-layer authorization of the Knostic class, which D6-ENTITLE-INFER grades, is required, is unresolved.
- **Restricted SharePoint Search retires on 2027-01-31.** New enablement has been blocked since 2026-07-31, and Microsoft does not migrate an RSS allow list to Restricted Content Discovery, which has been generally available since May 2025 and ships inside a Copilot licence through SharePoint Advanced Management. A program that narrowed Copilot's reach with RSS moves each restriction to RCD, or finishes remediating the sites, before that date.[^rss][^rcd]
- **Defender memory-poisoning detection maturity.** Microsoft publishes advanced-hunting queries for links that plant instructions in an assistant's memory, and the detection list of its AI-agent detection, in preview, names indirect prompt injection and no memory poisoning, so the capability is detective guidance today and short of a turnkey detection for D6-DETECT.[^mspoison][^defxdr]
- **FI-specific grounding stops at generic safeguarding.** [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-crosswalk-us-fi|The US regulated-finance crosswalk]] maps D6 to GLBA safeguarding of customer and member information and to the revised interagency model-risk guidance on model data inputs, which carves generative and agentic AI out of its scope. The crosswalk records the US regime as silent on RAG oversharing, vector-store scoping and memory poisoning, and a mapping of each L3 and L4 criterion to those anchors is open.
- **No enterprise data-governance-labor benchmark.** The remediation-effort estimate is directional, drawn from one managed-service provider's small-tenant figures.
- **Continuous validation does not reach backdoor poisoning.** The Exchange states that continuous validation is typically unsuitable against backdoor poisoning, because such attacks trigger on very specific inputs a test set would not normally contain and are often built to pass validation tests.[^aix-continuousvalidation] D6-HOLDOUT protects the reference corpus and grades nothing about its coverage, and no level here grades trigger discovery. A program presenting clean validation results against a protected test set has evidence about drift and permanent behavioural change, and no evidence about a trigger it never sampled. The Exchange's data-poisoning entry states the same limit from the attacker's side: a backdoor is built to pass regular testing, which is the intention behind choosing a trigger that normal inputs do not contain.[^aix-datapoison]
- **Detection versus structure for memory integrity.** The L4 list requires D6-DETECT beside the structural memory criteria. The Exchange states that separating legitimate memory updates from poisoning at scale remains difficult, and that structural access control and write authorization limit blast radius when detection is uncertain,[^aix-augintegrity] so whether a program holding the structural criteria and no detector should reach L4 is open.
