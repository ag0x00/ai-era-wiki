# Agentic AI Security CMM — D1 Governance & Accountability (Deep Dive)

Companion deep-dive to [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|the CMM]]'s D1 domain, written under the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026|recalibration method]]. D1 fixes who is accountable for agent behavior, with what authority, and on what auditable record. The recalibration makes one material change: **L5 no longer mandates a single certification.** The [[agentic-cmm-regulated-fi-stress-test|regulated-FI stress test]] flagged that a credit union's examiner gives [[aiuc-1|AIUC-1]] little weight and ISO/IEC 42001 more, and that L5 criteria pinned to just-released products outrun a regulated buyer's procurement cycle. The [[aiuc-1-critical-evaluation|AIUC-1 critical evaluation]] added the concentration argument, since one company sets the standard, arranges its audits and sells insurance priced against it, and found the mandate indefensible. D1-L5 now states a capability that several schemes satisfy: *current, independent, third-party assurance of the governance program*.

**The level boundaries and the cost model rest on one line of grounding.** They synthesize the wiki's own recalibration method against one representative-customer source, the [[agentic-cmm-regulated-fi-stress-test|regulated-FI stress test]], plus the [[aiuc-1-critical-evaluation|AIUC-1 evaluation]], rather than on independent sources that agree. They are wiki-internal calibration and not an externally ratified standard, and they firm up as later domains and crosswalks test them. Several L2 and L3 criteria take their content from outside sources, each cited at the criterion or in the commentary after its list: D1-RACI, the boundary record, the responsibility matrix and the technical-detail criteria from the OWASP AI Exchange, and the D1-HARNESS family from the harness vendor's settings documentation.

Part of the L2 criterion set has an external counterpart. The eight-step first iteration for AI governance published by the [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] names establishing a first policy and assigning responsibilities, which D1-POLICY and D1-RACI grade ([`/go/aiprogram/`](https://owaspai.org/go/aiprogram/)). Three further steps in that iteration sit at no level of this domain: identifying the applicable laws and regulations, running an AI-literacy program, and the proposal half of its inventory survey, which covers AI ideas and concerns as well as AI in use. D1-SHADOW's discovery reaches the agents in use and stops there. In the other direction, D1-SCHEME has no counterpart in the iteration. The level boundaries stay wiki-internal in every case.

## Threat coverage

D1 wraps the other eight domains in accountability, so its threats cut across them rather than falling into a single ASI category. The [[threat-taxonomy-reconciliation|Threat Taxonomy Reconciliation]] matrix resolves **Class 5 (jurisdictional adversary)** to this domain and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d9-operations|D9]] alone, because technical artifact controls do not mitigate a legal cutoff. The [[agentic-ai-threat-classes-2026|Agentic AI Threat Classes]] page carries the class detail and maps three of the defenses it names to this domain and one to D9:

- a multi-vendor model abstraction layer;
- jurisdiction tagging on data flows and model endpoints in the AI-BOM;
- contract resilience;
- a vendor-cutoff playbook, which that page maps to D9.

No D1 level grades any of the first three.

## Control landscape (dated)

Governance controls are documents, committees, and audits, so the landscape comprises assurance schemes and the platforms that hold the evidence. The D1-HARNESS family and D1-SHADOW are the exceptions, because the coding harness enforces its managed settings at runtime and discovery runs on inventory tooling.

| Layer | What ships today | Status (May 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Governance frameworks | [[nist-ai-rmf\|NIST AI RMF]] (Govern), [[iso-iec-42001\|ISO/IEC 42001]], [[eu-ai-act\|EU AI Act]] Art. 9 + Art. 17, [[csa-maestro\|CSA]] Agentic Trust Framework, [[cosai\|CoSAI]] AI Security Risk Governance workstream, [[google-saif\|SAIF]] Governance | Stable; no agentic Govern profile — see below |
| Governance control catalogues | [[owasp-ai-exchange\|OWASP AI Exchange]] G.U.A.R.D. steps with `AI PROGRAM`, `SEC PROGRAM`, `CHECK COMPLIANCE`, `SEC EDUCATE`, `DISCRETE` | Stable as of Aug 2026; supplies no organizational maturity criteria; grades some named standards' coverage of a control |
| Third-party assurance schemes | ISO/IEC 42001 certification (Schellman, BSI, SGS, A-LIGN); [[aiuc-1\|AIUC-1]] (AIUC accredits its auditors and issues every certificate); SOC 2 + AI controls as a partial proxy | ISO audits available now; AIUC-1 population small, with one fully accredited auditor and six provisional by September 2026[^aiuc-auditors] |
| Evidence and crosswalk platforms | GRC suites (ServiceNow, OneTrust, AuditBoard); Microsoft Purview / Compliance Manager; the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-crosswalk\|standards-crosswalk matrix]] | GA |
| Decision rights | [[decision-rights\|Decision Rights for AI Agents]] matrices | Pattern-level, not a product |
| Agent inventory and reaping | Agent / NHI inventory, such as the Microsoft Agent 365 registry; shadow-agent reaper ([[shadow-automation\|Shadow Automation]]) | Registry GA with Agent 365 on 2026-05-01, flagging a shadow agent for an administrator to act on; reaping pattern-level[^a365] |

**The framework layer is stable, and none of it grades a program.** NIST has not shipped an agentic Govern profile or named a date for one, and the AI RMF itself is under revision with no version or date announced ([NIST — AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)). The EU AI Act Art. 9 and Art. 17 anchors are duties and process rather than graded criteria, per [[standards-review-eu-ai-act-2026-Q2|the 2026-Q2 EU AI Act review]]. The SAIF anchors are category-level only and the CoSAI anchors carry no maturity criteria, per [[standards-review-saif-cosai-2026-Q2|the 2026-Q2 SAIF/CoSAI review]]. ISO 42001's Annex A controls that anchor D1 — A.2 Policies, A.3 Internal organization, A.5 Impact assessment — are organizational rather than technical, and [[standards-review-iso-42001-27090-2026-Q2|the 2026-Q2 ISO/IEC 42001 + 27090 review]] confirms them against the public structure only, citation-only and paywall-bounded. The Exchange states that boundary outside the paywall: 42001 extends the risk management system and focuses on governance, and does not discuss how to train models, how to do data lineage, continuous validation, versioning of AI models, project planning challenges, or how and when sensitive data is used in engineering ([[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]], [`/go/aiprogram/`](https://owaspai.org/go/aiprogram/)). The lifecycle half sits in ISO/IEC 5338 instead.

Outcome-based regulation states the result to be achieved, where control-focused regulation states the control to be operated. The Exchange gives the EU AI Act as the outcome-based example and ISO/IEC 27001 as the control-focused one, and advises extending the information security management system with assurance processes that show the risks were sufficiently mitigated ([[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]], [`/go/organize/`](https://owaspai.org/go/organize/)). D1-READINESS and D1-ASSURE read a program against a scheme's controls, and D1-ALLOCATE-RESIDUE records, threat by threat, the decision the outcome-based bar asks for. The Exchange states the sufficiency bar in a form a governance body can apply: an AI system is sufficiently secure when all identified risks can be treated, meaning transferred, avoided, or accepted, where acceptance sometimes follows directly and sometimes requires controls that bring the risk to an acceptable level ([`/go/riskanalysis/`](https://owaspai.org/go/riskanalysis/)). Its risk-treatment step lists the same options with mitigation named separately: mitigate, transfer, avoid, and accept. Its ISMS control `SEC PROGRAM` carries the same requirement into the security program, which must take the AI-specific assets and their threats onto its own books rather than treat AI as out of scope ([`/go/secprogram/`](https://owaspai.org/go/secprogram/)).

The Exchange grades standards and supplies no organizational maturity criteria. Each governance control lists the standards relevant to it, and against some of them records a verdict — "covers this control fully" or "covers this control minimally" — twelve such verdicts across the six controls in the governance group ([`/go/governancecontrols/`](https://owaspai.org/go/governancecontrols/)). Most of the standards each control lists carry no verdict at all. Each verdict judges how well a standard covers a control and says nothing about how mature a program is, so the Exchange supplies no criteria for program maturity. The nearest thing to level definitions the Exchange offers is a starting order: a three-step bare minimum — inventory current AI use and ideas, run risk analysis to identify threats, controls and who owns them, then continue with GUARD step 2 — sitting inside an eight-step first iteration ([`/go/aiprogram/`](https://owaspai.org/go/aiprogram/)). It sequences the work and grades neither its sufficiency nor its coverage.

The AIUC-1 certified population is small; [[aiuc-1-critical-evaluation|the evaluation]] carries the count and its provenance.

Platform-native evidence layers matter for single-stack buyers. An all-Microsoft enterprise can keep two of the three L4 records in Purview Compliance Manager, which comes with Microsoft 365 licensing, without a separate GRC purchase. Its templates map controls to regulations, and an organization can add custom templates, so Compliance Manager can hold the crosswalk D1-CROSSWALK reads. An assessment against its ISO/IEC 42001 template tracks each control's improvement actions and their status, and serves D1-READINESS where an independent assessor runs it. Its compliance score measures completed improvement actions, so the incidents, escalations and open findings D1-METRICS reads come from the organization's incident and risk records.[^cm-overview] Compliance Manager's four AI regulation templates are premium: an E5 tenant can use any three premium templates free, and each further template is a licence bought for one year.[^cm-templates] The licensing delta on the evidence layer therefore stays near zero for an E5 incumbent with a free template slot. The [[microsoft-zt4ai|Microsoft ZT4AI]] Governance pillar (verify explicitly) grounds this layer in named controls — the [[microsoft-rai|Responsible AI Standard]] and the Purview Compliance Manager AI templates — crosswalked to D1 in [[standards-review-microsoft-zt4ai-2026-Q2|the 2026-Q2 ZT4AI review]]. The RAI Standard states responsible-AI **goals** and specifies no technical control, and [[standards-review-microsoft-rai-agent-365-2026-Q2|the 2026-Q2 RAI / Agent 365 review]] separates those goals from the ZT4AI control catalogue. The review's matrix maps six Accountability and Transparency goals to D1 and places human oversight and control in D3 and D9:

- impact assessment;
- oversight of significant adverse impacts;
- fitness for purpose;
- data governance and management;
- system intelligibility for decision making;
- communication to stakeholders.

Cyera Agent Guardian is a vendor example of the agent-inventory row above. By Cyera's account, the product's Discover phase builds a live inventory of agents across cloud, SaaS, endpoint, and Shadow AI, and its Validate phase produces audit-ready regulatory evidence of the kind the platforms in the evidence and crosswalk row hold ([[cyera-agent-guardian-release|Cyera Agent Guardian Release]]). The release names no reaper and no decommission action, so reaping stays pattern-level.

## Capability-decoupled levels

Stated as capabilities per [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026|rule 1]]; a control counts when it operates in production per rule 2. A control implemented through a product in the organization's approved-vendor pipeline with a documented production date satisfies its criterion on that basis alone, and the production date is the date the control entered the organization's production. A planned date satisfies none, and neither does a product the organization does not yet run in production, whatever the vendor has announced.

- **L1 — Initial.** Nobody is accountable for AI governance, an approved policy, tier scheme or RACI is missing, or the deployment lacks a register entry, a current accountable person or a tier scored under the scheme's rule.
- **L2 — Developing.** The organization names the role accountable for AI governance and keeps an approved AI policy, risk-tier scheme and RACI, and each deployment carries a register entry with an accountable owner and a risk tier.
- **L3 — Defined.** A cross-functional risk body meets on a fixed cadence and gates each deployment by tier, discovery finds unregistered agents, and a review governs technical publication. Each deployment records its agents' decision rights and data handling, allocates its threats and their residue and classifies its technical details, and a coding deployment runs its harness under locked, reviewed managed settings.
- **L4 — Managed.** The board receives the quarter's AI risk metrics, the organization maintains its standards crosswalk, and a recent readiness assessment against a recognized scheme covers the deployment.
- **L5 — Optimizing.** Current, independent third-party assurance covers the deployment, the board attests the risk metrics each quarter, the risk body shows a year of decisions, and the crosswalk carries a revision each quarter.
- **L5+ — Leading Edge.** The organization contributes to a governance or standards body and publishes a governance artifact of its own.

D1-ASSURE asks for independent assurance that the governance program does what it claims, on any of three routes, so meeting it depends on no one vendor's product or audit cadence.

[[owasp-state-of-agentic-ai-security-governance|OWASP's State of Agentic AI Security and Governance]] supplies a separate governance-maturity scale, Levels 0–4, from unaware and ad hoc through experimentation without guardrails, policy-defined human-in-the-loop and integrated continuous oversight to adaptive self-regulation. Its upper levels call for runtime capabilities, such as real-time monitoring, kill switches, governance as code and cryptographic identity, that the CMM grades in D2, D3 and D7, so the two scales run alongside each other rather than level for level. Its headline finding frames D1: most organizations are deploying agents faster than they can govern them, and more budget for the programs they already run will not close that gap. The report pairs the governance levels with an Adoption Tier (AT0–AT8) so required governance scales with what is deployed, the right-sizing logic the deployment-shape table below applies.

## Assessor detail per level

L1 describes where a deployment starts and carries no criterion, so a deployment that does not meet every L2 criterion scores L1. It scores 0 under the measurement protocol's rubric only where no evidence of the L1 baseline exists, meaning the assessment holds no record and no interview answer about the domain for that deployment. The levels from L2 to L5+ each carry a criteria list below, each list stating what its own level adds.

Grading is cumulative: Level N requires every Level N–1 control plus the new criteria at Level N ([[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|the CMM]]), so a level is met only where every level below it is met.

Each criterion takes one of four verdicts. **Met** and **not met** are read from the evidence the criterion names, whoever operates the control. The assessment records the evidence's assurance class beside the verdict and names the artifact behind it, its issuer and its date, per [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|the measurement protocol]]:

- **Tested**: the customer or the assessor exercised the control and recorded what it did.
- **Inspected**: vendor tooling the customer can reach, or a record the organization keeps, such as an inventory or a plan, shows the control's state in the deployment.
- **Attested**: the vendor states the control in a document the customer holds, and the document's own scope names the control.

**Not applicable** is recorded where the deployment holds no instance of what the criterion governs, and the reduced scope is recorded as an intentional trade-off in the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-dependency-rules|effective-score]] strategic-rationale field. **Unanswerable** is recorded where the instance exists, the customer can run no test and keeps no record of the control's state, and the vendor supplies neither inspectable output nor an attestation that names the control. An unanswerable criterion never counts as met: its level stays open, and every level above it stays open under cumulative grading. The assessment names what would close it. A criterion that can be not applicable states that condition alongside the criterion. Each level's list below holds criteria only, and a paragraph after a list states no criterion: it carries maturity, market or provenance commentary, or the boundary between this domain and another.

**D1 grades one deployment at a time, and the organization once.** A deployment is the unit the assessment scope names: one agent application, or one agent platform a vendor provides together with the agents the organization runs on it. Evidence of a control counts only for the deployments the control covers. No active dependency rule caps D1 or reads its score, and an assessment grades D1 on the scope it graded [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]] on, so each deployment's matrix carries its D1 result beside its other domains. Five terms fix what the criteria range over:

- **An agent** is one deployed configuration: its code, instructions, tools and grants as the deployment runs them. Replicas of one configuration are one agent, and two configurations are two agents even where they share code. The deployment's inventory is the one D2-INVENTORY grades, whether or not it meets that criterion. A vendor-built agent the organization enables for its users, such as one of an in-suite assistant's own agents, is an agent of the deployment that provides it.
- **The agents in scope** are those the deployment runs in production, per rule 2. An agent outside production enters scope once it holds a production credential or reaches production data. Evidence from a pilot outside scope counts for no criterion, and the gap report can cite it as the planned route to a level. A deployment with no agent in scope, such as a pilot that holds no production credential and reaches no production data, takes no D1 result, and the assessment records it as outside scope.
- **The organization's estate** is every agent its people or systems run with access to its data or systems, in any deployment, registered or not and in production or not. D1-SHADOW ranges over the estate.
- **An agent type** is a group of agents that one boundary record governs, such as the agents one standard covers or the agents of one deployment. Every agent in scope belongs to at least one type.
- **An action** is one operation a call carries out on its target, such as a read, a write, a deletion or a send, as [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]] defines it.

**Unit tags.** Each criterion carries a unit tag after its name, organization or deployment. An organization criterion grades an artifact or a practice the organization keeps for all its AI systems at once: the assessment grades it once and reports its verdict once, in a table of the organization criteria beside the deployment matrices, and no deployment's row repeats it. A deployment criterion is graded for each deployment on the evidence that covers it, and its verdict is reported in that deployment's row. A deployment's cumulative grading reads each organization criterion's verdict at the level the criterion carries, so a deployment reaches a level only where every organization criterion at that level and below is met. An organization criterion not met or unanswerable at L3 therefore holds every deployment at L2, whatever the deployment's own criteria show, and each deployment's row names that criterion as the reason.

A criterion that names every agent, threat, party, finding or change is met only where it holds for each one. No percentage threshold applies, because the deployment's records enumerate its agents, its components and its suppliers, and one agent outside the rule is the failure D1 grades. An agent whose configuration the organization cannot produce, such as a workstation that has stopped reporting to device management, does not meet a criterion that ranges over it. The deployment's verdict is the weakest among its agents: not met where any agent fails the criterion, then unanswerable where any agent's verdict is unanswerable, and not applicable only where the criterion is not applicable to every agent. The assessment names the scope it graded, and where a narrower scope would change an agent's result, such as one repository of a shared CI workflow, it reports that agent beside the deployment's result. A record whose scope statement names part of a deployment, such as a risk assessment or a readiness assessment scoped to some of its agents or hosts, counts for the part it names. D1-READINESS and D1-ASSURE take the weakest verdict among the deployment's agents, so a scope that leaves an agent out fails them. D1-REGISTER-TIER reads the tier of the deployment as a whole, so a record that scores the rule's inputs for the deployment the register entry covers meets it, and the assessment reports the part the record's scope leaves out beside the deployment's result.

**Grading words.** The criteria use these terms in one sense each:

- **Approved**, or signed: decided on the record, with a date, by the body or the person the organization's rules give the decision, such as in a minute, on a signed form or by an approval stamp on the document. A table approved with the document that carries it, such as a policy's appendix, is approved with it. A body the gate rule places above an approver is one the rule names as the approver for a higher tier, or one to which the rule has that approver report its approvals.
- **Accountable for AI governance**: accountable for the organization's AI systems as a whole under its AI policy, in whatever words the naming document uses, such as "the executive accountable for AI risk" in the AI policy. A role accountable for one responsibility inside the program, such as validating models, approving the data an AI system uses or securing AI systems, is not accountable for AI governance.
- **The AI register**: the record the organization keeps of its AI systems under its AI policy, one entry for each system or deployment, whatever tool holds it, such as a model-risk inventory the policy extends to AI. An entry covers each agent it names and each agent of a deployment, platform or class it names, so one entry can cover a platform's agents, an in-suite assistant's own agents or two deployments.
- **Risk tier**: the tier the risk-tier scheme assigns an AI system as a whole, which sets its route through the deployment gate. D3 grades a different tier, the one a decision point returns for each action.
- **Member**: a person the risk body's charter names as a member. A person who attends only at the chair's invitation holds no seat.
- **Production**, for a deployment: the deployment entered production on the date its first agent entered scope, by holding a production credential or reaching production data. A production credential is one that reaches a system the organization runs in production, such as a code host's token over the organization's repositories or a person's own account on them, and production data is data such a system holds.
- **Condition**: a requirement an approval attaches to itself, such as a finding to close or a control to add, with the date by which it must be met where the approval states one. An approval that names a standard the deployment runs under sets no condition by naming it; the criteria that read the standard's controls grade them. A standard named inside a condition, such as the policy under which a finding may be accepted, is part of that condition.
- **Shadow agent**: an agent in the organization's estate that has no entry in the AI register, a third-party AI application acting with a user's delegated access to the organization's data or systems included. An agent whose owner has left is an orphan while its register entry stands, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d9-operations|D9]]'s D9-REAP, at D9 L3, acts on it.
- **Threat identification**: the analysis that selects the threats applying to the deployment, such as a threat model or the risk table of a risk assessment.
- **Component**: a part of the deployment the Exchange's responsibility step names: its model, a model extension, its application or its infrastructure ([`/go/riskanalysis/`](https://owaspai.org/go/riskanalysis/)). A model extension is a part the model can call, such as a tool, a connector or an agent.
- **Supplying party**: a party that supplies or hosts a component under an agreement the organization holds or terms it accepted, or an internal department or team that supplies data, a model or a fine-tuning artifact. A vendor's own supplier is a supplying party only where the organization holds an agreement with it.
- **Residue** and **disposition**: the residue is the risk a threat leaves after the controls the matrix records, and its disposition is the decision taken on it: accepted, mitigated by the organization's own control, or avoided.
- **Technical detail**: the model type and model implementation a deployment runs, and the technical content of material about the deployment that the organization publishes or approves for another party to publish, such as a vendor's customer case study.
- **Harness-configurable coding agent**: a coding agent whose harness loads settings, permission rules, hooks, MCP server definitions, subagents, skills or instruction files from files an administrator, a project or a user can change. Those files together are its **harness configuration tree**, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d4-runtime-guardrails|D4]]'s "agent's configuration" is the part of the tree the harness reads to decide what the agent may do or to start processes for it.
- **Managed settings**: the settings the organization's device or runner management delivers to the harness's managed location, which ranks above every other scope. A **local scope** is a user, project or local settings file, and a **managed-only lock** is a managed setting that makes the harness ignore one class of entries, such as permission rules or hooks, from every local scope. A device or runner that hosts a harness-configurable coding agent and carries no managed settings holds no managed list key and no managed file, so its agents fail D1-HARNESS-EXTEND and D1-HARNESS-RESTORE, and the not-applicable conditions of those two criteria do not reach it.
- **Board**: the board of directors or a committee of it.
- **Quarter**: a calendar quarter. A criterion that reads the last four quarters reads the four most recent complete quarters before the assessment starts.
- **Attest**: state on the record, over a named director's or a board committee's authority, that a report's figures are complete and accurate. A minute that records a report as received or noted attests nothing.
- **Recognized assurance scheme**: ISO/IEC 42001, AIUC-1, or a documented internal equivalent crosswalk-mapped to a recognized framework, such as the NIST AI RMF.
- **Independent**: performed by a party outside the reporting line of the governance program it reviews, which is a third party or, for a readiness assessment, the organization's internal audit function.
- **Qualified third party**: a third party that performs assurance engagements under an accreditation or a professional assurance standard, such as an accredited certification body or an audit firm.
- **Third-party assurance**: assurance on one of three routes, counted from the documented date it took effect, so assurance still in procurement counts for nothing:
    - an [[iso-iec-42001|ISO/IEC 42001]] certificate under the surveillance its certification body runs;
    - an [[aiuc-1|AIUC-1]] certificate within its one-year validity, with each quarterly red-teaming round completed;[^aiuc-recert]
    - a documented internal-equivalent attestation, independently reviewed by a qualified third party and crosswalk-mapped to a recognized framework.
- **Tested**: a test counts where it ran on a production device or runner, or on another carrying the managed settings and harness version production runs, and its record names the device and the versions. A demonstration the organization runs and the assessor observes is a test.

**Names and precedence.** Each criterion carries a stable name, D1 and a mnemonic for what the criterion requires, and the name leads the criterion in bold. The level and the unit tag are attributes recorded beside the name, so a criterion that changes level keeps its name. Members of a family share its first mnemonic, as D1-HARNESS and D1-HARNESS-LOCK do, and each member is a separate criterion because a deployment, or the organization, can hold one without the other and an assessor collects different evidence for each. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|The CMM]]'s D1 levels restate each criterion under the same name, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|the measurement protocol]]'s D1 questions and artifacts cite each one by that name, in plain text. A level's statement above summarizes its list, and where the two read differently the list governs.

**Vendor-supplied parts.** A vendor supplies part of a deployment where the vendor's service holds a component the organization runs no instance of, as with an in-suite assistant's model and its own agents, or a maker platform's hosting. The criteria stand for those parts, because the organization governs what it enables whoever builds it: vendor supply is no ground for a not-applicable verdict. D1-BOUNDARY reads each vendor-built agent the organization enables, and one record can govern them as one type, as an enablement policy admits them as one class. D1-ALLOCATE allocates each threat to the vendor, as the supplying party, for the components the vendor supplies. D1-DETAIL needs no entry for a detail the organization does not hold, such as a model type the vendor does not disclose. A certificate a vendor holds for its own service assures the vendor's program, and D1-READINESS and D1-ASSURE read the organization's.

**Not applicable across a level.** A level at which every criterion is not applicable for the deployment is not reached, and neither is any level above it, because a not-applicable verdict records that the deployment holds nothing for a criterion to govern and records no control. D1-REGISTER reads every deployment in scope, so every deployment in scope holds an instance at L2 and takes a D1 result.

**Conditions the rubric adds.** [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-measurement-protocol|The measurement protocol]]'s rubric adds two conditions to score 3 that no domain's criteria state: operational ID tagging of the domain's findings, and the live observation it requires, a trace, a policy decision and, where D3-APPROVE-GATE applies, a gate fire. A deployment that meets every D1 L3 criterion without them scores 2 under the rubric, and the assessment records which condition held it there.

### L2 detail

- **D1-ACCOUNTABLE.** *Organization.* A document the organization approved, such as its AI policy or a charter, names one role as accountable for the organization's AI governance, and a person holds that role on the assessment date. A document that names a committee, or several roles jointly, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the document naming the role, with the personnel record of the person who holds it.
- **D1-POLICY.** *Organization.* The organization approved an AI policy that applies to every AI system it builds, buys or uses, its agents included, and publishes it where every person it applies to can read it. A policy scoped to one class of use, such as employees' use of public AI tools, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the policy with its approval and its scope statement, and the place it is published.
- **D1-SCHEME.** *Organization.* An approved risk-tier scheme assigns each AI system, its agents included, one of a set of tiers by a stated rule, and the rule reads at least how far the system acts without a person reviewing its output. A scheme that leaves agents out of its scope, or assigns tiers by no stated rule, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the scheme with its rule, and the policy or standard that applies it to AI systems.
- **D1-RACI.** *Organization.* An approved RACI assigns one accountable party to each of the three responsibilities the [[owasp-ai-exchange|OWASP AI Exchange]] gives as examples, model accountability, data accountability and risk governance ([`/go/aiprogram/`](https://owaspai.org/go/aiprogram/)). A row over rating or validating a model assigns the first, a row over approving the data an AI system uses the second, and a row over approving an AI system for production or accepting its residual risk the third. A row can name its accountable party by risk tier, one party for each tier. *Evidence:* the RACI with its approval record, each responsibility matched to its row.
- **D1-REGISTER.** *Deployment.* The AI register holds an entry for the deployment that covers each of its agents and names the person accountable for the deployment, and the organization's personnel record shows that person as current. *Evidence:* the register entry, with the personnel record of the person it names.
- **D1-REGISTER-TIER.** *Deployment.* The deployment's register entry records the risk tier the scheme's rule assigns it, with the inputs the rule reads as scored for the deployment. A tier set without those inputs, such as a default applied to a platform, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the entry's tier, with the record that scored the rule's inputs, such as the deployment's risk assessment.

[[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d2-identity|D2]]'s D2-INVENTORY records each agent and every non-human identity it holds, and D2-OWNER, at D2 L3, names a current human owner for each agent and each identity. D1-REGISTER records the deployment in the governance register with the person accountable for it, so one register evidences both where it records each agent and its identities, and a platform entry whose agents lack owners of their own can meet D1-REGISTER and fail D2-OWNER. The risk tier sets a deployment's route through the gate, and D3-TIER records a tier for each action, so a deployment needs both records and neither stands in for the other. D1-REGISTER lists no component: [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d8-supply-chain|D8]]'s D8-INVENTORY lists each component a deployment's agents run, meaning each model with the service that hosts it, the harness or platform, each ability, each AI framework library and each acquired dataset.

### L3 detail

- **D1-BODY.** *Organization.* A standing AI risk body, chartered by the organization, counts among its members a person from each of four functions: security, legal, privacy and engineering, meaning a function that builds or runs one of the organization's AI systems. A function represented only by a person who attends at the chair's invitation holds no seat, so the body fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the charter's membership, each member matched to a function.
- **D1-BODY-CADENCE.** *Organization.* The body meets on a cadence its charter fixes, and its minutes record a meeting in each interval of that cadence over the twelve months before the assessment starts, or since its charter where that date is more recent. *Evidence:* the charter's cadence, with the dates of the minutes over the period.
- **D1-GATE-RULE.** *Organization.* An approved rule names, for each risk tier, the approver of a deployment entering production, the risk body itself for the highest tier, and requires that approval before the deployment enters production. *Evidence:* the rule, with the approver it names for each tier.
- **D1-GATE.** *Deployment.* The approver the gate rule names for the deployment's risk tier, or a body the rule places above that approver, approved the deployment for production before it entered production. An approval dated after the deployment entered production, or given by an approver the rule does not name for the tier, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the approval record with its approver and its date, set against the deployment's tier and the date it entered production.
- **D1-GATE-CONDITION.** *Deployment.* Each condition the approval set was met by the date the condition states, or before production where it states none, or the approver recorded a decision to change the condition before that date. Not applicable where the approval set no condition. *Evidence:* each condition of the approval, with the record that met it or the approver's decision.
- **D1-SHADOW.** *Organization.* Discovery runs on a schedule the organization sets and covers each place in the estate where an agent can run: the organization's endpoints, its software-as-a-service tenants, its cloud accounts and its code hosts. Each run is compared with the AI register, and each agent the register lacks is recorded as a shadow agent. A place no discovery source covers, or a comparison made once with no schedule, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the discovery sources with their schedule and coverage, set against the organization's endpoints, tenants, cloud accounts and code hosts, and the latest comparison with the register.
- **D1-SHADOW-REAP.** *Organization.* The organization sets a time within which each shadow agent is registered or removed, and each shadow agent discovery recorded in the twelve months before the assessment starts was registered or removed within that time. A finding past its time, or a class of finding with no time set, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the rule that sets the time, and the findings of the period with the date each was registered or removed.
- **D1-BOUNDARY.** *Deployment.* A dated record the organization keeps for each agent type states which of its agents' actions they take on their own, which wait for a human approval and whose, and which they never take, and every agent of the deployment falls under such a record. A record can state a default for the actions it does not list. A configuration that enforces the boundaries, such as an allowlist, is technical policy and serves as no boundary record. Not applicable where no agent of the deployment holds a tool. *Evidence:* the boundary record for each agent type, set against the deployment's agents.
- **D1-BOUNDARY-DATA.** *Deployment.* The boundary record states, for each agent type of the deployment, the classes of the organization's information classification its agents may read, and for each class where they may store it, with whom they may share it and when it may leave the organization. A general handling standard that names no agent type states no agent's data handling and fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the data-handling statement for each agent type, set against the classes its agents read.
- **D1-ALLOCATE.** *Deployment.* A responsibility matrix allocates each threat the deployment's threat identification selects to the party that addresses it, the organization or a supplying party, and it names every supplying party of the deployment. The threat identification covers each component the deployment runs on the assessment date. A supplying party the matrix omits, or a component the identification leaves out, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the matrix and the threat identification, set against the deployment's components and the agreements that make each supplying party one.
- **D1-ALLOCATE-RESIDUE.** *Deployment.* The residue of each threat the matrix allocates carries a recorded disposition, made by the party the organization's rules give that decision. A residual rating with no disposition, or an acceptance made by a party the rules do not give the decision, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the disposition of each residue, with the party who decided it and the rule that gives the decision.
- **D1-DETAIL.** *Deployment.* The organization's information-security asset inventory holds an entry, with a class under its classification scheme, for each technical detail of the deployment it holds: the record of the model type and the model implementation, and each item it has published, or approved for another party to publish, about the deployment. *Evidence:* the inventory entries with their classes, set against the deployment's design records and publications.
- **D1-DETAIL-RULE.** *Organization.* The organization's rule for external publication requires each item that describes an AI system's technical detail to pass, before publication, a review whose record states what the item withholds and what it discloses, set against the disclosure the Exchange's `AI TRANSPARENCY` control asks for. A publication rule that reviews security controls and names no AI system fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the publication rule, with the review it requires.
- **D1-DETAIL-REVIEW.** *Deployment.* Each item the organization published, or approved for another party to publish, about the deployment's technical detail passed a review before publication, and the review's record states what the item withholds and what it discloses, set against the disclosure `AI TRANSPARENCY` asks for. A review that records only whether an item describes security controls fails the criterion. Not applicable where the organization has published, and approved for publication, nothing about the deployment. *Evidence:* the review record of each item.
- **D1-HARNESS.** *Deployment.* Each harness-configurable coding agent of the deployment runs under managed settings on each device or runner that hosts it, and no local scope overrides a managed setting. An agent on a device or runner that carries no managed settings fails the criterion. Not applicable where the deployment runs no harness-configurable coding agent. *Evidence:* the settings as the harness resolved them on an enrolled device or runner, with the fleet record showing the managed source on each device or runner.
- **D1-HARNESS-LOCK.** *Deployment.* The managed settings set each managed-only lock the harness offers over permission rules, hooks, MCP servers, and plugins or skills from user or project sources. A lock the harness offers and the settings leave unset fails the criterion. Not applicable where the deployment runs no harness-configurable coding agent, or where the harness offers no managed-only lock. *Evidence:* the lock values in the resolved managed settings, set against the locks the harness documents.
- **D1-HARNESS-EXTEND.** *Deployment.* A record the organization keeps names each list key in the managed settings that a local scope can still extend, with the entries each local scope may add. A key the harness merges from a local scope and the record omits fails the criterion. Not applicable where the deployment runs no harness-configurable coding agent, or where no local scope can extend a managed list key. *Evidence:* the record, set against the harness's documentation of the keys that merge across scopes.
- **D1-HARNESS-RESTORE.** *Deployment.* The managed settings file on each device or runner returns to the published version whenever it differs: the fleet's management restores it at an interval the organization states, or each run starts from an image that carries it. Not applicable where the deployment runs no harness-configurable coding agent, or where no person or process on the device or runner can change the managed file. *Evidence:* the restore mechanism's configuration, with its log of a restore or a test of it.
- **D1-HARNESS-REVIEW.** *Deployment.* Each change to the deployment's harness configuration tree passes a documented review before it takes effect, whether it changes the managed settings or a hook, an MCP server definition, a subagent, a skill or an instruction file the harness loads. A change a developer makes on their own device with no review, or a project settings change that takes effect in the run of the pull request that carries it, fails the criterion. Not applicable where the deployment runs no harness-configurable coding agent. *Evidence:* the review records for a sample of changes drawn from the tree's change history.

The body, its cadence and the gate rule are the organization's, and the gate and its conditions are each deployment's, because one body gates many deployments and each approval is a record of its own. An approval from a delegate counts where the gate rule names that delegate for the tier, and each condition counts at the date it states, so an approval whose condition lapsed with no decision on record leaves D1-GATE met and D1-GATE-CONDITION not met. D3-PROMOTE, at D3 L4, moves an agent between autonomy stages on criteria the organization documents, which can include a governance sign-off, and D1-GATE admits the deployment to production at its tier.

The Exchange asks that an AI deployment's ethical and operational boundaries, among them oversight tiers, prohibited autonomous actions and data-handling rules, be documented in governance and reflected in technical policy ([`/go/aiprogram/`](https://owaspai.org/go/aiprogram/)). D1-BOUNDARY records the first two in one statement per action: the actions an agent takes only after an approval, or never, are its prohibited autonomous actions, and the three decision rights set each action's oversight. D1-BOUNDARY and D1-BOUNDARY-DATA grade the governance half, and [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d3-control-least-agency|D3]] grades the technical half: D3-RIGHTS applies a decision-rights matrix at call time with its approver, justification and time bound, and D3-TIER-ENFORCE returns each action's recorded tier, so one matrix can evidence D1-BOUNDARY and the documented half of D3-RIGHTS. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d6-data-rag|D6]]'s D6-CLASSIFY asks for a record that assigns a class to each data source an agent reads, and D1-BOUNDARY-DATA records which classes the agents of a type may handle and where each may go.

D1-SHADOW searches the organization's estate for agents the AI register lacks, and D2-DISCOVER, at D2 L5, searches the platforms a deployment's agents run on for agents that the registry the deploy pipeline maintains lacks. D1-SHADOW-REAP ends each finding in a registration or a removal, D2-KILL is the operation that stops one agent, and D9-REAP, at D9 L3, acts on a registered agent whose owner has left. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d7-observability|D7]]'s D7-POSTURE inventories the AI assets in the accounts that host a deployment, so one AI inventory can feed D7-POSTURE and serve as a discovery source. Endpoints count among the places discovery covers, because a developer installs a desktop or coding agent on a workstation, and no suite console lists it.

The allocation follows the Exchange's responsibility step: for each selected threat the organization determines who addresses it, and where a component is hosted it shares the responsibility with the hosting provider through a responsibility matrix ([`/go/riskanalysis/`](https://owaspai.org/go/riskanalysis/)). The Exchange places the internal half inside the same control: the supply chain can include the organization's own departments, since data and models may come from different departments and sources, which puts data provenance inside supply-chain management.[^aix-supplychainmanage] The three dispositions are the Exchange's options where a supplier is not open about its mitigation, and its residual-risk step asks that the risk left after the controls be accepted. The Exchange also directs that the analysis repeat regularly and when changes warrant it, which D1-ALLOCATE reads as coverage of each component the deployment runs on the assessment date. D8-SUPPLIER asks for an assessment of each supplier against the Exchange's seven supplier evaluation items, and one supplier record can feed that assessment and D1-ALLOCATE. D8-INVENTORY and D8-AIBOM list what a deployment is built from, so either can supply the component list against which D1-ALLOCATE checks the threat identification's coverage.

The technical-detail criteria read the Exchange's `DISCRETE`, which minimizes the technical details that could help an attacker by carrying them as an asset in information security management, weighed against the openness `AI TRANSPARENCY` may require ([`/go/discrete/`](https://owaspai.org/go/discrete/)). The Exchange supplies a direction for that trade-off and no threshold, so D1-DETAIL-REVIEW grades the recorded decision and sets no line. The rule is the organization's and each review the deployment's, because one rule governs every publication and each item passes its own review. D8-DOCS inventories model and development documentation, the documentation entry on the Exchange's twelve-item AI asset list that [[security-controls-for-ai-stacks|the controls thesis]] records in full, and D1-DETAIL carries the two entries no D8 criterion holds: the record of the model type and implementation, and each published item. D9-NOTICE and D9-NOTICE-PROPERTIES, at D9 L3, grade the user notice and the disclosure's coverage record, so D1-DETAIL-REVIEW's record of what was withheld and D9's record of what was published read `AI TRANSPARENCY`'s property list from opposite sides.

List keys merge entries from every scope, so a local file can extend a managed permission or hook list that it cannot override, and D1-HARNESS-LOCK and D1-HARNESS-EXTEND stand beside D1-HARNESS for that reason ([Claude Code settings](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/settings), [managed settings](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/managed-settings)). D3-POLICY-LOCK grades the harness's permission rules as the policy of a decision point that neither the agent nor its principal can widen, and D1-HARNESS-LOCK grades the managed-only locks over permission rules, hooks, MCP servers and customization, so a harness whose hooks a local file can add can meet D3-POLICY-LOCK and fail D1-HARNESS-LOCK. D1-HARNESS-REVIEW reviews the changes people make to the tree, D3-POLICY-REVIEW each release of a decision point's policy, and D4-SANDBOX-CONFINE bars the code an agent runs from changing the agent's configuration. Under D6-EXTEND a named person reviews each extension that adds a source to a corpus before its first use, so one review record of an MCP server that indexes content can evidence D6-EXTEND and D1-HARNESS-REVIEW.

### L4 detail

- **D1-METRICS.** *Organization.* The board received a report on the organization's AI systems for the most recent complete quarter before the assessment starts, and the report gives, for that quarter, the incidents involving them, the decisions escalated to the risk body, and the open findings against them grouped by the standards-anchor identifiers of the CMM's [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026#Global evidence rule|global evidence rule]], such as `ASI##` and [[owasp-aivss|AIVSS]] scores. A report that counts systems and assessments alone fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the report, with the minutes recording its receipt.
- **D1-CROSSWALK.** *Organization.* The organization keeps a crosswalk that maps each AI policy and standard it has in force to the frameworks and regulations it names, and the crosswalk's latest revision falls within the review cadence the organization sets for it. A policy or standard in force with no row fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the crosswalk's latest revision with its review rule, set against the AI policies and standards in force.
- **D1-READINESS.** *Deployment.* The deployment lies within the scope of a readiness assessment against a recognized assurance scheme, completed by an independent assessor in the twelve months before the assessment starts, and the report lists each gap it found against a clause or a control of the scheme. An assessment whose scope omits the deployment, or a self-assessment by the program's own owners, fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the readiness report with its scheme, assessor, date, scope and gap list.

D1-METRICS asks for three measures that need no detection program, and a report that also counts the alerts D7-DRIFT raises meets it as well. D1-CROSSWALK grades the organization's own map from its AI policies and standards to the requirements it names, and the CMM's [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-crosswalk|standards-crosswalk matrix]], which maps this model's domains to standards, can seed it. D1-READINESS reads twelve months because both named schemes run on a one-year clock: ISO/IEC 42001 certification holds an annual surveillance audit, as [[aiuc-1-critical-evaluation|the AIUC-1 evaluation]] sets out, and an AIUC-1 certificate is valid for a year.[^aiuc-recert] The internal audit function counts as independent for a readiness assessment, and D1-ASSURE needs a third party.

### L5 detail

- **D1-ASSURE.** *Deployment.* The deployment lies within the scope of third-party assurance of the organization's AI governance program that is current on the assessment date. *Evidence:* the certificate or the reviewed attestation, with its scope, its route and the record that keeps it current.
- **D1-METRICS-ATTEST.** *Organization.* The board attested the report D1-METRICS reads for each of the last four quarters. A report the minutes record only as received or noted fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the attestation for each of the four quarters.
- **D1-BODY-HISTORY.** *Organization.* The risk body's minutes span at least the twelve months before the assessment starts, from a minuted meeting held twelve months or more before it starts, and record each decision the body took in that period. A body whose first minuted meeting is more recent fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the minutes over the period, with each decision they record.
- **D1-CROSSWALK-REFRESH.** *Organization.* The crosswalk carries a revision dated in each of the last four quarters. *Evidence:* the crosswalk's revision history over the four quarters.

The prerequisite gate into L5, graded once for the program, accepts assurance current or scheduled against a recognized scheme for any domain scored L5, and D1-ASSURE requires it current and covering the deployment, so D1's own L5 asks more than the gate. ISO/IEC 42001 is the preferred route, because it is standards-body-governed, multi-auditor and regulator-recognized, and AIUC-1 is accepted with the concentration and freshness caveats [[aiuc-1-critical-evaluation|the evaluation]] documents. AIUC certifies the agents an organization names in scope and re-runs its red-teaming on them each quarter, so an AIUC-1 certificate's scope decides which deployments it covers.[^aiuc-recert]

### L5+ detail

- **D1-STANDARDS.** *Organization.* A named member of the organization contributed, in the twelve months before the assessment starts, to a governance or standards body's work on AI governance or AI risk management, through a pull request, an RFC or specification authorship, and membership alone falls short. *Evidence:* links to the contributions, with their dates.
- **D1-PUBLISH.** *Organization.* The organization published, in the twelve months before the assessment starts, a governance artifact of its own, such as its AI governance framework, a report on its AI governance or research on AI governance. A vendor's customer story about the organization fails the criterion. *Evidence:* the publication.

Each domain grades a contribution to its own subject: D2-STANDARDS to identity working groups, D8-STANDARDS to bill-of-materials and MCP registry-signing working groups, D9-STANDARDS to drift-detection patterns and bypass classes, and D1-STANDARDS to AI governance and risk management, so one piece of work counts in each domain whose subject it addresses. D9-PUBLISH grades published metrics on the operation of the organization's AI systems, and D1-PUBLISH a governance artifact, so one report that publishes both evidences both.

## Right-sizing by deployment shape

The realistic target per [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-recalibration-method-2026|rule 4]]:

| Deployment shape | Realistic D1 target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Internal RAG / support chatbot (no tools) | L2 → L3 | D1-REGISTER carries L2, and D1-GATE, D1-BOUNDARY-DATA and D1-ALLOCATE carry L3; D1-BOUNDARY is not applicable to a bot with no tool. D1-ASSURE is not warranted near-term |
| In-suite productivity assistant (Gemini for Workspace, Microsoft 365 Copilot) | L3 | D1-BOUNDARY reads the assistant and each vendor-built agent the tenant enables, and D1-DETAIL needs no entry for a model the vendor does not disclose |
| Desktop-agent productivity assistant ([[claude-cowork\|Claude Cowork]] class) | L3 | Connector permission categories, Always allow, Needs approval or Blocked, match D1-BOUNDARY's three decision rights, and D1-SHADOW's discovery reaches each endpoint |
| Data-science / coding copilot | L3 → L4 | The D1-HARNESS family carries L3, and L4 adds a readiness assessment whose scope takes in the coding deployment |
| MCP / skill provider serving others | L4 | Third-party exposure raises the accountability bar, and D1-READINESS's scope takes in the service provided |
| High-autonomy multi-agent mesh | L4 → selective L5 | D1-ASSURE's third-party assurance earns its cost where autonomy and blast radius are highest |

A sufficiently resourced program defaults to **L4 with selective L5** where deployment exposure justifies it, per [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-2026|the CMM]]'s default expectation; most enterprises reach the shape targets in the table above. A contained, low-agency design that legitimately needs less governance records the choice as an intentional trade-off in the [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-dependency-rules|effective-score]] strategic-rationale field.

### L3 evidence by deployment shape

- **In-suite productivity assistant.** D1-BOUNDARY reads the assistant as one agent, whatever the number of its users, and each vendor-built agent the tenant enables as an agent of the deployment. One record can govern those agents as one type, as the tenant's enablement policy admits them as one class.
- **Desktop-agent productivity assistant.** D1-SHADOW's discovery reaches each endpoint, because an unsanctioned install sits on the endpoint and no suite console lists it.

**Agentic coding adds a governance object, the harness configuration tree, and the D1-HARNESS family grades it.** D1 asks who is accountable for agent behavior on what auditable record, and for agentic coding that record includes the hooks, MCP manifests, subagents, skills and instruction files that compose runtime behavior from third-party parts. A developer can change that configuration in user, project or local settings unless managed policy locks it ([Claude Code settings](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/settings)). The family therefore asks for managed settings that no local scope overrides, the managed-only locks the harness offers for permission rules, hooks, MCP servers and user- or project-sourced customization, a record of each list key a local scope can still extend, a restore of the managed file, and a review of each change to the tree. Adoption outruns the inventory governance assumes. In the [[microsoft-cli-coding-agent-adoption-study|Microsoft CLI Coding Agent Adoption Study]], the strongest predictor of who tried GitHub Copilot CLI in a given week was whether the engineer's peers, especially the broader skip-level group, had already tried it. A tool that spreads through peers can reach a codebase before a security team enumerates it, so [[shadow-automation|Shadow Automation]] is the state to plan for. [[securing-agentic-coding|Securing Agentic Coding]] carries the controls for this shape. The D1-HARNESS family is the one part of D1 whose evidence that page supplies, and item 10 of [[cmm-known-limitations|CMM Known Limitations]] records the rest of the D1 levels as having no row there to cite. [[claude-code-control-sheet|Claude Code Control Sheet]] maps one harness's controls to the D1 criteria they evidence.

## Cost model

Governance is labor-heavy, and for an incumbent the licensing line stays near zero until the L5 certification audit.

Governance licensing differs by stack. A Microsoft E5 tenant keeps the crosswalk and the readiness assessment in Compliance Manager, inside entitlements it already owns while a free premium-template slot remains.[^cm-templates] Google's nearest instrument stops short of both. Compliance Manager in Google's Security Command Center audits cloud configuration against built-in frameworks and collects audit evidence only on the Premium and Enterprise tiers. Its framework reference lists no ISO/IEC 42001 or EU AI Act template, and its one NIST AI entry is a preview set of AI 600-1 privacy controls.[^gcp-cm] A Google Cloud or Google Workspace buyer therefore funds a GRC platform where the E5 buyer reads `~0`. Labor and certification fees are a constant on either stack.

| Level | Licensing | Operational labor | Run-rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| L2 | ~0 (policy authored in-house) | ~0.25–0.5 FTE to write policy, stand up the RACI and risk-tier scheme | — |
| L3 | ~0 | ~0.5–1 FTE recurring: risk-body cadence, decision-rights upkeep, shadow-agent reaping | — |
| L4 | GRC platform where used; ~0 on Compliance Manager for an E5 tenant with a free premium-template slot | board reporting + crosswalk upkeep; an independent readiness assessment every twelve months | — |
| L5 (ISO 42001 path) | certification audit + optional consulting | the dominant cost: internal hours to build and sustain the management system, plus annual surveillance | — |
| L5 (AIUC-1 path) | certification fee (not publicly listed) | heavier recurring labor than ISO's annual surveillance, because the certificate is re-tested on a quarterly cadence | — |

**The spend is governance labor and the certification-evidence treadmill.** A buyer choosing the AIUC-1 path inherits a quarterly re-test cycle; the ISO 42001 path runs on annual surveillance. Both recur, so price the recurring rhythm as well as the first audit.

## Customer critiques folded in

- *"L5 points at offerings too new for our vendor-risk process to have cleared."* Addressed: D1-ASSURE is scheme-neutral, and the production-maturity qualifier in the preamble to the level definitions applies. Assurance counts from the documented date it took effect, and a scheme still in procurement counts for nothing until then.
- *"A single-certifier mandate concentrates assurance in one company."* Raised by [[aiuc-1-critical-evaluation|the AIUC-1 critical evaluation]] rather than by the stress test. Addressed: AIUC-1 is demoted to one option among several, with ISO 42001 preferred.
- *"Cost is under-told."* Addressed: the cost model names labor and the evidence treadmill as the real spend, and marks licensing as near-zero for incumbents.
- *"Authority to certify is itself contested."* Acknowledged but out of scope: the control landscape above records the assurance schemes without ruling on their authority. An organization preparing for a US regulator maps D1 to its examiner's expectations via [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-crosswalk-us-fi|CMM: US Regulated-Finance Crosswalk (FFIEC and GLBA)]], which anchors D1 in the governance expectations of US banking and credit-union regulators.

## Open questions

- No official AIUC-1 certified-organization count is published; the figure in [[aiuc-1-critical-evaluation|the evaluation]] is reconstructed from press releases and should be re-checked each quarter.
- AIUC-1 pricing is not public, so the L5 AIUC-1 cost line is directional.
- The exact 2026 ISO/IEC 42001 certified population is not cleanly published.
- NIST has not shipped an agentic-specific Govern profile, and the EU AI Act leaves its control specifications to CEN/CENELEC harmonized standards still in development, per [[standards-review-eu-ai-act-2026-Q2|the 2026-Q2 EU AI Act review]], so D1's regulatory anchors will shift as both arrive.
- The disclosure bound is stated per property. [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-d9-operations|D9]]'s D9-NOTICE, at D9 L3, requires that each user be told an AI model is involved, and D9-NOTICE-PROPERTIES records each of the five properties `AI TRANSPARENCY` lists as covered in the published disclosure or omitted from it, so D1-DETAIL-REVIEW's record cannot silently drop a property. How much detail each property carries is unbounded: the Exchange supplies a direction and no threshold, and no level in this CMM states where the line falls.
- The measurement protocol collects an AI impact assessment and an AI-initiative inventory at Stage 1, and no D1 criterion grades either. ISO/IEC 42001's A.5 anchors impact assessment to D1 in [[agentic-ai-security-cmm-crosswalk|the crosswalk]], and the Exchange makes impact analysis an element of the AI program and a survey of AI in use and of AI ideas a step of its first governance iteration ([`/go/aiprogram/`](https://owaspai.org/go/aiprogram/)), so a criterion could grade each deployment's signed assessment and the organization's inventory of proposed uses. Whether D1 grades them is open.
- No D1 level grades the authorization or the scope of a testing programme, such as the rules-of-engagement form that names a test's target, environment, dates and techniques, although [[red-teaming-capability-framework|the red-teaming capability framework]] maps its first tier, governance and risk, to D1. The Exchange's testing guidance names defining objectives and scope as one step and gives no artifact for it ([`/go/testing/`](https://owaspai.org/go/testing/)), and D7-EVAL grades the evaluations themselves at D7 L4, so a test's authorization stays ungraded.
