# OpenAI Daybreak

**Sources:** [OpenAI — Daybreak](https://openai.com/daybreak/) · [Daybreak: Tools for securing every organization in the world](https://openai.com/index/daybreak-securing-the-world/) · [Expanding Daybreak as the Cyber Defense Window Narrows](https://openai.com/index/expanding-daybreak-as-the-cyber-defense-window-narrows/) · [AI for Cybersecurity Teams](https://openai.com/business/solutions/cybersecurity/)

OpenAI Daybreak is the programme through which [[openai|OpenAI]] puts its cyber-capable models in the hands of vetted defenders. OpenAI describes it as a governed cyber defense stack, built so that defenders reduce risk faster while access and action stay under human control.[^hub] The programme has four parts:[^hub]

- **Models in two access tiers**, Daybreak Blue and Daybreak Red.
- **Codex Security**, the application-security workflows that apply the models to code.
- **The Daybreak Defense Network**, partners that build the models into security products and managed services.
- **Patch the Planet**, an open-source remediation initiative founded with [[trail-of-bits|Trail of Bits]].

OpenAI's solutions page divides the work between the first two: the Daybreak models supply the cyber capability, and Codex Security supplies the context, tools, validation, workflow and review needed to apply it.[^solutions] That is the model-and-harness split [[frontier-ai-for-vuln-discovery|Frontier AI for Vulnerability Discovery]] argues from vendor and academic evidence, stated by a frontier lab about its own offering. OpenAI reports all of the programme's figures itself. Two posts carry most of them: the June 2026 announcement *Daybreak: Tools for securing every organization in the world*, and the August 2026 *Expanding Daybreak as the Cyber Defense Window Narrows*.

## Premise

OpenAI's June post states that frontier AI has moved the bottleneck in cyber defense from finding vulnerabilities to patching them, because defenders are now overwhelmed by the number of vulnerabilities found.[^june-inflection] A report protects no one by itself, the post continues: the value comes from validating the issue, understanding its impact, developing and testing a patch, coordinating disclosure and helping teams deploy the fix.[^june-inflection] Anthropic's one-month report, [[anthropic-glasswing-initial-update|Project Glasswing: Initial Update]], had named verification, disclosure and patching as the limit on 2026-05-22, a month earlier, and Patch the Planet and the Codex Security triage workflows both aim at the post-discovery stages that [[vulnops|VulnOps: Vulnerability Operations]] covers. The August post frames the programme as a race. OpenAI expects threat actors to use AI increasingly to attack at speed and scale, some of it fully autonomously, and its stated answer is to put frontier capability with trusted defenders before attackers deploy offensive AI at scale.[^aug]

## Codex Security in the programme

[[codex-security|Codex Security]] reads a team's code and its threat model, generating one where none exists, then identifies plausible vulnerabilities, checks reachability, gathers validation evidence and develops and verifies a targeted patch. Humans decide which findings to investigate, which changes to apply and what to share.[^june-cs] Its plugin exposes three skills:

- change review of a pull request, commit, branch diff or working-tree patch[^diff]
- a deep scan that repeats discovery passes over a repository or folder before validating[^deep]
- a fix workflow that takes one finding at a time, from Codex Security or an external tracker[^fix]

OpenAI reports that Codex Security cloud, launched in research preview in March 2026, had scanned over 30 million commits across more than 30,000 codebases by 2026-06-22. Human reviewers marked more than 70,000 findings as fixed, and over 500,000 findings were determined fixed automatically.[^june-cs] The solutions page shows "500K+ findings fixed", a figure that matches the automatic count, without saying which count it shows.[^solutions] Neither source gives a false-positive rate or explains the automatic determination, so the counts measure volume processed. [[codex-security|Codex Security]] carries the surfaces and the workflow detail.

## Models and access

### Two tiers

The August post introduced two tiers:[^aug]

- **Daybreak Blue** opens OpenAI's frontier general-purpose models, GPT-5.6 Sol among them, with the system-level safeguards that screen cybersecurity requests in production removed. OpenAI recommends it as the starting point for most defenders and lists vulnerability discovery, secure code review, malware analysis, incident response and patch validation among its uses.[^aug]
- **Daybreak Red** opens the purpose-trained cyber models for authorized vulnerability research, exploit validation and security testing. GPT-5.6-Cyber, built on GPT-5.6 Sol, is available through Red, and OpenAI's comparison places GPT-5.5-Cyber in Red as well.[^aug]

The solutions page maps the tiers onto six workflow cards. Four are Blue, three of them pairing Codex Security with Blue to find, validate and fix. Two are Red, and one of those puts malware, binary and firmware analysis under Red, where the August post lists malware analysis under Blue.[^solutions][^aug] The June post described the arrangement before the tiers had names: GPT-5.5 with Trusted Access for Cyber, used with Codex Security, as the starting point for most defenders, and the full GPT-5.5-Cyber for verified defenders whose authorized work needs more permissive behavior, paired with stronger verification, monitoring, scoped controls and review.[^june-model]

### Refusal gap between the tiers

With Blue access the system-level safeguards are gone, yet GPT-5.6 Sol still refuses some highly dual-use requests, and OpenAI names penetration testing of production systems as an example.[^aug] OpenAI trained GPT-5.6-Cyber to refuse fewer of them and measured the difference with an internal evaluation, the Advanced Cybersecurity Completion Rate, over requests in categories such as exploit-chain development, authentication bypass and privilege escalation. Each model ran at its highest publicly available reasoning level.[^aug]

| Model and access | Share of requests completed |
|---|---|
| GPT-5.6-Cyber (Daybreak Red) | 95.0%[^aug] |
| GPT-5.5-Cyber (Daybreak Red) | 57.3%[^aug] |
| GPT-5.6 Sol (Daybreak Blue) | 2.0%[^aug] |
| GPT-5.6 Sol (system-level safeguards on) | 1.5%[^aug] |

On these requests, removing the system-level safeguards raises the general model's completion rate by half a point, and switching to the purpose-trained model raises it by more than ninety points, so admission to Red is the access decision that changes the result.[^aug] OpenAI places Blue's gain in legitimate defensive work outside the categories the evaluation names, such as incident response, investigations and vulnerability management, which the same safeguards can block.[^aug] OpenAI notes that GPT-5.6-Cyber reasons at greater length and uses more tokens, and it presents the gain over GPT-5.5-Cyber as its answer to feedback from researchers who met persistent refusals from the earlier model.[^aug] [[sdlc-in-the-ai-attacker-era|SDLC in the AI-Attacker Era]] reads this gap as the defender-side ration on exploit generation.

### Capability claims

OpenAI reports GPT-5.6-Cyber on four further evaluations, presents the results as charts, and states no score in the text of the post:[^aug]

- **ExploitGym.** GPT-5.6-Cyber outperforms GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-5.5-Cyber on OpenAI's own implementation of the benchmark, in security-hardened, isolated environments with monitoring for misaligned behavior.[^aug]
- **Zero-day severity calibration.** On an internal dataset built from current releases of open-source repositories, scored on the severity and impact of findings and the quality of the write-up, GPT-5.6-Cyber outperforms GPT-5.6 Sol.[^aug]
- **Vulnerability discovery and report writing.** Both GPT-5.6 models improve on GPT-5.5-Cyber, and GPT-5.6-Cyber trails GPT-5.6 Sol, which OpenAI believes comes from the model sometimes writing shorter, less detailed reports.[^aug]
- **ExploitBench.** GPT-5.6 Sol performs best at the standard 300-turn limit and solves tasks more token-efficiently; at 600 turns the gap narrows. This run also used OpenAI's internal implementation.[^aug]

The results are mixed for the model OpenAI presents as its most advanced for cyber work: the specialized model leads on two evaluations and trails the general model on the other two, and every result comes from OpenAI's internal evaluations or its own implementations of public benchmarks. [[autonomous-exploit-generation|Autonomous Exploit Generation]] records the ExploitBench budget effect. OpenAI also gave GPT-5.6-Cyber before release to customer partners including SpecterOps, SentinelOne and [[palo-alto-networks|Palo Alto Networks]], and SpecterOps's CTO, Jared Atkinson, is quoted saying the model completed in under a day work that earlier models had not resolved after weeks of intermittent effort.[^aug]

### June benchmark scores

The June post reports GPT-5.5-Cyber against GPT-5.5 on three benchmarks, and states no difficulty level, time budget or harness for any of them. GPT-5.5-Cyber scores 85.6% against 81.8% on CyberGym, in single-model evaluations, 39.5% against 25.95% on ExploitGym, and 69.8% against 63.1% on SEC-bench Pro.[^june-model] OpenAI calls the CyberGym score the highest it has measured from a single model.[^june-model] Microsoft reported 88.45% at Level 1 in May 2026 for [[mdash|MDASH (Microsoft Agentic Scanning Harness)]], which orchestrates several generally available models.[^mdash] On 2026-09-28 the operator's Level-1 leaderboard listed the 85.6% as OpenAI's single-trial submission on OpenAI's agent, ranked above Anthropic's single-trial 83.1% for Claude Mythos Preview.[^cybergym-board] OpenAI's 25.95% for GPT-5.5 on ExploitGym matches none of the operator's counts for that model, so the ExploitGym figures are not directly comparable with the operator's.[^exploitgym-board] [[cybergym|CyberGym Benchmark]], [[exploit-benchmarks|ExploitBench & ExploitGym]] and [[ai-vuln-discovery-benchmark-landscape|AI Vuln-Discovery Benchmark Landscape]] carry the leaderboard figures. The June post also reports that GPT-5.5 and Codex Security helped defenders in early Daybreak work identify and validate vulnerabilities in Firefox, V8, Safari, OpenBSD, FreeBSD and HTTP/2 implementations.[^june-model]

### Disclosed findings and Preparedness assessment

OpenAI reports that GPT-5.6-Cyber found two previously unknown V8 vulnerabilities that can be chained. Its researchers validated them and reported them to Google through coordinated disclosure, and Google's fix carries CVE-2026-15903, which OpenAI describes as high severity.[^aug] OpenAI lists three further results without naming the products, and states that disclosure and remediation are under way with Daybreak partners and the open-source community:[^aug]

- at least five vulnerabilities in a popular mobile operating system
- three critical vulnerabilities in a popular database
- over 400 vulnerabilities that can lead to privilege escalation in a popular operating-system kernel

Under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-5.6-Cyber are both assessed High for cybersecurity capability and below the Critical threshold; the cyber model improved on GPT-5.6 Sol on some tasks it was trained for, short of Critical. A system card for GPT-5.6-Cyber is to follow.[^aug] The same post states, citing OpenAI's updates on the [[openai-hugging-face-agent-incident|OpenAI–Hugging Face Agent Incident]], that GPT-5.6-Cyber was not involved in exploiting Hugging Face and that no other model planned for an upcoming release was either.[^aug]

### Vetting and safeguards

Blue and Red are open to approved individuals and organizations doing authorized work. OpenAI controls access through identity verification, account security, monitoring, approved-use restrictions and legal attestations, and it points organizations to an application form and individuals to `chatgpt.com/cyber`.[^aug] The August post adds five steps:

- Daybreak customers using Codex are steered, through app defaults and interface features, from full-access mode to auto-review mode, which evaluates actions needing elevated permissions before they run and can block requests with a significant risk of destructive behavior.[^aug]
- Individual Daybreak accounts must use hardware security keys from 2026-09-01.[^aug]
- Improved monitoring, among further security measures in development, is to roll out in the weeks after the post.[^aug]
- Alignment training and testing take priority for upcoming Daybreak releases.[^aug]
- OpenAI's Codex documentation on safety best practices is updated to help teams keep cyber-capable agents within their intended security boundaries.[^aug]

OpenAI's best practices for the Daybreak models are the controls [[agent-sandboxing|Agent Sandboxing]] describes. It recommends running security workflows in environments cut off from sensitive production systems and the open internet, reviewing tool calls outside the Codex sandbox through auto-review mode, and enforcing the authorized scope with scoped permission profiles.[^aug] OpenAI names the residual risk itself: models running with reduced safeguards carry risk beyond standard use, from misuse or from misalignment. It holds that democratizing defenders' access is crucial to accelerating cyber defense despite that risk.[^aug]

## Daybreak Defense Network

The partner programme puts OpenAI's cyber models inside partner products and services; the programme page invites vendors to "build GPT Cyber models into your security offerings".[^hub] The June post launched it as the Daybreak Cyber Partner Program: partners use GPT-5.5 with Trusted Access for Cyber in the security products and services they provide to customers, direct model access stays with the partners, and OpenAI works with them on safeguards, monitoring and abuse-prevention standards.[^june-partners] The partner page, undated and fetched on 2026-09-28, calls it the Daybreak Defense Network and takes applications at a `daybreak-cyber-partner-program` address.[^network]

A partner joins through product integration, embedding approved capabilities in bounded workflows inside tools customers already use, or through a managed service, with trained partner teams hands-on-keyboard and the customer receiving the outcome.[^network] Partners work in application security, security operations, and AI and agent security. An applicant submits the partner interest form, OpenAI reaches out when the workflows fit, and the partner builds and validates with OpenAI's cyber deployment engineering, safeguards and controls included, before it launches. Registering interest does not guarantee inclusion, model access or a production timeline.[^network]

The partner page lists twenty partners:[^network]

- **With a wiki page.** [[cisco|Cisco]], [[cloudflare|Cloudflare]], [[crowdstrike|CrowdStrike]], [[elastic|Elastic]], [[palo-alto-networks|Palo Alto Networks]] and TrendMicro/TrendAI ([[trend-micro|Trend Micro]]).
- **Without one.** Akamai, Cato Networks, Check Point, Darktrace, Fortinet, IBM, Okta, Proofpoint, Red Hat, SentinelOne, SpecterOps, Sophos, Tenable and Zscaler.

Seven of the twenty, Akamai, Cisco, Cloudflare, CrowdStrike, Fortinet, Palo Alto Networks and Zscaler, also appear in the solutions page's "trusted by" strip, which adds Salesforce and Oracle and does not say what "trusted by" denotes. The same page quotes Cloudflare's CTO, Dane Knecht, on the potential of OpenAI's cyber capabilities to bring stronger reasoning and more agentic execution into security workflows.[^solutions]

The partner route changes who receives the capability. A security vendor carries OpenAI's model into its product, so an organization that never applies for access receives the output of a model it does not query itself. Under the June terms that model was GPT-5.5 with Trusted Access for Cyber.[^june-partners]

## Patch the Planet

Through Patch the Planet, OpenAI funds expert security researchers, equipped with Codex Security and its advanced models, to carry vulnerability work through to landed fixes in widely used open-source projects. OpenAI founded it with Trail of Bits, in collaboration with HackerOne and Calif.[^june-ptp] The design answers maintainer capacity. OpenAI cites Linux Foundation and Harvard research finding that 94% of the widely used projects studied had fewer than ten developers responsible for more than 90% of the code added in a year ([Census II report](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/download.aspx?name=Harvard+Census+II+of+Free+and+Open+Source+Software+-+Report.pdf)), and it states that faster AI-driven discovery leaves maintainers sifting thousands of reports, many of them low-quality false positives.[^june-ptp]

The engagement places the human work before the maintainer sees anything. Researchers consult the maintainers, who set their priorities, preferences and disclosure process, then validate and deduplicate both vulnerabilities and patches before either reaches a maintainer,[^june-ptp] and maintainers decide what lands.[^hub] Deduplicating patches as well as findings extends triage past the finding into the fix, which bears on the false-positive flood that [[agentic-soc-ra-exposure-vulnops|Agentic SOC Exposure and VulnOps Surface]] treats as the first bound on the function. Participating projects receive ChatGPT Pro, conditional access to Codex Security, and API credits.[^june-ptp] The June post counted more than 30 committed projects, among them cURL, Go, Python, Sigstore and pyca/cryptography; a five-day opening sprint surfaced hundreds of issues, merged dozens of patches and built reusable fuzzing, variant-analysis, differential-testing and specification-based testing workflows.[^june-ptp] The hub gives later counts, undated and fetched on 2026-09-28:

| Stage | Count |
|---|---|
| Open-source projects under review | 41[^hub-ptp] |
| Issues identified for validation and disclosure | 858[^hub-ptp] |
| Patches developed and tested before maintainer review | 263[^hub-ptp] |
| Patches accepted upstream | 143[^hub-ptp] |

Of the 263 patches, 143 had been accepted upstream when the hub was fetched, 54%; the hub does not say how many of the remaining 120 are still under review.[^hub-ptp] The hub puts OpenAI's commitment to open-source security at \$17M in API credits and direct support, including work with Trail of Bits and Calif.[^hub-ptp]

## Subsidized access and government

OpenAI commits \$1 billion in subsidized Daybreak access over six months, and five groups can register interest: state and local governments, critical-infrastructure operators, community banks, nonprofits and open-source maintainers.[^hub-billion] The offer aims at the population the [[cyber-poverty-line|Cyber Poverty Line]] concept describes, organizations short of the people, tooling and time to defend themselves.

The June post records four strands of government work:

- Trusted Access for Cyber partnerships established in the month before the post with Australia, Canada, France, Germany, Japan, the Republic of Korea and EU institutions including [[enisa|ENISA (European Union Agency for Cybersecurity)]].[^june-gov]
- A partnership with the UK government on cyber, testing and evaluation.[^june-gov]
- Pre-deployment testing of GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.5-Cyber with the Center for AI Standards and Innovation, and work with the Office of the National Cyber Director and the Office of Science and Technology Policy on a recent Executive Order.[^june-model]
- Planned direct work with eligible critical-infrastructure operators, government networks among them, on safeguards tailored to their systems.[^june-gov]

## Relation to Project Glasswing

Daybreak is OpenAI's counterpart to Anthropic's [[glasswing|Project Glasswing]]. Both give vetted defenders a frontier lab's most cyber-capable models, and both labs say the constraint has moved past finding vulnerabilities. They differ in who is admitted and to what:

| Dimension | Project Glasswing | OpenAI Daybreak |
|---|---|---|
| Unit of access | 12 named launch partners and over 40 additional organizations[^glasswing] | Approved individuals and organizations, by tier[^aug] |
| Models | [[mythos\|Claude Mythos Preview (Anthropic)]], with no general availability planned[^glasswing] | General-purpose models in Blue; purpose-trained cyber models in Red[^aug] |
| Partner role | Use the model in their own defensive work[^glasswing] | Embed the models in products and managed services[^network] |
| Stated funding | Up to \$100M in usage credits and \$4M in donations[^glasswing] | \$1 billion in subsidized access[^hub-billion]; \$17M for open source[^hub-ptp] |
| Open-source channel | Claude for Open Source and donations[^glasswing] | Patch the Planet's funded researchers[^june-ptp] |

- **The partner programme.** "Daybreak Cyber Partner Program" in the June post, and "Daybreak Defense Network" on the partner page and the hub.[^june-partners][^network][^hub]
